Exam Golden Rules

1. LTCL can ONLY be set off against LTCG (never STCG).
2. Capital loss cannot be set off against ANY other head.
3. House property loss cap is ₹2 Lakhs per year against other heads.
4. Unabsorbed depreciation can be carried forward indefinitely!

Section 13.1 • Core RIA Rule
Intra-Head (Inter-Source)

Intra-Head (Inter-Source) Set-Off of Losses (Section 70)

The general rule of Section 70 permits an assessee to set off a loss from one source against income from any other source within the same head of income for the same assessment year. However, the Act imposes 5 absolute exceptions:

The Five Inflexible Exceptions to Section 70:

1. Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL) ⭐ LTCL can ONLY be set off against Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG). It CANNOT be set off against Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG)! (However, Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL) can be set off against both STCG and LTCG).
2. Speculation Business Loss Speculation business losses (intra-day equity trades) can ONLY be set off against speculation business profits. They cannot be set off against non-speculation business income.
3. Specified Business Loss (Sec 35AD) Loss from any specified business eligible for deduction under Section 35AD (cold chain, warehousing, hospitals) can only be set off against profits of another specified business.
4. Owning & Maintaining Race Horses Losses incurred in the activity of owning and maintaining race horses can only be set off against income from the same activity.
5. Casual Winnings (Lotteries / Gambling / Online Gaming) No loss can be set off against winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, betting, gambling, or online money games (Sec 115BB / 115BBJ). Furthermore, losses from such activities cannot be set off against any income!
Section 13.2 • High Yield Focus
Inter-Head Restrictions

Inter-Head Set-Off of Losses (Section 71)

Where the net result in respect of any head of income is a loss after applying Section 70, the assessee is entitled under Section 71 to set off that loss against income from any other head in the same previous year. Four critical statutory restrictions govern this rule:

1. Capital Losses Cannot Cross Heads:

Capital Losses (whether Short-Term or Long-Term) CANNOT be set off against income under ANY other head (cannot touch Salary, House Property, PGBP, or IFOS). Capital losses are strictly quarantined within the Capital Gains head.

2. Business Loss Cannot Touch Salary:

Loss under the head "Profits and gains of business or profession" (PGBP) cannot be set off against income under the head "Salaries". (It can, however, be set off against House Property, Capital Gains, or IFOS in the same year).

3. House Property Loss Capped at ₹2,00,000 ⭐:

Loss from House Property can be set off against any other head of income in the same year, but subject to a strict ceiling of ₹2,00,000. Any unabsorbed house property loss beyond ₹2 Lakhs must be carried forward to subsequent years.

Carry Forward and Set-Off of Losses (Master Reference Table)

If a loss cannot be fully set off in the same assessment year, it may be carried forward to subsequent years subject to statutory duration and head restrictions:

Nature of Loss Max Years to Carry Forward Permitted Subsequent Set-off Mandatory Timely ITR (Sec 139(3))
House Property Loss 8 Assessment Years ONLY against Income from House Property NO (Can carry forward even if late)
Business Loss (Non-Speculation) 8 Assessment Years ONLY against Business Profits (PGBP) YES (Mandatory by due date)
Speculation Business Loss 4 Assessment Years Only ONLY against Speculation Profits YES (Mandatory by due date)
Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL) 8 Assessment Years Against STCG or LTCG YES (Mandatory by due date)
Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL) ⭐ 8 Assessment Years ONLY against Long-Term Capital Gains! YES (Mandatory by due date)
Unabsorbed Depreciation (Sec 32(2)) ⭐ INDEFINITE (No Time Limit!) Against ANY head of income (except Salaries) NO (Can carry forward even if late)
Section 139(3) Mandate: To carry forward business losses or capital losses, the assessee MUST file their return of income on or before the due date specified under Section 139(1). If filed belatedly under Section 139(4), the right to carry forward capital and business losses is permanently lost!
Section 13.4 & 13.5

Corporate Restructuring (Sec 47) & Securities Lending (SLB)

Amalgamation & Demerger (Section 47)

Amalgamation (Sec 47(vii)): Exchange of shares by a shareholder in an amalgamating company for shares in the amalgamated company is NOT regarded as a transfer (no capital gains tax triggered), provided the amalgamated company is an Indian company.
Cost & Holding Period: Cost of old shares is transferred to new shares, and the period of holding of old shares is tacked (included) in computing future capital gains.

Securities Lending & Borrowing (SLB)

Under Section 47(xv), any transfer of a capital asset in a transaction of lending of securities under the SEBI Securities Lending Scheme is NOT regarded as a transfer.
• Neither lending nor return of securities attracts capital gains tax.
• The lending fee received by the lender is taxable as business or other income.

Section 13.6 • High Yield Focus
Side-Pocketing Math

Segregated Portfolios in Mutual Funds & Scheme Consolidation

When a credit event occurs in a debt mutual fund, SEBI permits the AMC to separate distressed debt assets into a Segregated Portfolio (side-pocket). The Income Tax Act lays down exact cost and holding rules under Section 49(2AG) & Section 2(42A):

Cost Allocation Formula for Segregated Portfolio (Sec 49(2AG)):

Cost of Segregated Units = Cost of Original Units × [Net Assets of Segregated Portfolio ÷ Total Net Assets of Scheme Immediately Prior to Segregation]

The cost of the remaining units in the Main Portfolio is reduced by the cost allocated to the segregated portfolio.
Holding Period: The holding period of the segregated units includes the period for which the original units were held by the assessee before segregation!

Mutual Fund Scheme Consolidation & Plan Mergers (Sec 47(xviii) & 47(xix)):

• Consolidation of Schemes (Sec 47(xviii)): When two or more mutual fund schemes are merged (e.g. Large Cap Scheme A merges into Scheme B), the transfer of units by unitholders is NOT regarded as a transfer. No capital gains tax is triggered.
• Consolidation of Plans (Sec 47(xix)): When plans within a scheme are consolidated (e.g. Dividend/IDCW plan merged into Growth plan, or Regular plan into Direct plan), it is NOT a taxable transfer.
• In both cases, the cost and holding period of the original units are carried over to the new units.
