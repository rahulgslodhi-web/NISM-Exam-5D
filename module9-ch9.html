Section 9.1

Scope of Section 56: The Residuary Head

Section 56 of the Income Tax Act serves as the residuary head of income. If an item of income does not legally qualify to be assessed under Salaries, House Property, PGBP, or Capital Gains, it is automatically charged to tax under Income from Other Sources (IFOS).

Key Inclusions under Section 56: Dividends from domestic/foreign companies; Income from mutual fund units (IDCW); Interest on bank deposits, corporate deposits, and government securities; Taxable deemed gifts under Section 56(2)(x); Family pension received by legal heirs; Remuneration received by MPs/MLAs; Director sitting fees; and Winnings from lotteries, horse races, and gaming.
Section 9.2 • High Yield Focus
Dividend Regime

Dividend Taxation & Section 57 Expense Cap (20% Rule)

Following the abolition of the Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) by the Finance Act 2020, dividend income distributed by domestic companies and mutual fund IDCW plans is taxable directly in the hands of the investor at their marginal income tax slab rates.

Deduction of Expenses (Section 57)

ONLY Interest Expense incurred on capital borrowed for investing in shares or mutual fund units is deductible.
20% Cap: The deduction cannot exceed 20% of the gross dividend income included in total income for that year.
No Other Expenses: Brokerage, management fees, depository charges, or collection charges are strictly NOT deductible.

TDS Mandates (Sec 194 & 194K)

Corporate Dividends (Sec 194): TDS @ 10% if dividend paid to a resident individual shareholder exceeds ₹5,000 in a financial year.
Mutual Fund IDCW (Sec 194K): TDS @ 10% if total dividend paid to a resident unitholder exceeds ₹5,000.
Non-residents: TDS @ 20% under Section 195 (subject to DTAA rates).

Worked Numerical: Section 57 Cap Application

An investor receives ₹1,00,000 as dividend from Indian companies. To purchase these shares, he borrowed funds and paid an interest of ₹28,000 during the year.

• Maximum allowable interest deduction = 20% of ₹1,00,000 = ₹20,000.
• Disallowed excess interest = ₹28,000 − ₹20,000 = ₹8,000.
• Net Taxable Dividend Income under IFOS = ₹1,00,000 − ₹20,000 = ₹80,000.

Section 9.4 • High Yield Focus
Heavily Tested

Deemed Gifts under Section 56(2)(x)

Section 56(2)(x) is an anti-abuse provision designed to tax receipts of money or property without consideration or for inadequate consideration in the hands of the recipient.

1. Sum of Money (Cash/Bank)

If aggregate money received without consideration from non-relatives during the financial year exceeds ₹50,000, the ENTIRE amount is taxable (not just the excess above ₹50,000).

2. Immovable Property

Without Consideration: Taxable if Stamp Duty Value (SDV) exceeds ₹50,000.
Inadequate Consideration: Difference (SDV − Consideration) is taxable IF difference exceeds ₹50,000 AND exceeds 10% of consideration (safe harbour tolerance).

3. Movable Property

Applies only to specified movable assets (shares, securities, jewellery, paintings, sculptures, bullion). Taxable if aggregate Fair Market Value (FMV) exceeds ₹50,000 (without consideration) or if FMV exceeds consideration by > ₹50,000.

Exemptions: Gifts NOT Subject to Tax under Section 56(2)(x)

Any sum of money or property received in the following circumstances is 100% EXEMPT from tax, regardless of value:

  • From any Relative (defined in statute)
  • On the occasion of the marriage of the individual
  • Under a Will or by way of inheritance
  • In contemplation of death of the donor
  • From any local authority or registered charitable/educational trust
Definition of "Relative" for Individuals: (i) Spouse, (ii) Brother or sister, (iii) Brother or sister of the spouse, (iv) Brother or sister of either of the parents, (v) Any lineal ascendant or descendant, (vi) Any lineal ascendant or descendant of the spouse, and (vii) Spouses of the persons referred to in (ii) to (vi).
Crucial Exam Note: Cousins and friends are NOT "relatives"! Gifts from friends or cousins exceeding ₹50,000 are fully taxable.
Section 9.5

Family Pension & Casual Winnings (Lotteries/Betting)

Family Pension (Section 57(iia))

Family pension received by legal heirs of a deceased employee is taxed under IFOS.
Standard Deduction: Lower of 33.33% (1/3rd) of pension or ₹15,000 under Old Regime.
New Tax Regime (Sec 115BAC): Finance Act 2024 enhanced the standard deduction on family pension to ₹25,000.

Casual Winnings (Sec 115BB / 115BBJ)

Winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, horse races, card games, and online money games.
• Taxed at a flat rate of 30% (plus cess).
• No basic exemption limit or lower tax slab benefits.
• No deductions under Chapter VI-A or expenses allowed.
• No set-off of any losses allowed against such winnings.
