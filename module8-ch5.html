Section 5.1 • Institutional Framework
PFRDA Architecture

National Pension System (NPS) Architecture

The National Pension System (NPS) was instituted by the Government of India on January 1, 2004 for new civil service recruits (except armed forces), and subsequently opened to All Citizens of India (including NRIs and OCIs) aged 18 to 70 years from May 1, 2009.

1. PFRDA

Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority; statutory apex regulator established under the PFRDA Act, 2013.

2. NPS Trust

Holds legal ownership of all assets and funds of the NPS for the collective benefit of subscribers, executing fiduciary oversight.

3. CRAs (Recordkeeping)

Central Recordkeeping Agencies (Protean e-Gov, KFintech, CAMS). Maintain subscriber PRAN (Permanent Retirement Account Number) and unit ledgers.

4. Pension Fund Managers

Regulated asset managers (SBI, LIC, UTI, HDFC, ICICI, Kotak, Axis, Tata, etc.) that manage the investment pools under strict asset allocation caps.

5. Trustee Bank & Custodian

Axis Bank serves as the Trustee Bank managing cash clearing. Stock Holding Corporation of India Ltd (SHCIL) serves as Custodian of securities.

6. POPs (Points of Presence)

Commercial banks, post offices, and financial distributors acting as customer-facing registration and contribution collection gateways.

NPS Tier I vs Tier II

NPS Tier I vs Tier II Accounts: Structural Comparison

Every NPS subscriber opens a primary pension account (Tier I). Subscribers can optionally activate a secondary, liquid investment facility (Tier II).

Feature NPS Tier I (Primary Pension Account) ⭐ NPS Tier II (Voluntary Investment Account)
Account Character Non-withdrawable retirement pension account Open-ended voluntary savings/investment facility
Pre-condition Independent; can be opened directly by any citizen aged 18-70 Must hold an active, valid Tier I PRAN account
Min Opening Deposit ₹500 ₹1,000
Min Contribution / Year ₹500 per contrib; ₹1,000 per financial year ₹250 per contrib; no annual minimum requirement
Liquidity / Lock-in Locked until age 60 (strict partial withdrawal exceptions) 100% Liquid anytime; no lock-in or exit load
Tax Deductions Sec 80CCD(1), 80CCD(1B) [₹50k], & 80CCD(2) No tax deductions for private citizens (Sec 80C only for Central Govt with 3-yr lock-in)

Section 5.3 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
NPS Tax & Exits

NPS Tax Deductions & Exit Regulations

NPS Tier I provides a powerful three-tier tax deduction under the Income Tax Act, 1961, making it one of the most tax-efficient accumulation vehicles in India.

Tier 1 Tax Deduction Section 80CCD(1)

Employee or individual contribution up to 10% of Salary (Basic + DA) for salaried, or up to 20% of Gross Total Income (GTI) for self-employed individuals. Subject to the overall ceiling of ₹1.5 Lakhs under Section 80CCE (clubbed with 80C and 80CCC).

Exclusive Additional Deduction Section 80CCD(1B) ⭐

An exclusive deduction of up to ₹50,000 for contributions to NPS Tier I. This is over and above the ₹1.5 Lakh limit under Section 80CCE, creating a combined total personal deduction of ₹2,00,000!

Employer Contribution Section 80CCD(2) ⭐

Employer contribution up to 10% of Salary (Basic + DA) for private corporate employees, or up to 14% for Central and State Government employees. Has no upper rupee limit under 80CCD, but is subject to the aggregate ₹7.5 Lakh cap across PF + NPS + Superannuation.

NPS Exit Rules (Normal vs Premature vs Partial)

1. Normal Superannuation (Age 60) ⭐

• Lump Sum: Up to 60% of total corpus can be withdrawn as lump sum. This 60% is 100% Tax-Free!

• Mandatory Annuity: Minimum 40% of corpus must be used to purchase an immediate annuity from an approved life insurer.

• Small Corpus Rule: If total corpus is $\le$ ₹5 Lakhs, the subscriber can withdraw 100% as lump sum tax-free without buying any annuity!

2. Premature Exit (Before Age 60) ⭐

• Permitted only after completing at least 5 years of subscription.

• Mandatory Annuity: Minimum 80% of corpus must be annuitized.

• Lump Sum: Maximum 20% of corpus can be withdrawn (tax-free).

• Small Corpus Rule: If total corpus is $\le$ ₹2.5 Lakhs, 100% lump sum withdrawal is permitted.

3. Partial Withdrawal (During Tenure)

• Allowed after 3 years of subscription.

• Maximum 25% of own contributions (excluding employer contribution and returns).

• Permitted maximum 3 times during entire tenure for specified purposes (higher education/marriage of children, buying first residential house, critical illness).

• 100% Tax-Exempt under Section 10(12B).

Section 5.4 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
NPS Investment Choices

NPS Asset Classes & Choices: Active vs Auto (Life Cycle)

NPS offers four underlying asset classes managed by authorized Pension Fund Managers (PFMs):

Class E Equities (High Risk)
Class C Corporate Debt (Medium)
Class G Govt Securities (Low Risk)
Class A Alternative Assets (Max 5%)
Option A: Active Choice Self-Directed

Subscriber actively decides the asset allocation across Classes E, C, G, and A according to risk appetite:

  • • Equity (E) Cap: Maximum allocation to Equity is 75% up to age 50.
  • • Tapering Rule: From age 51 to 60, the maximum equity cap reduces by 2.5% every year, tapering to 50% at age 60.
  • • Alternative Assets (A) Cap: Strictly capped at a maximum of 5% at all times.
  • • Govt Securities (G): Can be up to 100%.
Option B: Auto Choice (Life Cycle) ⭐ Age-Based Rebalancing

Asset allocation automatically adjusts based on subscriber age according to a pre-defined life-cycle matrix:

• Aggressive (LC75): Starts with 75% in E up to age 35, tapering annually to 15% at age 55.

• Moderate (LC50) - DEFAULT: Starts with 50% in E up to age 35, tapering annually to 10% at age 55. If subscriber does not select an option, LC50 is applied automatically!

• Conservative (LC25): Starts with 25% in E up to age 35, tapering annually to 5% at age 55.

Section 5.5 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
APY Scheme

Atal Pension Yojana (APY)

Administered by PFRDA under the National Pension System architecture, APY is primarily targeted at unorganized sector workers to provide a government-guaranteed minimum monthly pension.

Entry Age Horizon 18 to 40 Years

Contributions must continue for a minimum of 20 years until age 60.

Pension Slabs (Age 60) ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 / mo

Guaranteed fixed options: ₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000 monthly.

Triple Benefit Waterfall Subscriber → Spouse → Nominee

On subscriber death, spouse receives same pension; on spouse death, corpus is returned to nominee.

Taxpayer Bar (Oct 2022) ⭐ Income Taxpayers Ineligible

Anyone who is or has been an income-tax payer is barred from joining APY since Oct 1, 2022.

Section 5.6 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
EPF, EPS & EDLI

Statutory Social Security: EPF, EPS (1995) & EDLI

Governed by the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, applicable to establishments employing 20 or more persons.

The 12% + 12% Contribution Breakdown

Employee Contribution 12.0% of Basic + DA

100% of the employee's 12% is credited directly to the EPF Account. Eligible for Section 80C deduction.

Tax Alert: Interest earned on employee contribution exceeding ₹2.5 Lakhs per year is taxable as income!
Employer Contribution 12.0% of Basic + DA

The employer's 12% is split into two mandatory statutory buckets:

• 8.33% to EPS: Capped at ₹15,000 wage ceiling = ₹1,250/month maximum!
• 3.67% to EPF: Balance credited to the employee's EPF account (plus any surplus above ₹15k wage cap).
Exam Formula Card EPS 1995 Pension
$$\text{Monthly Pension} = \frac{\text{Pensionable Salary} \times \text{Pensionable Service}}{70}$$

• Eligibility: Minimum 10 years of contributory service required to qualify for superannuation pension at age 58.

• Pensionable Salary: Average monthly pay drawn in the last 60 months of contributory service, capped at ₹15,000/month (unless higher pension opted under SC judgment).

• Maximum Regular Pension: With 35 years maximum recognized service: $\frac{15000 \times 35}{70} = \mathbf{₹7,500\text{ per month}}$.

Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance (EDLI): Provides life insurance coverage up to ₹7,00,000 to the nominee upon death of an active EPF member. Funded 100% by employer (0.5% of wage).
Section 5.7 • Small Savings Instruments
Govt Guaranteed

Small Savings: PPF, SCSS & PMVVY

Accumulation Public Provident Fund (PPF)

• Tenure: 15 years, extendable in blocks of 5 years (with or without contributions).

• Limits: Min ₹500, Max ₹1,50,000 per financial year.

• Tax: Pure EEE status (Exempt on contribution under 80C, Exempt on interest, Exempt on maturity).

• Attachment Protection: Cannot be attached by any court decree for debts.

Decumulation ⭐ Senior Citizens Savings Scheme

• Eligibility: Age 60+ (55 for VRS retirees, 50 for defense retirees).

• Enhanced Cap: Max ₹30,00,000 deposit per individual (doubled from ₹15L in Budget 2023).

• Payout: Quarterly interest credited directly to bank account.

• Tax: Sec 80C on deposit. Quarterly interest is fully taxable (TDS applies if interest > ₹50,000/yr).

Govt Subsidized PMVVY (Pradhan Mantri Vaya Vandana)

• Operated Exclusively: Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC).

• Status: Closed to new enrollments on March 31, 2023 (existing policies run full 10-year term).

• Max Investment: ₹15 Lakhs per senior citizen (age 60+).

• Ceiling: Monthly pension ceiling of ₹9,250 to ₹10,000.

Section 5.8 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
Annuity Contracts

Annuity Contracts & Payout Classification

An annuity is a financial contract issued by a life insurance company that converts a lump sum corpus into a guaranteed series of periodic cash payouts for life.

Immediate Annuity

Purchased with a single lump sum premium; income payouts begin immediately at the end of the first chosen interval (1 month, 3 months, or 1 year). Mandatory upon NPS exit at age 60.

Deferred Annuity

Features a deferment (accumulation) period. Premium accumulates interest or market-linked NAV before the annuitization phase begins years later.

The 5 Core Life Annuity Options Tested in NISM Series X-B

Option How Payout Works On Annuitant's Death Relative Payout Rate
Life Annuity without Return of Purchase Price (No ROP) Guaranteed fixed cash payout for life of annuitant Annuity terminates immediately; purchase price retained by insurer! Highest Initial Payout (e.g. 8.5% - 9%)
Life Annuity with Return of Purchase Price (ROP) ⭐ Guaranteed fixed cash payout for life of annuitant 100% of initial purchase price refunded to nominee Lower Payout Rate (e.g. 6.2% - 6.8%)
Joint Life Last Survivor (with ROP) Paid to primary annuitant for life, then 100% to spouse for life On death of both spouses, purchase price refunded to nominee Lowest Payout Rate (insures two distinct lives)
Increasing Annuity (Inflation-Adjusted) Annuity payout increases by 3% to 5% every year Depends on whether ROP option was selected Starts very low, but escalates over time
Guaranteed Period Annuity (5 / 10 / 15 / 20 Years) Guaranteed for fixed term (e.g. 15 yrs) even if annuitant dies, and for life thereafter If death occurs in yr 4, nominee receives payout for remaining 11 yrs Moderate Payout Rate
Taxation Alert: Regardless of annuity type, all annuity payouts are treated as Income from Other Sources and taxed at the retiree's normal income tax slab rate!
Section 5.9 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
Reverse Mortgage

Reverse Mortgage Scheme (RMS) for Senior Citizens

Introduced by the National Housing Bank (NHB) and commercial lenders, a Reverse Mortgage enables "house-rich, cash-poor" senior citizens to convert the equity in their self-occupied residential property into tax-free periodic income streams without losing possession.

Eligibility & Property Norms

• Age: Primary borrower must be at least 60 years old. In joint applications, spouse must be at least 55 years old.

• Property: Self-acquired, clear-titled, self-occupied residential property with a residual life of at least 20 years. Commercial properties are ineligible.

• Tenure: Typically 10 to 20 years, or until the lifetime of the last surviving spouse.

• LTV: Typically 60% to 80% of assessed market value (revalued every 3-5 years).

Tax Exemption: Section 10(43) ⭐

Under Section 10(43) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, any loan installments received by the senior citizen (monthly, quarterly, or lump sum) are 100% Tax-Exempt because they represent loan disbursements, NOT income!

No repayment of principal or interest is ever required during the lifetime of the borrower or surviving spouse while they reside in the house!
Settlement upon Demise of Both Borrowers:
Option A: Heirs Redeem Property Legal heirs have the first right to settle the accumulated loan balance (principal + interest) from their own funds and reclaim clear ownership of the home.
Option B: Bank Sells Property The bank sells the property. If sale proceeds exceed the loan balance, the surplus is paid to legal heirs. If sale proceeds fall short (shortfall), the bank cannot seek recovery from the heirs (non-recourse to heirs).
