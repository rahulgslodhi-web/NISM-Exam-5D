CASE STUDY 1 OF 4 • 10 MARKS Core Theme: Human Life Value, Need-Based Insurance & Education Goals

Client Dossier: Vikram & Ananya Sharma (Mid-Career IT Executive & Architect)

Family Profile

• Vikram (Client): Age 42, VP Software Engineering at a US MNC. Retirement age planned at 60 (18 working years remaining).
• Ananya (Spouse): Age 39, Independent Architect & Partner in a design studio.
• Child 1 (Aarav): Age 12, higher education planned at age 18 (6 years away).
• Child 2 (Diya): Age 8, higher education planned at age 18 (10 years away).

Monthly Cash Flows

• Vikram Take-Home: ₹2,50,000 / month (Annual: ₹30,00,000 net).
• Ananya Net Income: ₹1,20,000 / month (Annual: ₹14,40,000).
• Household Living Expenses: ₹1,60,000 / month (excludes EMIs). Vikram's personal consumption is 25% of his salary (₹62,500/mo).
• Home Loan EMI: ₹68,000 / month (Outstanding Principal: ₹60 Lakhs; 12 years remaining).

Balance Sheet & Protection

• Liquid Equity Mutual Funds: ₹45,00,000
• PPF & EPF (Vikram): ₹35,00,000
• Bank Savings & FDs: ₹15,00,000
• Self-Occupied House: Market Value ₹1.80 Crores (Mortgaged).
• Existing Life Insurance: Vikram has ₹50 Lakhs employer corporate cover + ₹10 Lakhs personal endowment. Ananya has Nil.

Case 1 Questions (5 Multi-Part Problems)

Question 1.1 • Human Life Value (Income Replacement) 2 Marks

Calculate Vikram's Human Life Value (HLV) using the Income Replacement Method. Assume his annual take-home is ₹30 Lakhs, personal consumption is 25%, remaining working career to age 60 is 18 years, and the net real discount rate (investment return minus inflation) is 3.0% per annum.

Question 1.2 • Need-Based Insurance Adequacy & Shortfall 2 Marks

The adviser conducts a Need-Based Life Insurance Analysis. Family ongoing living expenses need a capital pool of ₹2.10 Crores (PV of family support), outstanding home loan is ₹60 Lakhs, and children's higher education requires a provision of ₹50 Lakhs. Vikram has ₹95 Lakhs in liquid financial assets (MFs, PPF/EPF, FDs) and ₹10 Lakhs in personal life insurance. His employer's ₹50 Lakh cover cannot be counted as it is contingent on employment. What is Vikram's net life insurance shortfall?

Question 1.3 • Higher Education Goal Future Value 2 Marks

Son Aarav (Age 12) will enter engineering college at age 18 (in exactly 6 years). The 4-year degree currently costs ₹25,000,00 (₹25 Lakhs) in today's terms. Higher education inflation is estimated at 8.0% per annum. What will be the estimated future cost of Aarav's education at age 18?

Question 1.4 • Emergency Liquidity Buffer Adequacy 2 Marks

The family has monthly household living expenses of ₹1,60,000, school fees of ₹30,000, and a mandatory home loan EMI of ₹68,000. Under prudent advisory standards, how much should be maintained in the family's emergency reserve (6 months of mandatory outflows), and is their current ₹15 Lakh bank savings/FD reserve adequate?

Question 1.5 • Insurance Product Restructuring (BTID Strategy) 2 Marks

Vikram is paying ₹95,000 per year towards an old traditional endowment policy offering a sum assured of only ₹10 Lakhs. A pure term insurance policy offering ₹2.50 Crores cover for Vikram up to age 60 costs ₹32,000 per year. What should the adviser recommend under the "Buy Term and Invest the Difference (BTID)" principle?

CASE STUDY 2 OF 4 • 10 MARKS Core Theme: Pre-Retirement Step-Up Corpus, Real Rate of Return & SWP Decumulation

Client Dossier: Rajesh & Meena Kulkarni (Pre-Retirement & Decumulation)

Demographics & Horizon

• Rajesh: Age 52, Senior Operations Director in Auto Manufacturing. Retiring at age 60 (8 years accumulation phase).
• Meena: Age 50, Homemaker. Life expectancy: Rajesh 85, Meena 88.
• Planning Horizon: 28 years post-retirement (from Rajesh age 60 to Meena age 88).
• Debt Status: Completely debt-free; primary residence fully owned.

Cash Flows & Assumptions

• Current Monthly Living Expenses: ₹1,10,000 / month (Annual: ₹13,20,000).
• Pre-Retirement Inflation: 6.0% per annum.
• Post-Retirement Inflation: 5.5% per annum.
• Pre-Retirement Return: 11.0% p.a. (SAA 65% Equity : 35% Debt).
• Post-Retirement Return: 8.5% p.a. (SAA 30% Equity : 70% Debt).

Existing Retirement Pool

• EPF + PPF Balance: ₹70,00,000 (Expected growth: 7.5% p.a. including employee/employer contributions).
• Equity Mutual Funds: ₹65,00,000 (Expected growth: 11.0% p.a.).
• Non-Agricultural Plot: Market Value ₹50,00,000 (Not earmarked for retirement).
• Monthly Investable Surplus: ₹95,000 / month available for fresh SIPs.

Case 2 Questions (5 Multi-Part Problems)

Question 2.1 • First-Year Post-Retirement Living Expense 2 Marks

Current annual household living expenses are ₹13,20,000 (₹1,10,000/month). Pre-retirement inflation is 6.0% per annum. What will be the annual household living expense required during the first year of retirement when Rajesh reaches age 60 (in 8 years)?

Question 2.2 • Total Retirement Corpus Requirement (Real Rate of Return) 2 Marks

The first year's living expense at age 60 is ₹21,04,000 (payable at the beginning of each year - Annuity Due). Post-retirement return is 8.5% p.a., post-retirement inflation is 5.5% p.a., and the retirement phase is 28 years. Using the exact Fisher real rate formula r = [(1 + 0.085) / (1 + 0.055)] - 1 = 2.8436%, what is the total retirement corpus required at age 60?

Question 2.3 • Future Value of Existing Assets & Deficit 2 Marks

Rajesh's EPF/PPF corpus of ₹70 Lakhs will grow at 7.5% p.a. over 8 years to reach ₹1.25 Crores. His Equity MFs of ₹65 Lakhs will compound at 11% p.a. over 8 years to reach ₹1.50 Crores. Total accumulated wealth from existing assets at age 60 will be ₹2.75 Crores. With the required corpus being ₹4.07 Crores, what is the net retirement corpus deficit at age 60?

Question 2.4 • Monthly Step-Up SIP Requirement 2 Marks

To accumulate the ₹1.32 Crore deficit over 8 years (96 months) at an expected CAGR of 11.0% p.a., what monthly SIP is required today if Rajesh adopts a fixed flat SIP vs. can his current ₹95,000 monthly surplus comfortably support this plan?

Question 2.5 • Decumulation: SWP vs. Life Insurance Annuity 2 Marks

Rajesh's insurance agent suggests buying an Immediate Life Annuity with the entire ₹4.07 Crore corpus offering 6.2% annual payout. Why should the Investment Adviser firmly reject this in favor of a 3-Bucket Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)?

CASE STUDY 3 OF 4 • 10 MARKS Core Theme: Budget 2024 Capital Gains Amendments, Section 54, 54EC & 54F Exemption Optimization

Client Dossier: Dr. Sanjeev & Radhika Singhania (Asset Sale & Tax Optimization)

Client Profile & Tax Bracket

• Dr. Sanjeev Singhania: Age 58, Chief of Orthopedics at a super-specialty hospital. Professional income: ₹65 Lakhs p.a.
• Radhika: Age 55, Clinical Pathologist & Lab Director. Net income: ₹20 Lakhs p.a.
• Tax Bracket: Highest individual income tax slab (30% + 15% Surcharge + 4% Cess = effective 35.88%).

Asset Liquidation Events

• 1. Ancestral House in Pune: Sold in August 2024 for ₹4.80 Crores (Cost of Acquisition in May 2004 was ₹80 Lakhs; CII in 2004-05 was 113, CII in 2024-25 is 363).
• 2. Unlisted Diagnostic Shares: Sold for ₹1.20 Crores (Purchased 5 years ago for ₹30 Lakhs).
• 3. Listed Equity Mutual Funds: Long-term capital gains realized: ₹45 Lakhs.

Intended Capital Deployment

• New Residential Apartment in Mumbai: Negotiated purchase price of ₹2.50 Crores within 1 year of sale.
• Section 54EC Bonds: Looking to invest maximum allowed in REC / PFC capital gains bonds.
• Residual Proceeds: To be deployed into multi-asset conservative income portfolio.

Case 3 Questions (5 Multi-Part Problems)

Question 3.1 • Real Estate LTCG under Budget 2024 Grandfathering Amendment 2 Marks

Under the revised Budget 2024 amendments to Section 112, resident individuals who acquired immovable property prior to July 23, 2024 have the option to pay tax at: (A) 20% with indexation OR (B) 12.5% without indexation, whichever is lower! Given the sale price of ₹4.80 Crores and indexed cost of ₹2.57 Crores (₹80L × 363 / 113), which option yields lower tax and what is the base tax payable before exemptions?

Question 3.2 • Section 54 Residential Property Exemption 2 Marks

The Long-Term Capital Gain from the Pune house under indexation is ₹2.23 Crores (₹4.80 Cr - ₹2.57 Cr). Dr. Singhania reinvests ₹2.50 Crores to purchase a new residential apartment in Mumbai within 1 year. Under Section 54 of the Income Tax Act, how much capital gain is exempt from income tax?

Question 3.3 • Section 54EC Capital Gain Bonds Rules 2 Marks

What is the maximum statutory investment limit per financial year under Section 54EC in specified bonds (REC, PFC, NHAI), what is their lock-in period, and how is the annual interest coupon taxed?

Question 3.4 • Unlisted Shares Capital Gains & Section 54F Proportionality 2 Marks

Dr. Singhania sold unlisted diagnostic shares for ₹1.20 Crores (Net Consideration) which generated a long-term capital gain of ₹90 Lakhs. If he wishes to claim exemption under Section 54F by investing in a residential house, what is the crucial condition regarding the investment amount compared to Section 54?

Question 3.5 • Equity Mutual Funds LTCG Tax Calculation (Budget 2024 Rates) 2 Marks

Dr. Singhania liquidated equity mutual funds units held for over 2 years, realizing a Long-Term Capital Gain of ₹45,00,000. Under the post-Budget 2024 tax regime (Section 112A), the annual exemption is increased to ₹1,25,000 and the tax rate is revised to 12.5%. What is the total tax payable on these gains (excluding surcharge and cess)?

CASE STUDY 4 OF 4 • 10 MARKS Core Theme: Private Family Trust, Special Needs Succession, Relative Exemptions & Portfolio Rebalancing

Client Dossier: Arvind & Kavita Mittal (Estate Planning & Trust Structuring)

Family & Succession Dynamics

• Arvind Mittal: Age 64, Patriarch & Founder of a precision components manufacturing enterprise.
• Kavita (Spouse): Age 62, Co-Director.
• Son Rohan: Age 34, Managing Director, actively running factory operations (married with 1 child).
• Daughter Pooja: Age 30, unmarried daughter with severe cognitive disability requiring lifetime medical care and legal guardianship.

Total Estate Value: ₹48 Crores

• Operating Business Shares: ₹24.00 Crores (60% unlisted equity).
• Commercial Real Estate: ₹6.00 Crores (generating ₹30 Lakhs annual rental income).
• Liquid Financial Portfolio: ₹8.00 Crores (Equity MFs: ₹6.0 Cr; Debt: ₹2.0 Cr; drifted to 75% Equity : 25% Debt).
• Residential Bungalow in Mumbai: ₹10.00 Crores.

Succession Objectives

• Pooja's Protection: Guarantee lifetime care, accommodation, and institutional medical funding insulated from family disputes or business bankruptcy.
• Business Continuity: Seamless transfer of operating company control to son Rohan without probate delays.
• Portfolio Risk Reduction: Restore liquid portfolio to Strategic Asset Allocation (40% Equity : 60% Debt).

Case 4 Questions (5 Multi-Part Problems)

Question 4.1 • Special Needs Trust Structuring vs. Will 2 Marks

Why is creating an Irrevocable Private Family Trust with a professional corporate trustee and protector significantly superior to relying solely on a testamentary Will for securing daughter Pooja's lifetime welfare?

Question 4.2 • Taxation of Private Trust for Dependent with Disability (Section 164) 2 Marks

Under Section 164(1) of the Income Tax Act, discretionary trusts are normally taxed at the Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR = 39% or 42.74%). What is the crucial statutory exception provided for a trust created solely for a beneficiary with disability (like Pooja)?

Question 4.3 • Business Share Transfer to Son Rohan (Section 56(2)(x) Relative Exemption) 2 Marks

Arvind transfers his unlisted company shares worth ₹24 Crores to his son Rohan via an executed Gift Deed during his lifetime. How is this transaction treated under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act?

Question 4.4 • Liquid Portfolio Rebalancing Calculation 2 Marks

Arvind's liquid portfolio is ₹8.00 Crores, currently standing at 75% Equity (₹6.00 Crores) and 25% Debt (₹2.00 Crores). His strategic target asset allocation is 40% Equity : 60% Debt. How much equity must be liquidated and shifted to debt to re-align with the target SAA?

Question 4.5 • Will Execution & Mandatory Probate Rules 2 Marks

Arvind executes a registered Will in Mumbai for his residual estate (including the ₹10 Crore residential bungalow in Mumbai). Under Section 213 and Section 57 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, is obtaining a court Probate mandatory for a Hindu testator's Will in Mumbai?
