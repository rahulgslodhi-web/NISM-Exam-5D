Memorize the new rates effective July 2024: STCG Sec 111A is 20% (up from 15%), LTCG Sec 112A is 12.5% (up from 10%), and the annual exemption is ₹1.25 Lakhs (up from ₹1 Lakh).

Section 11.1 • High Yield Focus
Sec 111A & 112A

Taxation of Listed Shares & Equity Mutual Funds

An Equity-Oriented Mutual Fund is defined under Section 112A as a fund where the investible funds are invested in equity shares of domestic companies to the extent of more than 65% of the total proceeds.

Section 111A 20% Flat Rate

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)

  • • Holding Period: Held for ≤ 12 Months.
  • • Condition: Transfer entered into on a recognized stock exchange where STT is paid.
  • • Tax Rate: 20% (raised from 15% by Finance (No. 2) Act 2024).
  • • Deductions: Chapter VI-A deductions are NOT allowable against 111A gains.
  • • Basic Exemption Adjustment: Resident individuals/HUFs can adjust unexhausted basic exemption against 111A gains.
Section 112A 12.5% above ₹1.25L

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)

  • • Holding Period: Held for > 12 Months.
  • • Condition: STT paid on acquisition and transfer (for shares) or transfer (for equity MF units).
  • • Annual Exemption: Gains up to ₹1,25,000 per financial year are completely EXEMPT.
  • • Tax Rate: Excess gains above ₹1.25 Lakh are taxed @ 12.5% without indexation.
  • • Grandfathering: Gains accrued up to 31 January 2018 protected.
Worked Example: Section 112A Tax Calculation

An investor realizes Long-Term Capital Gains of ₹3,75,000 from the sale of equity mutual fund units during FY 2024-25. STT was paid on redemption.

• Total LTCG Realized = ₹3,75,000

• Less: Annual Statutory Exemption under Section 112A = (−) ₹1,25,000

• Net Taxable LTCG = ₹2,50,000

• Base Tax @ 12.5% on ₹2,50,000 = ₹31,250

• Add: 4% Health & Education Cess = ₹1,250

• Total Tax Liability = ₹32,500

Section 11.2

Taxation of Unlisted Equity Shares (Startups & Private Companies)

Unlisted shares are shares of unlisted public or private limited companies. STT is not payable on unlisted share transfers.

Short-Term (≤ 24 Months)

Taxable under normal income heads at the investor's applicable marginal income tax slab rates.

Long-Term (> 24 Months)

Post Finance Act 2024, holding period is >24 months (reduced from 36m). Taxed @ 12.5% without indexation under Section 112 (unified rate).

Section 11.3

Securities Transaction Tax (STT) Framework

STT is a direct turnover tax levied on every purchase or sale of securities listed on recognized stock exchanges in India.

Taxable Securities Transaction STT Rate (Post FA 2024) Payable By
Equity Shares - Delivery Basis 0.1% Both Purchaser & Seller (0.1% each)
Equity Shares - Intraday (Non-Delivery) 0.025% Seller Only
Equity Derivatives - Futures 0.02% (Raised from 0.0125%) Seller Only
Equity Derivatives - Options Premium 0.1% (Raised from 0.0625%) Seller Only
Equity Mutual Fund Units (Sale to Fund / Exchange) 0.001% Seller Only
Deductibility Rule: STT paid is NOT allowed as a deduction against Capital Gains. It is deductible under Section 36(1)(xv) ONLY if the income from securities trading is assessed as business income under PGBP.
Section 11.4 • High Yield Focus
Anti-Avoidance

Dividend Stripping (Section 94(7))

Section 94(7) curbs the tax avoidance practice where an investor buys securities cum-dividend, collects dividend, and immediately sells ex-dividend at a capital loss to set off against other capital gains.

Three Cumulative Trigger Conditions:
Condition 1: Purchase Buys securities/units within 3 months prior to the Record Date.
Condition 2: Sale Sells securities within 3 months (or units of MFs within 9 months) after the Record Date.
Condition 3: Dividend Receives dividend or income that is exempt from income tax.
Consequence: Capital loss arising on such sale is DISALLOWED / IGNORED to the extent of the dividend income received!
Section 11.5 • High Yield Focus
Expanded to Shares

Bonus Stripping Provisions (Section 94(8))

Bonus stripping is an aggressive tax manoeuvre where an investor purchases units/shares cum-bonus, receives bonus units at zero cost, sells the original units at a reduced ex-bonus price to book a capital loss, while holding on to the bonus units.

Three Mandatory Conditions for Section 94(8):
  • Assessee acquires units or securities within 3 months prior to the record date.
  • Assessee is allotted additional bonus units/securities without any payment on the basis of such holding.
  • Assessee sells or transfers all or any of the original units/securities within 9 months after the record date, while continuing to hold all or any of the bonus units/securities.

Statutory Legal Consequences:

1. The capital loss arising on the sale of the original units is completely DISALLOWED.

2. The disallowed loss is DEEMED to be the Cost of Acquisition of the bonus units!

Important Scope Extension: Finance Act 2022 amended Section 94(8) to explicitly apply to both equity shares and mutual fund units (previously, it applied only to mutual funds).

Worked Numerical: Bonus Stripping Loss Adjustment

• Buys 1,000 shares of XYZ Ltd @ ₹150 = ₹1,50,000 on 1st July (Record date: 20th July).
• Receives 1:1 bonus (1,000 bonus shares) on 20th July.
• Sells original 1,000 shares on 10th October (within 9 months) @ ₹75 = ₹75,000.
• Realized apparent loss = ₹75,000 − ₹1,50,000 = −₹75,000.

Result: The loss of ₹75,000 cannot be set off against any capital gains. Instead, this ₹75,000 becomes the deemed Cost of Acquisition of the 1,000 bonus shares (₹75 per bonus share).
