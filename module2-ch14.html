Core Concepts

Stock Market Indices: Concept, Economic Functions & Desirable Attributes

A statistical barometer capturing collective market movements, serving as the cornerstone for valuation, asset allocation, and derivatives.

A stock market index is a statistical indicator that aggregates the price performance of a representative basket of underlying securities. It translates fluctuations in individual stock prices into a single composite number relative to a designated base period and base value (e.g., Nifty 50 with base year November 1995 = 1,000; BSE Sensex with base year 1978–79 = 100).

Five Vital Economic Functions of an Index

1. Macro Barometer

Acts as a real-time sentiment thermometer for the national economy, reflecting industrial output, corporate profitability expectations, and capital flows.

2. Fund Benchmark

Provides a standardized reference hurdle for evaluating active fund managers (Mutual Funds, PMS, AIFs) to distinguish true alpha from market beta.

3. F&O Underlying

Serves as the underlying asset for cash-settled Index Futures and Options, enabling cost-effective systematic risk hedging without stock delivery.

4. Passive Investing

Underpins Index Funds and Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) that physically replicate index constituents, offering low-cost market returns.

5. Risk Modeling & Asset Allocation

Represents the empirical "market portfolio" used in the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) to compute stock beta (β), Value-at-Risk (VaR), and institutional strategic asset allocation.

Desirable Attributes of a Well-Constructed Index
Broad Market Representation: Accurately mirrors the underlying industry sectors and macroeconomic composition.
High Investibility & Liquidity: Component stocks must have ample floating supply and low impact cost for seamless replication.
Rule-Based & Transparent: Inclusion, exclusion, and capping rules must be publicly documented, objective, and non-discretionary.
Low Portfolio Turnover: Periodic rebalancing should avoid excessive constituent churning, curtailing transaction costs for index trackers.

Weighting Methodologies

Index Construction & Weighting Methodologies

Comparing Price-Weighted, Full Market-Cap, Free-Float Market-Cap, and Equal-Weighted frameworks.

The weighting methodology dictates how much influence each constituent stock exerts on the index level. NISM repeatedly tests the mechanics, mathematical formulas, and inherent distortions of these four methodologies:

1. Free-Float Market Cap (Global Standard) Nifty 50, Sensex

Weights constituents strictly by their publicly tradable market capitalization (Share Price × Shares Outstanding × IWF).

Weighti = [Pricei × Sharesi × IWFi] / ∑ [Pricej × Sharesj × IWFj]

• Key Advantage: Reflects true investment capacity; immune to distortions from non-tradable promoter or sovereign blocks.

2. Full Market Cap Weighting Early BSE 500

Weights constituents by their total company market value (Share Price × Total Outstanding Shares), regardless of whether shares are tradable.

Weighti = [Pricei × Total Sharesi] / ∑ [Pricej × Total Sharesj]

• Critical Flaw: Companies with huge valuations but negligible public float (e.g. 90% PSU or promoter owned) receive huge index weights that cannot be replicated in practice.

3. Price-Weighted Index Dow Jones, Nikkei 225

Constituents are weighted solely by their absolute nominal share price. Total shares outstanding and company market cap are completely ignored.

Weighti = Pricei / ∑ Pricej

• Critical Exam Trap: A stock trading at ₹10,000 exerts 10× more influence than a stock at ₹1,000, even if the ₹1,000 company is 50× larger in market capitalization! A 2-for-1 stock split arbitrarily halves the company's index weight overnight.

4. Equal-Weighted Index Nifty 50 Equal Weight

Every constituent stock is assigned the exact same initial weight (e.g., 2.0% for each of the 50 stocks in Nifty 50 Equal Weight).

Weighti = 1 / N (where N = Number of constituents)

• Key Dynamics: Exhibits an inherent contrarian/value tilt. Periodic quarterly rebalancing forces selling recent winners (whose weight rose above 2%) and buying laggards (whose weight dropped below 2%). Higher portfolio turnover and tracking costs.

Free-Float & IWF

Free-Float Market Capitalization & Investible Weight Factor (IWF)

Mathematical calculation of investible float, regulatory exclusions, and weight determination.

Core Free-Float Formulations:

Full Market Cap = Current Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding

Free-Float Market Cap = Current Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding × Investible Weight Factor (IWF)

Investible Weight Factor (IWF) = (Total Shares Outstanding - Non-Free Float Shares) / Total Shares Outstanding

Free-Float Market Cap = Full Market Capitalization × IWF

The Investible Weight Factor (IWF) is a decimal factor between 0.00 and 1.00 that represents the proportion of total shares readily available to the general investing public on the open market.

Excluded from Free-Float (Non-Free Float)
  • Promoter & Promoter Group: All direct and indirect controlling promoter blocks.
  • Government Holdings: Central, State, statutory bodies, and strategic public holdings.
  • Strategic Cross-Holdings: Shares held by associate or group corporations.
  • Employee Welfare / ESOP Trusts: Shares locked in employee trusts.
  • Statutory & Pre-IPO Lock-ins: Anchor investor lock-ins, promoter minimum contribution lock-in.
  • GDR/ADR Underlying Equity: Shares not fungible for secondary domestic trading.
  • Pledged Shares: Promoter shares under litigation or custodial seizure.
Included in Free-Float (Tradable Universe)
  • Retail Individual Investors: Open market public holdings.
  • Domestic Mutual Funds: Open market mutual fund equity holdings.
  • Insurance Companies & Pension Funds: Life/General insurers, NPS, EPFO.
  • Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs/FIIs): Non-strategic institutional holdings.
  • Non-Promoter Corporate Bodies & Trusts: General corporate entities without board representation.
  • High Net Worth Individuals (HNIs): Non-promoter public investors.

Divisor Mechanics

Index Maintenance & Divisor Adjustments

Ensuring seamless mathematical continuity when corporate actions alter market capitalization.

The index level is calculated by scaling aggregate free-float market capitalization by a scaling constant called the Index Divisor:

Index Level = Aggregate Free-Float Market Capitalization / Index Divisor
Index Divisor = Aggregate Free-Float Market Capitalization / Index Level

The Divisor was originally established at index inception (Base Market Cap / Base Index Value) and remains fixed unless a capital event alters total market cap arbitrarily.

Why Must the Divisor Be Adjusted?

The index exists solely to capture genuine price movements reflecting investor sentiment and economic reality. When a corporate action (e.g. rights issue or constituent replacement) changes aggregate market cap without any change in investor valuation, leaving the Divisor constant would cause an artificial, non-market jump or drop in the index. The Divisor is adjusted immediately so that the index level before and after the event remains identical:

Index LevelOld = Index LevelNew &implies; New Divisor = New Market Capitalization / Existing Index Level

Master Divisor Adjustment Decision Matrix

Corporate Action Impact on Aggregate M-Cap Divisor Adjustment? Pedagogical Reason
Rights Issue Increases (New capital injected) YES • Increases New shares sold bring fresh cash into firm; market cap expands arbitrarily.
Constituent Replacement Changes (Incoming ≠ Outgoing) YES • Adjusted Replacing Stock X with Stock Y of different free float changes total market cap.
Stock Split (e.g. 2 for 1) ZERO change NO • Unchanged Share price halves, share count doubles. Price × Shares remains identical!
Bonus Issue (Stock Dividend) ZERO change NO • Unchanged Price falls proportionally to bonus ratio; total company capitalization unchanged.
Secondary Offering / QIP Increases YES • Increases Fresh shares issued to institutional buyers expand free float market cap.
Share Buyback & Cancellation Decreases YES • Decreases Company extinguishes shares; total outstanding float contracts.
Ordinary Cash Dividend Zero (in Price Return Index) NO • Unchanged Price Return Index (PRI) ignores cash dividends; TRI incorporates reinvestment.
Worked Numerical Example: Stock Replacement Divisor Recalibration

Suppose an index stands at 20,000.00 with an aggregate Free-Float Market Cap of ₹20,00,000 Crore.

• Initial Divisor = ₹20,00,000 Cr / 20,000 = 100.00

• Index Rebalancing Event: Stock X (Free-Float M-Cap = ₹30,000 Cr) is removed, and Stock Y (Free-Float M-Cap = ₹80,000 Cr) is inducted.

• New Aggregate Free-Float M-Cap = ₹20,00,000 - ₹30,000 + ₹80,000 = ₹20,50,000 Crore

• New Divisor = New M-Cap / Existing Index Level = ₹20,50,000 / 20,000 = 102.50

• Verification: New Index Level = ₹20,50,000 / 102.50 = 20,000.00 (Seamless continuity preserved!)

Liquidity Metric

Impact Cost: Practical Measure of Market Liquidity

Quantifying institutional order execution slippage against the pre-trade mid-quote price.

While the simple Bid-Ask Spread indicates the price gap for the single best bid and ask, it fails to measure liquidity for institutional orders (e.g. ₹50 Lakhs or 10,000 shares) that consume multiple price tiers down the order book. Impact Cost measures the actual percentage slippage incurred relative to the ideal pre-trade price (mid-point of best bid and best ask).

Standard Impact Cost Formulations:

Ideal Price (Mid-Quote) = (Best Bid Price + Best Ask Price) / 2

Actual Execution Price = Weighted Average Price across executed limit order tiers

Buy Impact Cost (%) = [ (Actual Buy Price - Ideal Price) / Ideal Price ] × 100

Sell Impact Cost (%) = [ (Ideal Price - Actual Sell Price) / Ideal Price ] × 100

Key Axiom: Lower impact cost represents deeper liquidity and tighter order books. Impact cost is always positive for executed market orders.

Step 1: Calculate Ideal Pre-Trade Price:

• Ideal Price = (Best Bid + Best Ask) / 2 = (₹199.00 + ₹201.00) / 2 = ₹200.00

Case A: Buy Order of 2,000 Shares:

• Buys 1,000 shares @ ₹201.00 (from 1st Ask layer) = ₹2,01,000

• Buys remaining 1,000 shares @ ₹202.00 (from 2nd Ask layer) = ₹2,02,000

• Total Cost = ₹2,01,000 + ₹2,02,000 = ₹4,03,000

• Actual Buy Price = ₹4,03,000 / 2,000 = ₹201.50

• Buy Impact Cost = [ (201.50 - 200.00) / 200.00 ] × 100 = +0.75%

Case B: Sell Order of 2,000 Shares:

• Sells 1,000 shares @ ₹199.00 (to 1st Bid layer) = ₹1,99,000

• Sells remaining 1,000 shares @ ₹198.00 (to 2nd Bid layer) = ₹1,98,000

• Total Proceeds = ₹1,99,000 + ₹1,98,000 = ₹3,97,000

• Actual Sell Price = ₹3,97,000 / 2,000 = ₹198.50

• Sell Impact Cost = [ (200.00 - 198.50) / 200.00 ] × 100 = +0.75%

Key Takeaways on Impact Cost:
  • Impact cost increases as order size increases (a 10,000 share order cuts deeper into the book than a 2,000 share order).
  • Impact cost varies continuously throughout the trading day as quotes enter and cancel.
  • SEBI mandates impact cost evaluation over a 6-month trailing period for index eligibility.

Regulatory Framework

SEBI Eligibility Norms for Derivatives on Equity Indices

Concentration caps, constituent liquidity criteria, and governance standards for index F&O trading.

To prevent market cornering, excessive single-stock dominance, and price manipulation in derivative settlements, SEBI prescribes strict eligibility criteria for equity indices permitted for futures and options trading:

1. Constituent Count

The index must contain a minimum of 10 constituent stocks. Sectoral or thematic indices with fewer than 10 stocks cannot launch derivatives.

2. Diversification Caps
  • Single Stock Cap: No individual stock weight can exceed 33%.
  • Top 3 Stocks Cap: Cumulative weight of top 3 stocks cannot exceed 62%.
3. Constituent Liquidity
  • Traded on at least 80% of trading days over prior 6 months.
  • Average impact cost ≤ 1.0% for order size of ₹50 Lakhs over prior 6 months.
Index Governance & Surveillance Protocols

• Independent Administration: The index must be maintained by an independent professional index provider with public rules and established index committees.
• Rebalancing Frequency: Evaluated periodically (quarterly or semi-annually).
• Surveillance & Breach Cure Period: If constituent price moves cause a stock's weight to exceed 33% or top 3 stocks to exceed 62%, the index provider enforces an immediate re-capping adjustment at the subsequent scheduled rebalance to maintain derivative eligibility.

Section 14.7 • Regulatory Mandate
Benchmarking Standards

Total Return Index (TRI) vs Price Return Index (PRI)

SEBI's mandatory benchmarking transition for Indian Mutual Funds.

Price Return Index (PRI)

Measures only capital gains/losses arising from changes in constituent share prices. Cash dividends declared by constituents are totally omitted from the index calculation.

• Flaw: Understates true total equity return by 1.2% to 1.8% per annum (the average dividend yield of Indian equities).

Total Return Index (TRI)

Measures capital price appreciation PLUS reinvestment of gross cash dividends paid by constituent companies on their ex-dividend dates into the index basket.

• Gold Standard: Accurately reflects the total wealth generated for a buy-and-hold index investor.

SEBI Mandate (Effective February 1, 2018):

SEBI mandated that all Indian Mutual Funds must benchmark their scheme performance against the Total Return Index (TRI) variant rather than the Price Return Index (PRI).

Rationale: Prior to 2018, mutual fund portfolios (which naturally collect and reinvest dividends) benchmarked against PRI, artificially overstating their generated "alpha" by ~1.5% annually. Benchmarking against TRI provides honest, apples-to-apples transparency to unitholders.
