Section 8.1

Capital Asset (Sec 2(14)) & Transfer (Sec 2(47)) Defined

Under Section 45(1), any profits or gains arising from the transfer of a capital asset effected in the previous year are chargeable to income tax under the head "Capital Gains".

Capital Asset Includes:

  • Property of any kind held by an assessee, whether connected with business or not.
  • Securities held by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs/FPIs).
  • Jewellery, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, or any work of art.

Capital Asset Specifically EXCLUDES:

  • Stock-in-trade: Consumable stores or raw materials held for business (taxable under PGBP).
  • Personal effects: Movable personal items (wearing apparel, furniture, cars, mobile phones).
  • Rural Agricultural Land: Agricultural land in India outside specified municipal limits.
  • Specified Gold Bonds (6.5% Gold Bonds 1977, National Defence Gold Bonds 1980, Special Bearer Bonds 1991, Gold Deposit Bonds / Deposit Certificates under Gold Monetisation Scheme 2015).

FA 2024 Rationalized

Holding Periods: Short-Term vs Long-Term Capital Assets

The Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 streamlined holding periods into two universal tiers: 12 months for listed financial assets and 24 months for all other assets (eliminating the previous 36-month threshold).

Section 8.3 & 8.4

Computation of Capital Gains & Indexation Framework

Standard Computation Equation (Sec 48):

Full Value of Consideration (Sale Price)

(−) Transfer Expenses (Brokerage, commission, stamp duties on transfer)

= Net Consideration

(−) Cost of Acquisition / Indexed Cost of Acquisition (ICOA)

(−) Cost of Improvement / Indexed Cost of Improvement (ICOI)

= Gross Capital Gains (STCG / LTCG)

(−) Exemptions under Sections 54 / 54EC / 54F

= Net Taxable Capital Gains

Indexed Cost of Acquisition (ICOA) Formula:

ICOA = Cost of Acquisition × (CII of Year of Sale ÷ CII of Year of Purchase or 2001-02, whichever is later)

Base Year Rule: For assets acquired prior to 1 April 2001, the Cost of Acquisition is taken as Higher of: (Actual Cost) and (Fair Market Value as on 1 April 2001), capped at the stamp duty value (SDV) on 1 April 2001 for immovable property.

⭐ Real Estate Grandfathering Option (Finance (No. 2) Act 2024):

For immovable property (land or building) acquired BEFORE 23rd July 2024, resident individuals and HUFs are given a taxpayer-friendly dual choice:

  • Option A: Pay tax @ 12.5% without indexation.
  • Option B: Pay tax @ 20% with indexation.

The assessee can calculate under both methods and pay whichever tax amount is LOWER! For property acquired on or after 23 July 2024, the tax is strictly 12.5% without indexation.

Section 8.5 • High Yield Focus
Sec 112A Math

Grandfathering Provisions under Section 112A for Listed Equities

When Long-Term Capital Gains on listed equities and equity mutual funds were re-introduced from 1 April 2018, all capital gains accrued up to 31 January 2018 were grandfathered (exempted from tax).

Deemed Cost of Acquisition Formula:

For assets acquired on or before 31st January 2018, the Cost of Acquisition is determined as:

Cost of Acquisition = HIGHER of:

1. Actual Cost of Acquisition

2. LOWER of:

(a) Fair Market Value (FMV) as on 31 January 2018*

(b) Full Value of Consideration (Actual Sale Price)

*FMV is the highest traded price on 31-Jan-2018 for listed shares, or the NAV on 31-Jan-2018 for mutual fund units.

Worked Numerical Examples (Must Memorize):

Scenario 1: Full Gain Protected

• Actual Cost: ₹100

• FMV on 31-Jan-2018: ₹150

• Sale Price: ₹220

Lower of (150, 220) = 150.
Higher of (100, 150) = ₹150 (Cost).
LTCG = ₹220 − ₹150 = ₹70

Scenario 2: Sold Below FMV

• Actual Cost: ₹100

• FMV on 31-Jan-2018: ₹150

• Sale Price: ₹130

Lower of (150, 130) = 130.
Higher of (100, 130) = ₹130 (Cost).
LTCG = ₹130 − ₹130 = ₹0 (No gain/loss)

Scenario 3: FMV Below Cost

• Actual Cost: ₹100

• FMV on 31-Jan-2018: ₹80

• Sale Price: ₹140

Lower of (80, 140) = 80.
Higher of (100, 80) = ₹100 (Cost).
LTCG = ₹140 − ₹100 = ₹40

Exemption Threshold: Post Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, LTCG under Section 112A is exempt up to ₹1,25,000 per financial year (raised from ₹1,00,000), and the excess is taxed @ 12.5% (raised from 10%).

Section 54F Proportionate Exemption Formula:

Unlike Section 54 (which requires reinvesting only the capital gain), Section 54F requires reinvesting the entire Net Sale Consideration. If only a part is reinvested, proportionate exemption is granted:

Exemption under Sec 54F = Long-Term Capital Gain × (Amount Reinvested ÷ Net Consideration)

Worked Example: Listed shares sold for Net Consideration ₹1 Crore generating LTCG of ₹60 Lakhs. The investor reinvests ₹50 Lakhs in a new residential flat. Exemption = ₹60L × (₹50L ÷ ₹100L) = ₹30 Lakhs. Remaining ₹30 Lakhs is taxable LTCG.

Section 8.7

Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS), 1988

If the capital gain (for Sec 54) or net consideration (for Sec 54F) is not utilized for purchase/construction of the new house before the due date of filing the income tax return under Section 139(1), the unutilized amount MUST be deposited into a Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) with an authorized bank to claim the exemption in that tax return.

Account Types in CGAS

Account 'A' (Savings Deposit): For ongoing piecemeal construction payments.
Account 'B' (Term Deposit): Suitable when full funds are required on a fixed future date for purchase.

Failure to Utilize Funds

If the deposited amount is not utilized within the prescribed period (3 years from date of transfer of original asset), the unutilized balance is treated and taxed as Long-Term Capital Gain of the previous year in which the 3-year period expires.
