10-Year GoI Bond IRF

10-Year GoI Bond Futures Contract Specifications

In January 2014, SEBI and RBI introduced single-bond, cash-settled Interest Rate Futures on 10-year Government of India (GoI) benchmark securities on exchange platforms (NSE, BSE). The standardized contract features are heavily examined.

Contract Parameter Regulatory Specification (SEBI & RBI Prescribed)
Underlying Instrument Specific on-the-run 10-Year Government of India dated security (e.g., 7.18% GS 2033 or current 10-Yr benchmark bond). Single-bond contract design.
Contract Lot Size / Multiplier 2,000 underlying bonds with Face Value of ₹100 each.
Total Face Value per Contract = 2,000 × ₹100 = ₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakhs)
Quotation Convention Quoted as Clean Price per ₹100 face value in INR, with 4 decimal places (e.g., ₹99.8525). Excludes accrued interest.
Minimum Tick Size ₹0.0025 (which equals 0.25 paise or one-fourth of a paisa).
Monetary Tick Value (per contract) ₹5.00 per contract for every minimum tick movement of ₹0.0025.
Trading Cycle / Tenors Available 3 serial monthly contracts (near-month, next-month, far-month) followed by 4 quarterly contracts of the March, June, September, December cycle.
Expiry / Last Trading Day Last Thursday of the contract month. If the last Thursday is an exchange trading holiday, the contract expires on the preceding business day.
Trading Hours 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM (Monday through Friday). Aligned directly with the trading hours of the interbank secondary G-Sec market (NDS-OM).
Settlement Mechanism 100% Cash Settled in Indian Rupees against the Final Settlement Price (FSP). No physical delivery of government bonds takes place.
Daily Price Operating Range Dynamic price bands of ±3% of the previous day's closing settlement price, flexibly adjusted upon touching the limit during trading hours to prevent market disruption.
Step-by-Step Derivation of Tick Value (₹5.00) • Tested in Every Exam

Understanding why 1 tick equals exactly ₹5.00 is essential for both numerical questions and trading profit/loss computations:

Step 1: Face Value & Units

Face value of 1 bond = ₹100.
Contract lot size = 2,000 bonds.
Total Contract Face Value = ₹2,00,000.

Step 2: Quotation & Tick Size

Price is quoted per ₹100 face value.
Minimum tick size = ₹0.0025 (0.25 paise per ₹100 face value bond).

Step 3: Multiplication Formula

Tick Value = Lot Size × Tick Size
= 2,000 × ₹0.0025
= ₹5.00 per contract

Alternative Formula: Tick Value = (Contract Face Value / 100) × Tick Size (₹2,00,000 / 100) × 0.0025 = 2,000 × 0.0025 = ₹5.00
Numerical Application Example: Tick Movement to Profit/Loss

A portfolio manager buys 25 contracts of 10-year GoI Bond Futures at ₹99.4500. Later in the day, the market rallies and she closes the position at ₹99.6000. Calculate the gross profit.

1. Price change per bond = ₹99.6000 - ₹99.4500 = ₹0.1500 (15 paise).
2. Number of ticks moved = ₹0.1500 / ₹0.0025 = 60 ticks.
3. Profit per single contract = 60 ticks × ₹5.00 = ₹300.00.
4. Total profit for 25 contracts = 25 × ₹300.00 = ₹7,500.00.

Underlying Instruments: 10-Yr G-Sec vs. 91-DTB

The Reserve Bank of India permits exchange trading of interest rate futures across both short-term money market bills and long-term sovereign benchmark bonds.

Feature 10-Year Benchmark GoI Bond Futures 91-Day Treasury Bill Futures (91-DTB)
Underlying Instrument Specific on-the-run 10-Year Central Government coupon-bearing bond. 91-Day Government of India Treasury Bill (zero-coupon money market paper).
Quotation Mechanism Clean price per ₹100 face value (e.g., ₹99.8525). Quoted as 100 minus Annualized Discount Yield (e.g., 100 - 6.75 = 93.25).
Contract Lot Size 2,000 bonds (₹2,00,000 Face Value). 2,000 units (₹2,00,000 Face Value).
Tick Size & Tick Value ₹0.0025 tick size → ₹5.00 tick value. ₹0.0025 tick size → ₹5.00 tick value.
Final Settlement Benchmark NDS-OM last 2 hours (3:00 PM - 5:00 PM) VWAP price. Weighted average cut-off yield in the weekly RBI 91-DTB primary auction on expiry day.
Settlement Type 100% Cash Settled in INR. 100% Cash Settled in INR.
How 91-DTB Quotation Works (100 - Yield)

In global and Indian money markets, short-term debt instruments are traded in terms of yield. To make futures trade like a standard asset (where buying low and selling high produces a profit), the contract price is quoted as:

Quoted Futures Price = 100 - Annualized Discount Yield (Y)

• If the 91-day T-bill annualized yield is 6.85%, the quoted futures price is 100 - 6.85 = 93.15.
• If yields drop to 6.50% (bullish for bonds), the price rises to 100 - 6.50 = 93.50. Long position profits!

Theoretical Pricing

Pricing Interest Rate Futures: Cost of Carry Framework

Like all financial futures, IRF pricing relies on the Cost of Carry Model. However, because government bonds pay semi-annual coupons and trade on a clean price plus accrued interest basis, forward pricing requires distinct dirty and clean price adjustments.

Spot Clean Price vs Spot Dirty Price

Clean Price: The agreed quoted market price of the bond excluding accrued coupon interest.
Dirty (Invoice) Price: The actual total cash amount paid to acquire the bond on spot settlement date:

Spot Dirty Price = Spot Clean Price + Accrued Interest

Accrued interest is calculated from the last coupon payment date to the spot settlement date (typically using 30/360 day count for Indian G-Secs).

Forward Dirty vs Forward Clean Price

To determine the fair futures quotation, calculate the forward dirty price and subtract the coupon interest accrued up to the futures expiry date:

Futures Clean Price = Futures Dirty Price - Accrued Interest at Expiry

Because exchange IRF contracts are quoted in clean price terms, the theoretical clean price is the benchmark traded on screen.

Cost of Carry Mathematical Model
1. Theoretical Forward Dirty Price (Fdirty):
Fdirty = Sdirty × [1 + r × (T / 365)] - Future Value of Interim Coupon (if any)
Where Sdirty = Current spot dirty price, r = Money market repo financing rate, T = Days to futures contract expiration.
2. Theoretical Forward Clean Price (Fclean):
Fclean = Fdirty - Accrued Interest from previous coupon date to Futures Expiration Date
Worked Exam Numerical 1: Zero Interim Coupon Payment
Problem: A 10-year GoI bond has a Spot Clean Price of ₹101.50 and accrued interest to spot date of ₹1.20 (making Spot Dirty Price = ₹102.70). The repo borrowing rate is 6.50% p.a. The futures contract expires in 73 days (0.20 year). No coupon payment occurs during the 73-day tenure. Accrued interest at futures expiry will be ₹2.64. Calculate:
  1. Financing cost of carry
  2. Theoretical Forward Dirty Price
  3. Theoretical Forward Clean Price (fair screen quotation)
1. Financing Cost = Sdirty × r × (T / 365) = 102.70 × 0.065 × (73 / 365) = 102.70 × 0.065 × 0.20 = ₹1.3351.
2. Fdirty = Sdirty + Financing Cost = 102.70 + 1.3351 = ₹104.0351.
3. Fclean = Fdirty - Accrued Interest at Expiry = 104.0351 - 2.64 = ₹101.3951.
Result: The fair futures contract screen price is ₹101.3951 (or ₹101.3950 rounded to tick size).
Worked Exam Numerical 2: Bond with Interim Coupon Inflow

When a coupon is paid by the government before futures expiration, the bondholder receives cash income. This cash reduces the carrying cost of holding the bond, thereby lowering the theoretical forward price:

• Spot Dirty Price = ₹103.00 | Financing Cost to Expiry = ₹1.50 | Total Cost before coupon = ₹104.50.
• Semi-Annual Coupon of ₹3.60 received 30 days before expiry; reinvestment income earned = ₹0.02.
• Future Value of Coupon at Expiry = ₹3.60 + ₹0.02 = ₹3.62.
• Fdirty = ₹104.50 - ₹3.62 = ₹100.88.
• If accrued interest at expiry is ₹0.60, then Fclean = 100.88 - 0.60 = ₹100.28.
Scenario Market Condition Arbitrage Strategy Execution Mechanics
Cash & Carry Arbitrage Futures Market Price > Theoretical Fair Price (Futures is Overpriced). Buy Spot Bond + Sell (Short) Futures Borrow funds at repo rate, purchase physical G-Sec in spot market, simultaneously short overpriced IRF. Deliver/settle at expiry to lock in riskless spread.
Reverse Cash & Carry Arbitrage Futures Market Price < Theoretical Fair Price (Futures is Underpriced). Sell (Short) Spot Bond + Buy (Long) Futures Short sell G-Sec via RBI repo window, lend cash proceeds at reverse repo rate, buy cheap IRF. Re-absorb position at expiry to capture spread.

Settlement Protocol

Cash Settlement & Final Settlement Price (FSP) Determination

Earlier IRF contracts in 2003 and 2009 failed due to physical delivery basket disputes and Cheapest-to-Deliver (CTD) manipulation. The modern framework relies entirely on 100% Cash Settlement linked directly to RBI's institutional bond dealing screen.

The Exact FSP Computation Rule (Tested Extensively)

On the expiration day (last Thursday of the month), all open interest positions are automatically closed out and cash settled against the Final Settlement Price (FSP):

FSP = Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP) of the underlying bond traded on RBI's NDS-OM platform during the LAST TWO HOURS of trading (between 3:00 PM and 5:00 PM).
NDS-OM Institutional Platform: Negotiated Dealing System - Order Matching is the anonymous electronic order book operated by Clearing Corporation of India (CCIL) for the RBI.
Fallback Procedure: If no trades or insufficient trading volume occurs on NDS-OM between 3:00 PM and 5:00 PM, the FSP is taken from the theoretical valuation matrix published by FIMMDA / FBIL.
1. Daily Mark-to-Market (MTM) Settlement
  • Occurs every regular trading day after 5:00 PM.
  • Daily Settlement Price is based on the VWAP of the IRF contract during the last 30 minutes of exchange trading.
  • Profits and losses are debited/credited in cash to the trading account on a T+1 basis before market opens.
2. Final Cash Settlement on Expiry Day
  • Occurs on the contract expiration date (last Thursday).
  • Position is settled against the FSP (NDS-OM 3:00 PM - 5:00 PM VWAP).
  • Final Cash Flow = (FSP - Previous Day's Settlement Price) × 2,000 × No. of Contracts.
  • All open obligations terminate with zero physical delivery hassle.

Margins & Limits

Margining Architecture & Position Limits

The clearing corporations (NSE Clearing Ltd & Indian Clearing Corporation Ltd) employ multi-layered risk containment for IRF.

Initial Margin (SPAN)

Calculated using the SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) system. Evaluates the portfolio under 16 simulated risk scenarios. Scaled to cover 99% Value-at-Risk (VaR) over a 1-day holding period.

Typical Range: 1.5% to 2.3% of contract value
Extreme Loss Margin (ELM)

A fixed prudential margin collected upfront to cover tail-risk market events exceeding the 99% VaR confidence level.

Prescribed Rate: Fixed at 0.5% of Contract Value (Compared to 3.5% for Equities)
Calendar Spread Margin

When a trader holds offsetting positions across two different expiry months (e.g. Long near-month and Short far-month), directional risk cancels out.

Substantially Lower Margin: Typically fixed at ₹1,500 - ₹2,500 per spread
Entity Category Gross Open Position Limit (Exchange-Level)
Trading Member (Broker) 15% of total open interest or ₹1,000 Crores, whichever is higher.
Mutual Fund / AMC 10% of total open interest or ₹500 Crores per mutual fund scheme.
Client / Individual Investor 3% of total open interest or ₹200 Crores, whichever is higher.
Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI) Monitored separately under overall sovereign debt investment ceilings specified by RBI and SEBI.

Portfolio Management

Hedging & Duration Immunization with IRF

Mutual funds and Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs) frequently utilize 10-year GoI Bond Futures to protect debt portfolios against adverse yield movements without selling underlying bonds into an illiquid secondary cash market.

Short Hedge (Bearish / Rate Hike Defense)

Objective: Protect an existing bond portfolio against rising interest rates (falling bond prices).
Action: Sell (Short) IRF contracts. If market interest rates rise, bond portfolio value declines, but the short IRF position gains in value, offsetting the cash loss.

Long Hedge (Bullish / Rate Cut & Cash Inflow)

Objective: Lock in current yields for anticipated future cash inflows (e.g. maturing fixed deposits or scheme subscription inflows).
Action: Buy (Long) IRF contracts. If market interest rates fall, bond prices rise. The gain on long IRF offsets the higher price of purchasing physical bonds later.

Duration-Based Hedge Ratio Formula

Because bonds of different maturities and coupons have different sensitivities to interest rates, hedging requires matching Modified Duration (MD):

Number of Contracts (N) = - [ (Vp × Dp) / (Vf × Df) ]
Vp: Value of Cash Portfolio
Dp: Modified Duration of Portfolio
Vf: Value of 1 IRF Contract (Price × 2,000)
Df: Modified Duration of IRF Underlying Bond
Worked Exam Numerical 3: Immunizing a ₹50 Crore Mutual Fund Portfolio
Problem Statement: An Asset Management Company manages a G-Sec mutual fund portfolio with a current market value of ₹50,00,00,000 (₹50 Crores) and a Modified Duration of 6.8 years. The fund manager expects the RBI to hike repo rates and wants to completely immunize the portfolio against rate hikes using 10-year GoI Bond Futures.
The futures contract is trading at ₹100.00 (Lot size = 2,000 bonds; Contract Value = 2,000 × ₹100 = ₹2,00,000). The underlying benchmark bond has a Modified Duration of 6.5 years.
Questions:
  1. How many IRF contracts must the fund manager short?
  2. Verify the hedge if market yields immediately rise by 25 basis points (0.25%).
Step 1: Calculate Total Portfolio Dollar Duration (Sensitivity):
SensitivityPortfolio = Vp × Dp = ₹50,00,00,000 × 6.8 = ₹3,40,00,00,000
Step 2: Calculate Single IRF Contract Sensitivity:
SensitivityFutures = Vf × Df = ₹2,00,000 × 6.5 = ₹13,00,000
Step 3: Compute Number of Contracts (N):
N = ₹3,40,00,00,000 / ₹13,00,000 = 261.54 contracts → Short 262 contracts
Step 4: Proof of Immunization against a 25 bps (+0.25%) Yield Surge:
  • Loss on Cash Portfolio = - Vp × Dp × Δy = - 50 Cr × 6.8 × 0.0025 = - ₹85,00,000.
  • Gain on 262 Short IRF = + 262 × (2,00,000 × 6.5 × 0.0025) = 262 × ₹3,250 = + ₹85,15,000.
  • Net P&L = + ₹15,000 (Near-perfect immunization achieved!).
