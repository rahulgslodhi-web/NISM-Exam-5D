Section 4.1 • ⚡ Fast-Track Refresher
Statutory Architecture

Financial Regulators in India & SEBI's Mandate

The Indian financial system is regulated by specialized statutory bodies coordinated under the High-Level Committee on Financial Markets and the Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC) chaired by the Union Finance Minister.

RBI Central Bank

Governs banking system, money markets, government securities (G-Secs), foreign exchange (FEMA), and monetary policy.

SEBI Securities Apex

Apex statutory regulator for securities markets, stock exchanges, mutual funds, RTAs, custodians, portfolio managers, and SIFs.

IRDAI Insurance

Regulates life and general insurance companies, insurance intermediaries, reinsurance, and ULIP market conduct.

PFRDA Pensions

Regulates the National Pension System (NPS), Atal Pension Yojana, Pension Funds, Central Recordkeeping Agencies (CRAs), and POPs.

SEBI's Statutory Mandate & Regulatory Powers

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) was established as a statutory body on April 12, 1992 in accordance with the provisions of the SEBI Act, 1992. The Preamble of the SEBI Act mandates a threefold objective:

1. Protecting the interests of investors in securities
2. Promoting the development of the securities market
3. Regulating the securities market & intermediaries

• SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996: Comprehensive regulations governing registration of mutual funds, approval of Sponsors/AMCs/Trustees, portfolio valuation norms, investment limits, disclosure standards, and scheme categorization.

• Regulatory & Investigative Powers: Under Section 11, 11B (power to issue directions in investor interest), 11D (cease and desist orders), Section 15 (adjudication of monetary penalties), and Section 24 (criminal prosecution through Special Courts), SEBI can conduct inspections, audit books of AMCs/Trustees, levy penalties, freeze folios, and debar errant entities.

SEBI Categorization

Comprehensive SEBI Scheme Categorization Framework

Issued in October 2017 (and continuously updated), SEBI's categorization framework ensures "True to Label" schemes. An AMC is permitted to offer only ONE scheme per category (with strict exceptions for Index Funds/ETFs tracking different indices, overseas FoFs, and Sectoral/Thematic funds).

Standardized Market Capitalization Definition (AMFI Semi-Annual List):

To ensure consistency across all AMCs, AMFI prepares a standardized list of stocks based on average full market capitalization of the preceding 6 months (updated as on June 30 and December 31 each year):

1st to 100th Large Cap Companies
101st to 250th Mid Cap Companies
251st company onwards Small Cap Companies

All equity schemes (except Multi Cap and ELSS) must allocate a defined minimum percentage to equity according to their investment strategy.

2 Debt Schemes (16 Categories based on Macaulay Duration & Credit Quality)

Debt schemes are standardized based on the Macaulay duration of the portfolio or specific issuer types.

1. Overnight Fund

Securities having maturity of 1 business day. Nil interest rate and credit risk.

2. Liquid Fund

Securities with maturity up to 91 days only. Graded exit load if redeemed within 7 days.

3. Ultra Short Duration Fund

Macaulay duration of portfolio between 3 months and 6 months.

4. Low Duration Fund

Macaulay duration between 6 months and 12 months.

5. Money Market Fund

Invests in money market instruments having maturity up to 1 year (CPs, CDs, T-Bills, TREPS).

6. Short Duration Fund

Macaulay duration between 1 year and 3 years.

7. Medium Duration Fund

Macaulay duration between 3 years and 4 years.

8. Medium to Long Duration Fund

Macaulay duration between 4 years and 7 years.

9. Long Duration Fund

Macaulay duration greater than 7 years.

10. Dynamic Bond Fund

Investment across durations dynamically managed based on interest rate cycle outlook.

11. Corporate Bond Fund

Minimum 80% in highest rated corporate bonds rated AA+ and above.

12. Credit Risk Fund

Minimum 65% in corporate bonds rated AA and below (excluding AA+ rated instruments).

13. Banking and PSU Fund

Minimum 80% in debt instruments of Banks, Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), and PFIs.

14. Gilt Fund

Minimum 80% in Government Securities (G-Secs) across maturity horizons. Nil credit risk.

15. Gilt Fund (10-Yr Constant Maturity)

Minimum 80% in G-Secs such that portfolio Macaulay duration is equal to 10 years.

16. Floater Fund

Minimum 65% in floating rate instruments (or fixed rate debt swapped to floating rate).

Solution Oriented Schemes (5-Year Lock-in)

1. Retirement Fund: Lock-in of 5 years or till retirement age (whichever is earlier).
2. Children's Fund: Lock-in of 5 years or till child attains age of majority (whichever is earlier).

Other Schemes (Index, ETF, FoF)

• Index Funds / ETFs: Minimum 95% in securities of the tracking index.
• FoF (Domestic): Minimum 95% in units of underlying domestic MF.
• FoF (Overseas): Minimum 95% in units of overseas mutual funds/ETFs.

SIF & MF Lite Frameworks

Specialized Investment Funds (SIF) & MF Lite

The landmark regulatory reforms introduced by SEBI bridge the structural gap between retail mutual funds, PMS, and AIFs, while simultaneously liberalizing passive funds under MF Lite.

New Asset Class

Specialized Investment Fund (SIF)

Minimum Ticket: ₹10 Lakhs
Market Positioning

Fills the gap between Mutual Funds (₹500), PMS (₹50 Lakhs), and Category III AIFs (₹1 Crore). Designed for sophisticated HNIs.

₹10 Lakh Investment Rule

The minimum ticket of ₹10 Lakhs applies per investor across all SIF investment strategies offered by a single AMC.

Derivative Flexibility

Permitted to take unhedged derivative positions, complex options strategies, and participate in short selling via SLB.

Three Permitted SIF Investment Strategies:
1. Long-Short Equity: Combines long equity positions with short derivative exposure to generate alpha in bull or bear markets.
2. Inverse / Leveraged Strategies: Uses exchange-traded derivatives to deliver inverse or multi-factor index returns.
3. Specialized Debt / Hybrid: Employs structured credit, opportunistic high-yield debt, and dynamic cross-asset derivatives.
Mandatory SIF Branding Rule: SIF offerings must have a distinct brand name and logo separate from the regular mutual fund. In all marketing collaterals, the font size of the sponsor/mutual fund's brand name must be equal to or smaller than the SIF brand name.

Mutual Fund Lite (MF Lite) for Passive Schemes

Passive Only

SEBI created MF Lite as a differentiated, relaxed regulatory regime specifically for AMCs managing exclusively passive schemes (Index Funds and Exchange Traded Funds).

Relaxed Sponsor Entry

Lower net worth requirements and relaxed 5-year track record/profitability norms compared to traditional active AMCs.

Simplified Governance

Reduced reporting overheads, streamlined trustee oversight, and simplified disclosure mandates since index replication is rule-based.

Direct Investor Benefit

Substantially lower compliance and operational costs enable ultra-low Total Expense Ratios (TER) for retail passive investors.

Investment Restrictions

Key Investment Restrictions (Schedule 7 & Regulations)

The Seventh Schedule of SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 sets prudential investment limits to prevent concentration risk, self-dealing, and illiquidity.

1. Delivery Basis & Derivatives

Every mutual fund shall buy and sell securities strictly on a delivery basis (take actual physical/demat delivery on purchases and deliver upon sales). Speculative intraday square-offs without delivery are prohibited.
Exceptions: Permitted exchange-traded derivative transactions for hedging/portfolio rebalancing and short selling under SEBI's Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) framework.

2. Single Issuer Equity Limits

A mutual fund scheme cannot invest more than 10% of its NAV in equity shares or equity-related instruments of a single company.
Exception: In Index funds, ETFs, and Sectoral/Thematic funds, the single issuer limit can go up to the weight of the company in the benchmark index or 12% of NAV (whichever is higher).

3. Single Issuer Debt Limits

A mutual fund scheme shall not invest more than 10% of its NAV in debt instruments issued by a single corporate issuer (investment grade rated). Extendable to 12% of NAV with prior approval of Trustees.
Exemption: Government Securities (G-Secs), Treasury Bills, and collateralized repos in G-Secs/TREPS are exempt from single issuer limits.

4. Group & Sector Exposure Limits

• Group Exposure: Debt schemes cannot invest > 20% of NAV in debt of a single corporate group (extendable to 25% with Trustee approval).
• Sector Exposure: Total debt exposure to a single sector cannot exceed 20% of NAV (additional 10% allowed for HFCs and 5% for retail NBFCs).

5. Fund of Funds (FoF) Restrictions

• A regular mutual fund scheme CANNOT invest in any Fund of Funds scheme.
• A Fund of Funds scheme CANNOT invest in any other Fund of Funds scheme (no daisy-chaining).

6. Inter-Scheme Transfers (IST)

Transfers of securities between schemes of the same mutual fund are permitted ONLY at prevailing market price (spot basis) and strictly if the security matches the investment objective of the transferee scheme, after the transferring scheme has explored other market avenues.

Unclaimed Dividend & Redemption Norms:

Unclaimed amounts must be deployed in call money, liquid, or overnight funds.
• Claimed within 3 years: Investor receives payment based on prevailing NAV (includes accrued interest/returns).
• Claimed AFTER 3 years: Investor receives payment based on the NAV at the end of the 3rd year! Accrued income beyond 3 years transfers to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF).

Mandatory TRI Benchmarking (Feb 1, 2018):

All mutual fund schemes must benchmark performance against Total Return Indices (TRI) rather than Price Return Indices (PRI). TRI captures capital gains plus cash dividends and interest distributions, eliminating misleading alpha claims.

Advertisement Code

SEBI Advertisement Code (Fifth Schedule)

The Fifth Schedule regulates all mutual fund promotional materials (print, TV, radio, hoardings, SMS, websites, and digital social media) to guarantee objective, truthful, and non-misleading communication.

Strict Ban on Celebrity Endorsements

• NO Celebrities (actors, sports stars, entertainers, or financial influencers) are allowed to endorse individual mutual fund schemes or individual AMCs.

• Single Exception: SEBI permits celebrity endorsement exclusively at the industry level through AMFI for general investor awareness campaigns (e.g., "Mutual Funds Sahi Hai") with prior approval of SEBI.

Mandatory Standard Warning Rule

"Mutual Fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully."

• Exactly 14 words — no words may be added, omitted, or altered.
• In audio-visual media (TV, YouTube, digital video): Both the visual disclaimer and the voiceover must run for at least 5 seconds in clear, audible tempo.

Standard Performance Disclosures in Advertisements:
< 6 Months Existence Past performance CANNOT be advertised under any circumstances!
6 Months to 1 Year Existence Show simple annualized returns for the past 6-month period.
1 Year to 3 Years Existence Show CAGR returns for 1 Year and Since Inception alongside the benchmark.
≥ 3 Years Existence Show CAGR for 1Y, 3Y, 5Y, and Since Inception + point-to-point rupee growth on a standard investment of ₹10,000.

• Liquid & Overnight Funds: May show simple annualized returns for 7 days, 15 days, and 30 days.
• Fund Manager Track Record: Must disclose performance of ALL other schemes managed by the same fund manager.

Insider Trading & Alignment

Prohibition of Insider Trading (PIT) & Skin in the Game

SEBI extended the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 to mutual funds, establishing rigorous curbs on personal transactions by fund employees and mandating alignment of interest.

PIT Framework for Mutual Funds

• Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI): Advance knowledge of major portfolio changes, bulk purchases/sales, valuation write-downs, impending credit downgrades, or liquidity distress in scheme assets.

• Designated Persons: Trustees, AMC Directors, CEO, CIO, Fund Managers, Research Analysts, Dealers, Compliance Officers, and their immediate relatives.

• Trading Window & Pre-Clearance: Mandatory pre-clearance of personal trades. Trading windows are closed during sensitive periods to prevent front-running, self-dealing, or exploiting advance portfolio knowledge.

• Minimum Holding Period: Mandatory holding period (e.g., 30 days or 6 months) to deter short-term speculative trading in mutual fund units.

"Skin in the Game" (Alignment of Interest)

To align fund manager interests directly with unitholders, SEBI mandates that Key Employees (Fund Managers, Chief Investment Officers, Research Heads) must receive a minimum percentage (typically 20% of net annual CTC / salary / variable pay) in the form of units of the mutual fund schemes they manage.

Statutory Lock-in Period:

These units are locked in for a minimum period of 3 years (or the tenure of the scheme, whichever is less). Units cannot be redeemed or pledged during the lock-in period, ensuring complete fidelity to scheme performance.

Investor Rights & AMFI Code

Investor Rights, SCORES 2.0 & AMFI Code of Conduct (AGNI)

Statutory protections provided to mutual fund unitholders alongside AMFI's disciplinary mechanism against errant distributors.

Up to 10 Nominees

Unitholders can register up to 10 nominees per folio with specified percentage shares (split equally if unspecified).

Fundamental Changes

Written notice + ad + mandatory 30-day exit window to exit at prevailing NAV without any exit load.

75% Unitholders' Power

75% of unitholders can pass a resolution to terminate the AMC or wind up any mutual fund scheme.

Statutory Operational Timelines:

• IDCW (Dividend) Payout: Must be dispatched within 7 working days from the record date.
• Redemption Proceeds: Must be dispatched within 3 working days (T+1 for Liquid funds).
• Annual Report: Must be emailed or abridged summary sent within 4 months from closure of financial year.

Grievance Redressal (SCORES 2.0 & SMART ODR):

Investors lodge unresolved complaints on the web-based SEBI Complaints Redress System (SCORES 2.0).
• The AMC is mandated to resolve complaints within 21 calendar days of receipt.
• Unresolved matters escalate automatically to designated supervisory bodies or the SMART ODR (Online Dispute Resolution) portal for conciliation and online arbitration.

AMFI Code of Conduct Disciplinary Action (AGNI):

Upon receipt of a complaint against an intermediary for violating the AMFI Code of Conduct, AMFI issues a show-cause notice granting 3 weeks to submit a written explanation.

First Minor Offense: Warning letter issued with instructions to rectify conduct.
Proven Second Violation: Immediate cancellation of AMFI Registration (ARN) and written intimation to all AMCs to debar the distributor!
