Core Formula & Rules

Computation of Net Asset Value (NAV) & Operational Mandates

The Net Asset Value (NAV) represents the real-time or end-of-day market worth of a single unit of a mutual fund scheme. It reflects the liquidation value of the portfolio after discharging all liabilities, operational expenses, and accrued charges.

Standard NAV Formula (SEBI Format) Per-Unit Metric
NAV = [Market Value of Investments + Receivables + Accrued Income + Other Assets] - [Current Liabilities + Accrued Expenses + Payables]
               Number of Units Outstanding as on Valuation Date

Key Nuance: Expenses and income are accrued daily. NAV is calculated after incorporating the day's accrued investment management fees, trustee fees, custodian charges, and other permissible operating expenses.

Practical Numerical Walkthrough: Daily NAV Calculation

Gross Portfolio Assets
  • • Market Value of Listed Equities: ₹850.00 Cr
  • • Market Value of Debt Securities: ₹320.00 Cr
  • • Accrued Dividends & Interest: ₹8.50 Cr
  • • Pending Trades Receivable: ₹12.50 Cr
  • • Cash & Bank Balances: ₹25.00 Cr
  • Total Assets (A): ₹1,216.00 Cr
Scheme Liabilities & Accruals
  • • Accrued AMC & Operating Expenses: ₹2.40 Cr
  • • Pending Redemptions Payable: ₹6.60 Cr
  • • Securities Purchase Payables: ₹7.00 Cr
  • Total Liabilities (B): ₹16.00 Cr
  • Net Assets (A - B): ₹1,200.00 Cr
  • Outstanding Units: 40.00 Cr units
NAV = ₹1,200.00 Crores / 40.00 Crore Units = ₹30.0000 per unit
Equity & Hybrid Rounding

Equity-oriented, hybrid, and index schemes must compute and publish NAV rounded off to at least 2 decimal places (e.g., ₹45.82).

Debt & Liquid Rounding

Liquid, overnight, money market, and debt schemes must round off NAV to at least 4 decimal places (e.g., ₹1,048.2754) due to high transaction sizes and small daily yield shifts.

365 Days Declaration

Liquid and Overnight schemes declare NAV for all 365 days of the year (including Sundays, bank holidays, and national holidays) as income accrues over weekends.

Regulatory Timelines for Daily NAV Disclosure & Penalties

Standard Cut-off: 11:00 PM All open-ended mutual fund schemes must calculate and publish their daily NAV on the AMFI website and the AMC portal by 11:00 PM on every business day.
FoFs & Overseas Funds: 10:00 AM Next Day Fund of Funds (FoFs) and schemes investing in overseas securities are permitted an extended deadline up to 10:00 AM on the next business day due to international exchange time zones.

⚠️ Regulatory Consequence: If an AMC repeatedly fails to upload NAV by the mandated deadline without valid technical justification, AMFI issues operational warnings, and SEBI may bar the AMC from launching new fund offers (NFOs) until full compliance is restored.

Seventh Schedule Mandate

Seventh Schedule: 10 Overarching Valuation Principles & Debt Waterfall Hierarchy

Under the Seventh Schedule of SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996, the overarching objective is Fair Valuation: securities must be valued at their true realizable value in an arm's length transaction so that no investor class (existing, subscribing, or redeeming) is unfairly enriched or diluted at the expense of others.

The 10 Overarching Valuation Principles

1. Fair Valuation Principle:

Assets must be valued fairly so that incoming, continuing, and exiting investors are treated equitably without inter-generational wealth transfer.

2. Realisable Value Basis:

Valuation must reflect the price that an asset would reasonably fetch in an orderly, arm's length commercial transaction between knowledgeable parties.

3. Board-Approved Valuation Policy:

Every AMC must institute a detailed, written Valuation Policy approved by the Boards of both the AMC and the Board of Trustees.

4. Independent Periodic Review & Audit:

The Valuation Policy and its operational execution must be reviewed at least annually by an independent statutory/internal auditor and the Trustees.

5. Independent Valuation Agencies (CRISIL & ICRA):

Debt, money market, and illiquid instruments must be valued using matrix/pricing inputs provided by AMFI-appointed independent rating agencies (CRISIL & ICRA).

6. AMC Valuation Committee:

A high-level AMC Valuation Committee (including CIO, CRO, Compliance Officer, and CFO) must actively oversee pricing, exceptions, and illiquid assets.

7. Escalation & Documented Deviations:

Any deviation from agency-recommended valuations must be documented with comprehensive economic rationale and reported to the Trustees and SEBI.

8. Temporal & Scheme Consistency:

Valuation methodologies cannot be changed opportunistically. Policies must be consistently applied across time and across all schemes managed by the AMC.

9. Conflict of Interest Mitigation:

Rigorous operational firewalls (Chinese Walls) must isolate fund managers and dealmakers from the independent valuation and fund accounting teams.

10. Public Transparency & Disclosures:

The full Valuation Policy, detailed methodologies, and any exceptional deviations must be published prominently on the AMC's website and in periodic reports.

Asset-Specific Valuation Rules & Debt Waterfall Hierarchy

1. Traded & Non-Traded Equities

• Traded Equities: Valued at the closing price on the Principal Stock Exchange (where the stock had the highest trading volume on that valuation day). If untraded on the principal exchange, the closing price on the other recognized stock exchange is used.
• Non-Traded / Illiquid Equities: A share is deemed illiquid if monthly traded volume is < 50,000 shares AND monthly trading value is < ₹5 Lakhs. Valued on the basis of SEBI's formula combining Net Worth per share (Book Value) and Capitalized Earning Value with prescribed illiquidity discounts.

2. Debt & Money Market Instruments (100% Mark-to-Market)

• Phasing Out of Amortization: Historical amortization (< 30 days residual maturity) has been completely abolished by SEBI. 100% of debt and money market instruments across all maturities must be Marked-to-Market (MTM) daily.
• Independent Agencies: Prices/matrices are provided daily by CRISIL and ICRA. The simple average of prices from both agencies is taken for valuation.

The Three-Tier Debt Waterfall Valuation Mechanism
Tier 1: Traded VWAP Volume Weighted Average Price of trades reported on F-TRAC / CBRICS / BSE / NSE meeting qualifying threshold (min ₹5 Cr for G-Sec/SDL; min ₹5 Cr & 2 trades for corporate bonds).
Tier 2: Comparable Trades If un-traded, evaluate recent secondary market trades of securities issued by the same issuer, or peer issuers with identical credit rating, sector, and maturity band.
Tier 3: Agency Matrix Valued using the daily yield matrix developed by CRISIL and ICRA based on benchmark G-Sec yield curves + issuer credit risk and liquidity spreads.

Regulation 52 Limits

Total Expense Ratio (TER) Limits & Slabs (SEBI Regulation 52)

The Total Expense Ratio (TER) represents the annual operating expenses of a mutual fund scheme expressed as a percentage of daily net assets (AUM). SEBI enforces tiered slabs: as the scheme's AUM expands, the permitted expense ratio declines to pass economies of scale directly to investors.

AUM Slabs (Daily Net Assets) Equity-Oriented Schemes Other Schemes (Debt / Income) Close-Ended Schemes
First ₹500 Crores 2.25% 2.00% Equity: Max 1.25%
Debt: Max 1.00%
Next ₹250 Crores 2.00% 1.75%
Next ₹1,250 Crores 1.75% 1.50%
Next ₹3,000 Crores 1.60% 1.35%
Next ₹5,000 Crores 1.50% 1.25%
Next ₹40,000 Crores Reduction of 0.05% for every increase of ₹5,000 Crores of daily net assets or part thereof
On the Balance AUM 1.05% 0.80%
Index Funds & ETFs

The maximum permissible TER for Index Schemes and Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) cannot exceed 1.00% of daily net assets.

Fund of Funds (FoF)

Total expenses (underlying schemes + FoF management fee) cannot exceed the maximum open-ended category cap. FoF management fee itself is capped at 1.00%.

Daily Accrual Formula

Daily Expense = Daily AUM × (Annual TER / 365)
Deducted daily from gross asset value before computing NAV.

Direct Plan vs. Regular Plan: The Strict TER Differential Mandate

Direct Plans and Regular Plans share the exact same underlying investment portfolio. However, SEBI strictly mandates that no distribution expenses or commissions can be charged to Direct Plans.

Mandatory Rule: TER of Direct Plan = TER of Regular Plan − Distribution Expenses / Commission.
Example: If Regular Plan TER is 1.95% and distributor commission is 0.75%, the Direct Plan TER must be exactly 1.20%. Because less expense is debited daily, the Direct Plan NAV compounds higher over time.

Permissible Additions

Permissible Additional Expenses over Base TER & GST Accounting

SEBI permits AMCs to debit specific additional expenses over and above the base TER under strict regulatory ceilings. Understanding the boundaries between scheme-chargeable costs and AMC-absorbed costs is a major exam focus.

1. Brokerage and Transaction Costs on Trades

Capped by SEBI

Brokerage and transaction costs incurred for executing secondary market transactions may be charged to the scheme over and above the base TER up to:

Cash Market Transactions: 0.12% (12 bps)

Maximum chargeable to the scheme on cash trades.

Derivatives Transactions: 0.05% (5 bps)

Maximum chargeable to the scheme on F&O trades.

⚠️ Critical Exam Rule: Any brokerage or transaction execution fees charged by brokers exceeding 0.12% (cash) or 0.05% (derivatives) CANNOT be debited to the scheme. The excess amount MUST be borne directly by the AMC out of its own profits!

2. Additional 5 bps (Exit Load Schemes)

Under Regulation 52(6A)(c), schemes that levy an exit load are permitted to charge an additional expense of up to 0.05% (5 bps) of daily net assets, since 100% of collected exit load is credited back to the scheme.

3. Additional 30 bps (B-30 Retail Inflows)

Under Regulation 52(6A)(b), AMCs could charge up to 30 bps (0.30%) if B-30 retail inflows are ≥ 30% of gross inflows or ≥ 15% of average AUM (YTD). Subject to 1-year clawback. (Note: Currently kept in abeyance by SEBI pending revised distributor framework).

4. Goods & Services Tax (GST) Accounting & Investor Education

GST on Management Fees

Can be charged to the scheme in addition to the maximum limit of TER (over and above the base TER ceiling).

GST on Other Operating Expenses

GST on registrar, audit, custodian, and distributor fees must be accommodated strictly within the base TER limit.

Investor Education Allocation

AMCs must set aside at least 2 bps (0.02% p.a.) within TER for investor awareness (1 bp retained by AMC; 1 bp remitted to AMFI).

Pricing & Loads

Unit Pricing, Stamp Duty & Exit Load Accounting

Mutual fund units are bought and sold based on transparent mathematical rules governed by SEBI. Entry loads are obsolete, stamp duty is deducted upfront, and exit loads protect continuing unitholders.

1. Sale Price (Subscription / Purchase)

The price an investor pays to acquire one unit from the fund house. Since SEBI abolished entry loads in August 2009:

Sale Price = Applicable NAV
Stamp Duty on Allotment (w.e.f. July 1, 2020):

A uniform stamp duty of 0.005% (5 paise per ₹1,000 or ₹5 per ₹1 Lakh) is deducted upfront from the gross purchase amount before unit allotment.

2. Repurchase Price (Redemption Price)

The price per unit an investor receives from the fund house upon redeeming or liquidating units:

Repurchase Price = Applicable NAV × (1 − Exit Load %)
SEBI Boundary Constraint:

The repurchase price shall not be lower than 95% of NAV. (The difference between sale price and repurchase price cannot exceed 5% of sale price).

Numerical Example: Purchase with Stamp Duty & Redemption with Exit Load

Purchase Scenario
  • • Gross Investment: ₹1,00,000.00
  • • Stamp Duty (0.005%): − ₹5.00
  • • Net Investable Amount: ₹99,995.00
  • • Applicable NAV: ₹50.0000
  • Units Allotted = ₹99,995 / 50 = 1,999.900 units
Redemption Scenario
  • • Units Redeemed: 1,000 units
  • • Applicable NAV: ₹60.0000
  • • Exit Load Applicable: 1.00%
  • • Repurchase Price: ₹60 × (1 − 0.01) = ₹59.4000
  • Gross Payout = 1,000 × ₹59.40 = ₹59,400.00
100% Exit Load Credit Mandate: In the redemption above, the total exit load collected is ₹600 (1,000 units × ₹0.60). 100% of this exit load (net of GST) must be credited directly back to the scheme on the same business day to preserve the wealth of continuing unitholders.

Specialized Instruments

AT-1 Basel III Perpetual Bonds & Segregated Portfolio Valuation Rules

Specialized fixed income valuation rules are heavily tested in NISM Series V-D. Basel III Additional Tier-1 (AT-1) bonds carry equity-like loss absorption risks, while Segregated Portfolios (side-pocketing) insulate performing assets from distressed defaults.

Basel III AT-1 Perpetual Bonds 100-Year Rule

AT-1 bonds are perpetual debt instruments issued by banks to meet regulatory Tier-1 capital requirements. They carry loss-absorption clauses (coupon discretionary deferral, principal write-down, or conversion to equity upon Point of Non-Viability).

SEBI Valuation & Exposure Norms:
  • 100-Year Maturity Convention: Must be valued to yield assuming a deemed 100-year maturity from the issuance date (phased transition ended March 31, 2023).
  • Portfolio Cap: A mutual fund scheme cannot invest more than 10% of debt AUM in perpetual debt instruments.
  • Single Issuer Cap: Maximum 5% of debt AUM in perpetual bonds of a single banking issuer.
Segregated Portfolio (Side-Pocketing) 0% TER Cap

When a credit event occurs (downgrade to below investment grade < BBB- or default), the distressed asset is carved out into a Segregated Portfolio to prevent panic redemptions from hurting ongoing investors.

Key Regulatory Requirements:
  • 1:1 Unit Allotment: All existing unitholders receive units in the segregated portfolio in exact proportion to their holding on the credit event date.
  • Mandatory Exchange Listing: Segregated portfolio is closed to subscriptions/redemptions and must be listed on a recognized stock exchange within 10 working days for liquidity.
  • Zero Management Fee (0% TER): AMC cannot charge any management or operating TER. Only actual legal/recovery costs can be allocated up to a maximum cap of 9 bps (0.09%) upon actual realization.
