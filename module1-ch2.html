Section 2.1 • Quick Refresher
Core Concept

Concept of a Mutual Fund & Its Economic Role

Key Distributor Principle: Investing "Through", Not "In"

Technically, one does not invest in a mutual fund, but invests through a mutual fund. A mutual fund is not an asset class that competes with shares or bonds; it is a pass-through vehicle (constituted as a Trust) that pools money from investors to purchase an underlying portfolio of equities, debt, gold, or money market instruments.

1. Pass-Through Vehicle

All profits, capital gains, interest, dividends, or losses belong solely to the unitholders. No other entity (Sponsor, Trustee, AMC) participates in the profits or losses; they only earn regulatory-capped fees.

2. Capital Formation

Mobilizes retail household savings into corporate equity and debt financing, funding national infrastructure, generating employment, and boosting tax revenues.

3. Market Stabilizer ⭐

Acts as a domestic counterforce to volatile Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI) flows. In 2022 and 2025, domestic SIP inflows absorbed massive FPI selling, providing market resilience.

Investment Objective vs. Investment Policy

Every scheme has a pre-announced Investment Objective stated in the Scheme Information Document (SID) addressing safety, liquidity, and return expectations. To achieve this, the AMC formulates an Investment Policy which specifies two critical aspects:

1. Asset Allocation Permissible percentage allocation across equity, debt, money market, and gold/commodities.
2. Investment Style & Concentration Growth style vs. Value/Contra style; Top-down vs. Bottom-up; Focused (concentrated) vs. Well-diversified.

Key Metrics

Core Concepts & Valuation Terminology

Units & Face Value

An investor's capital is divided into fractional units. Standard face value is typically ₹10 per unit (can also be ₹100 or ₹1,000 for liquid/overnight funds). Used primarily for accounting.

Unit Capital = Units Issued × Face Value
Net Asset Value (NAV) ⭐

The true intrinsic worth per unit. Also represents the net realizable liquidation value per unit if all assets are sold and liabilities paid. Deducts recurring expenses daily.

NAV = Net Assets ÷ Outstanding Units
Assets Under Management (AUM)

Total market value of all investments held by the scheme (NAV × Outstanding Units). Rises with market appreciation & net inflows; drops on market fall, redemptions & IDCW.

Mark to Market (MTM)

The daily process of valuing every security in the scheme's portfolio at current fair market price. Daily MTM drives daily NAV fluctuations.

Recurring Expenses (TER)

Annual management fees, custodian, registrar, and audit charges deducted daily from scheme assets as a percentage of AUM. Higher expenses lower the NAV.

IDCW Option

Formerly called "Dividend Option". Renamed by SEBI to Income Distribution Cum Capital Withdrawal to clarify that payments are capital withdrawals, not corporate dividends.

Standard NAV Formula:

$$ ext{NAV} = rac{ ext{Market Value of Investments} + ext{Receivables \& Accrued Income} + ext{Other Assets} - ( ext{Accrued Expenses} + ext{Other Liabilities})}{ ext{Number of Units Outstanding at Valuation Date}}$$

Pros vs Cons

Advantages & Limitations of Mutual Funds

Core Advantages for Investors

1. Professional Management Experienced fund managers, dedicated research analysts, corporate actions handling, and daily portfolio accounting.
2. Affordable Diversification Achieve proportionate ownership in 40-60 diversified securities even with an investment of just ₹500.
3. Economies of Scale ⭐ Large pooled corpus spreads fixed research and office costs, and enables negotiating lower brokerage and transaction fees.
4. High Transparency Three key sources: Scheme documents (SID, SAI, KIM), monthly factsheets (complete portfolio disclosures), and daily NAV.
5. Tax Deferral & Section 80C Growth plans compound tax-free until withdrawal. ELSS allows up to ₹1,50,000 tax deduction under Sec 80C (Old Regime).
6. Systematic Discipline Automated SIP (regular investing), SWP (regular cash flow), and STP (staggered allocation across funds).

Structural Limitations of Mutual Funds

No Customization ⭐ Unlike PMS (Portfolio Management Services) where wealthy investors tailor portfolios, a mutual fund unitholder cannot choose or exclude specific stocks.
Choice Overload Thousands of scheme options make selection complex (mitigated by SEBI categorization & distributor guidance).
No Control on Costs Operating and management expenses are deducted uniformly across all unitholders proportional to their unit holdings.
No Guaranteed Return Mutual funds are pass-through vehicles without guaranteed yields. Returns depend on underlying market movements and manager skills.
Section 2.4 • Structural Classification
Scheme Structures

Open-Ended, Close-Ended, Interval Funds & ETFs

Feature Open-Ended Funds Close-Ended Funds Interval Funds
Maturity Period Perpetual (No fixed maturity) Fixed maturity (Ceases on maturity date) Largely close-ended with periodic open windows
Post-NFO Transactions Directly with AMC on all business days at NAV NO transaction with AMC; only counter-party trades on stock exchange Direct with AMC during "Transaction Periods"; exchange-listed otherwise
Unit Capital Fluctuating (Expands on inflows, shrinks on redemptions) Fixed & Stable (Trades between investors on exchange) Variable during transaction periods, fixed in interval periods
Stock Exchange Listing Not mandatory (optional) Compulsory listing on recognized exchange (except ELSS) Compulsory listing on recognized exchange
Price vs. NAV Transacts exactly at current NAV (adjusted for exit load) Market price usually trades at a discount to NAV (buyer bargaining power) NAV during transaction windows; market price discount on exchange

Interval Fund Specific Rules ⭐

• Transaction Period: Window when units can be bought/redeemed with the fund. Minimum duration = 2 working days.
• Interval Period: Period between transaction windows. Minimum duration = 15 calendar days.
• No redemption allowed outside the transaction period.
• Note: Close-ended and interval SIF strategies must also be compulsorily listed on stock exchanges.

Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)

Passive schemes traded intraday on stock exchanges. While open-ended funds transact at a single end-of-day NAV, ETFs allow continuous real-time execution throughout the trading session. Authorised Participants (APs) create and redeem units in creation units to keep market prices tightly aligned with real-time NAV.

⭐ 2026 Regulatory Update

Active vs. Passive Funds & The SEBI "MF Lite" Framework

Actively Managed Funds

Fund managers exercise full analytical discretion to select, overweight, or underweight stocks and bonds to beat a benchmark index (generate Alpha). Carries higher research and operational expenses (higher TER).

Passively Managed Funds (Index/ETFs)

Replicate a chosen underlying benchmark (e.g., Nifty 50 or Sensex) in identical proportion and weightage. Objective is to mirror index returns with minimum tracking error, not to beat the market. Very low expense ratios.

NEW

Mutual Fund Lite ("MF Lite") Framework (Chapter X, SEBI MF Regulations 2026) ⭐

Effective December 31, 2024, SEBI introduced the Mutual Fund Lite (MF Lite) regulatory framework to drive low-cost passive investing and deepen retail market penetration in India:

1. Exclusive Scope An "MF Lite" can launch only passive schemes: Index Funds, Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs), Fund of Funds (FoFs) investing in passive schemes.
2. Relaxed Compliance Significantly lower net-worth eligibility criteria and streamlined operational and reporting requirements for sponsoring AMCs.
3. Investor Benefit Eliminates costly overheads of active fund research desks, leading to rock-bottom TERs and wider distribution through fintech channels.

SEBI Categorization: Equity Schemes (13 Sub-Categories) & Market Caps

SEBI issued standardization circulars to ensure "one scheme per category" and establish uniform, transparent investment parameters across all AMCs.

SEBI Market Capitalization Tiers (Box 2.2) ⭐

All AMCs must strictly adhere to the unified AMFI list of listed companies ranked by full market capitalization (updated half-yearly):

Large Cap Companies 1st to 100th company in full market cap
Mid Cap Companies 101st to 250th company in full market cap
Small Cap Companies 251st company onwards in full market cap
# Scheme Category Minimum Investment Mandate Key Exam Rules & Nuances
1 Multi Cap Fund ⭐ Min 75% in equity: 25% Large + 25% Mid + 25% Small Cap Mandatory equal 25% allocation floors across all three cap segments. Residual 25% unconstrained.
2 Large Cap Fund Min 80% of total assets in Large Cap stocks Focuses on Top 100 established market leaders.
3 Large & Mid Cap Fund Min 35% Large Cap AND min 35% Mid Cap Blends stability of large caps with high growth of mid caps. Total equity min 70%.
4 Mid Cap Fund Min 65% in Mid Cap stocks (101st-250th) Higher return potential than large caps with higher volatility.
5 Small Cap Fund Min 65% in Small Cap stocks (251st onwards) Highest liquidity risk and volatility among market cap funds.
6 Dividend Yield Fund Min 80% in dividend-yielding stocks Invests in cash-rich companies with consistent dividend histories.
7 Value Fund ⭐ Min 80% in equity (Value investment strategy) AMC can offer both Value and Contra funds ONLY if scheme portfolio overlap is ≤ 50%.
8 Contra Fund ⭐ Min 80% in equity (Contrarian strategy)
9 Focused Fund Min 80% in equity; Maximum 30 stocks Must declare focus universe in SID (e.g. focused large-cap, focused multi-cap).
10 Sectoral Fund ⭐ Min 80% in a specific sector (e.g. Banking, Pharma) No more than 50% overlap with other sectoral/thematic & equity schemes (except large cap).
11 Thematic Fund ⭐ Min 80% in a specific theme (e.g. Infrastructure, ESG)
12 ELSS – Tax Saver Fund Min 80% in equity & equity related instruments Statutory lock-in of 3 years. Section 80C tax deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakhs (Old Tax Regime).
13 Flexi Cap Fund ⭐ Min 65% in equity & equity related instruments Dynamic allocation across large, mid, and small cap without any mandatory minimum floor (unlike Multi Cap).
ESG Sub-Strategies under Thematic: AMCs can launch ESG funds with one of 6 defined strategies: (a) Exclusion, (b) Integration, (c) Best-in-class & Positive Screening, (d) Impact investing, (e) Sustainable objectives, (f) Transition or transition-related investments.

⭐ 7 Hybrid Schemes & Glide Path

Hybrid Schemes (7 Categories) & Life Cycle Funds

Hybrid funds combine growth (equity) with stability (debt). AMCs can offer only one type per hybrid category.

# Hybrid Category Mandated Asset Allocation Key Exam Nuance
1 Conservative Hybrid Fund 75% - 90% Debt + 10% - 25% Equity Primary focus on capital preservation with slight equity kicker.
2 Balanced Hybrid Fund ⭐ 40% - 60% Equity + 40% - 60% Debt NO Arbitrage is permitted in Balanced Hybrid. AMC cannot offer both Balanced and Aggressive Hybrid (must choose one).
3 Aggressive Hybrid Fund 65% - 80% Equity + 20% - 35% Debt Equity-oriented for taxation purposes (qualifies as equity fund).
4 Dynamic Asset Allocation (BAF) 0% - 100% Equity / 0% - 100% Debt Commonly known as Balanced Advantage Fund. Uses in-house valuation models (P/E, P/B, yield gap) to dynamically swing allocation.
5 Multi Asset Allocation Fund ⭐ At least 3 asset classes with min 10% in each Foreign securities are NOT treated as a separate asset class for meeting this 10% threshold!
6 Arbitrage Fund ⭐ Min 65% in equity/equity arbitrage Simultaneous cash-futures arbitrage. Debt limited strictly to G-Secs < 1 yr and repo of government bonds. Low volatility.
7 Equity Savings Fund Min 65% Equity (gross) + min 10% Debt; Net unhedged equity: 15% - 40% Combines outright directional equity, arbitrage, and fixed income debt. Hedged/unhedged split must be stated in SID.

Category D: Life Cycle Funds (Glide Path Strategy) ⭐

Exam Essential

Designed to support goal-based investing (such as retirement or higher education) through a pre-determined glide path strategy. As the scheme approaches maturity, the asset allocation dynamically and automatically decreases equity exposure and increases debt exposure to lock in gains and shield against market downturns.

Eligible Asset Classes Equity, Debt, InvITs, Exchange Traded Commodity Derivatives (ETCDs), Gold & Silver ETFs.
Tenure Limits Minimum tenure of 5 years, maximum tenure of 30 years; launched only in multiples of 5 years.
AMC Scheme Cap A maximum of 6 Life Cycle Funds can be active for subscription by an AMC at any given point in time.
Section 2.9 • Specialized Vehicles
REITs, InvITs & Debt

Other Schemes, Real Estate & Infrastructure Vehicles

Category E: Other Schemes

• Index Funds / ETFs: Minimum 95% in securities of the replicated/tracked index.
• Fund of Funds (Domestic/Overseas): Minimum 95% in the underlying fund.
• Single Scheme Exception: AMCs are restricted to 1 scheme per category, except: (1) Index/ETFs tracking different indices, (2) FoFs investing in different underlying schemes, (3) Sectoral/Thematic funds with different sectors/themes.

FMPs, TMFs & Capital Protection Funds

• Fixed Maturity Plans (FMPs): Closed-end debt schemes whose portfolio maturity strictly matches scheme tenure (passive buy-and-hold).
• Target Maturity Funds (TMFs): Open-ended passive debt funds with a fixed maturity date (2-14 yrs), investing in G-Secs/SDLs to eliminate reinvestment risk.
• Capital Protection Oriented Funds: Closed-end hybrids where principal is invested in debt to compound to 100% at maturity; remainder in equity derivatives.

Section 2.10 • Super High Exam Yield
⭐⭐ NEW PRODUCT LINE: SIF

SEBI Specialized Investment Fund (SIF) Product Line

To bridge the product and ticket size gap between standard Mutual Funds (₹500 min, highly restricted shorting) and PMS (₹50 Lakhs min) / AIFs (₹1 Crore min), SEBI amended the Mutual Funds Regulations to establish the Specialized Investment Fund (SIF).

Crucial NISM Series V-D Mandate

Minimum Investment Ticket Size: ₹10 Lakhs

The investor must commit a minimum of ₹10 Lakhs across all investment strategies of the same AMC.

Min ₹10,00,000

The 7 Permitted SIF Investment Strategies

AMCs can offer only ONE investment strategy per SIF category to avoid strategy proliferation:

A. Equity Oriented Investment Strategies
1. Equity Long-Short Fund

• Min 80% in equity & equity related instruments.
• Max short exposure via unhedged derivatives: 25% of NAV.
• Structure: Open-ended or Interval.
• Redemptions: Daily or lesser frequency.

2. Equity Ex-Top 100 Long-Short

• Min 65% in stocks excluding the top 100 by market cap (Mid & Small caps).
• Max short exposure: 25% of NAV in other than large-cap stocks.
• Structure: Open-ended or Interval.
• Redemptions: Daily or lesser frequency.

3. Sector Rotation Long-Short

• Operates at the sector level: if shorting a sector, all stocks from that sector must be held short.
• Min 80% in equity; Maximum 4 sectors.
• Max short exposure: 25% of NAV.
• Redemptions: Daily or lesser frequency.

B. Debt Oriented Investment Strategies
1. Debt Long-Short Fund

• Interval fund investing across duration.
• Unhedged short exposure via exchange-traded debt derivatives.
• Redemptions: Once a week or lesser frequency.

2. Sectoral Debt Long-Short Fund

• Interval fund investing in debt of minimum 2 sectors.
• Max exposure to a single sector: 75%.
• Short positions must cover all instruments of that sector; Max short: 25% of NAV.
• Redemptions: Once a week or lesser frequency.

C. Hybrid Investment Strategies
1. Active Asset Allocator Long-Short Fund

• Interval fund dynamically investing across equity, debt, derivatives, commodity derivatives, REITs, and InvITs.
• Max short positions in equity/debt derivatives: 25% of NAV.
• Redemptions: Two times a week or lesser frequency.

2. Hybrid Long-Short Fund

• Interval fund investing in equity, debt, and their derivatives.
• Mandatory minimum: 25% Equity and 25% Debt.
• Max unhedged short exposure in equity & debt: 25% of net assets.
• Redemptions: Two times a week or lesser frequency.

Exam Note on SIF Redemption Frequencies: Equity SIF strategies allow daily or lesser redemption; Debt SIF strategies allow once a week or lesser; Hybrid SIF strategies allow twice a week or lesser. All close-ended/interval SIF schemes must be compulsorily listed on stock exchanges.
