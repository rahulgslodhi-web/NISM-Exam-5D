Section 7.1

Basic Tax Concepts, Assessment Year & Previous Year

The Income Tax Act, 1961 is the primary statute governing direct taxation in India. Income earned in a given financial year is taxed in the immediately following assessment year. Understanding key statutory definitions is essential:

Previous Year (PY) vs Assessment Year (AY)

Previous Year (PY): The 12-month financial year (1 April to 31 March) in which income is earned.
Assessment Year (AY): The consecutive period of 12 months immediately following the previous year in which that income is assessed and taxed (e.g., for PY 2024-25, AY is 2025-26).

Person & Assessee (Sec 2(7) & 2(31))

Person: Includes Individual, HUF, Company, Firm/LLP, AOP, BOI, Local Authority, and Artificial Juridical Person.
Assessee: Any person by whom any tax, penalty, or interest is payable under the Income Tax Act.

GTI vs Total Income: Gross Total Income (GTI) is the aggregate of income computed under the 5 heads before Chapter VI-A deductions. Total Income (Taxable Income) is GTI minus eligible deductions under Chapter VI-A.

Residential Status Determination (Section 6)

Tax incidence in India is strictly determined by Residential Status in the previous year, NOT by citizenship or domicile. An Indian citizen can be a Non-Resident, while a foreign citizen can be a Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR).

Step 1: Resident vs Non-Resident (Basic Conditions - Sec 6(1))

An individual is considered a Resident in India if they satisfy EITHER of the following two basic conditions:

Condition A: 182 Days Test Presence in India for a period of 182 days or more during the relevant Previous Year.
Condition B: 60 Days + 365 Days Test Presence in India for 60 days or more in the relevant PY AND 365 days or more across the 4 preceding PYs.
Critical Exceptions where Condition B (60 days) is Modified:
  • Leaving India for Employment / Crew Member: For an Indian citizen leaving India during the PY for employment abroad or as a crew member of an Indian ship, the 60-day threshold is replaced by 182 days (Condition B does not apply at 60 days).
  • Indian Citizen / PIO Visiting India (Income ≤ ₹15 Lakhs): If total income (excluding foreign sources) is up to ₹15 Lakhs, 60 days is replaced by 182 days.
  • Indian Citizen / PIO Visiting India (Income > ₹15 Lakhs) ⭐: If Indian taxable income exceeds ₹15 Lakhs, the 60-day threshold is substituted with 120 days (i.e., ≥120 days in PY + ≥365 days in 4 preceding years makes them a Resident, specifically RNOR).
  • Deemed Resident (Section 6(1A)) ⭐: An Indian citizen having total income (other than foreign sources) exceeding ₹15 Lakhs who is not liable to tax in any other country by reason of domicile/residence is deemed to be a resident (and automatically classified as RNOR).

Step 2: ROR vs RNOR (Additional Conditions - Sec 6(6))

A Resident is classified as Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR) only if they satisfy BOTH of the following additional conditions:

1. Resident in India in at least 2 out of the 10 previous years preceding the relevant PY.
2. Physical presence in India for a total of 730 days or more during the 7 preceding previous years.

If a resident fails to satisfy either (or both) of these additional conditions, they are classified as Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).

Section 7.3

Scope of Total Income by Residential Status (Section 5)

The scope of income subject to Indian tax depends directly on whether the individual is ROR, RNOR, or Non-Resident (NR):

Nature of Income ROR RNOR Non-Resident (NR)
Income received or deemed to be received in India Taxable Taxable Taxable
Income accruing or arising (or deemed to accrue) in India Taxable Taxable Taxable
Income accruing outside India from a business controlled from or profession set up in India Taxable Taxable Not Taxable
Income accruing outside India from business outside India (pure foreign income) Taxable (Global) Not Taxable Not Taxable
Section 7.4

The Five Heads of Income (Section 14)

All taxable receipts of an assessee must fall strictly into one of the five mutually exclusive heads of income:

1. Income from Salaries (Sec 15-17)

Employer-employee relationship prerequisite. Standard deduction: ₹75,000 under New Tax Regime (raised by Finance Act 2024 from ₹50,000); ₹50,000 under Old Regime.

2. Income from House Property (Sec 22-27)

Rental income and annual value of property. Standard deduction @ 30% of Net Annual Value (NAV) under Sec 24(a). Home loan interest up to ₹2 Lakhs deductible under Sec 24(b) for self-occupied property (Old Regime only).

3. Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP)

Revenues minus admissible business expenses. Presumptive schemes: Section 44AD for small businesses (8% / 6% of turnover) and Section 44ADA for specified professionals (50% of gross receipts).

4. Capital Gains (Sec 45-55A)

Profits or gains arising from the transfer of a capital asset (Short-Term vs Long-Term). Subject to special rates under Sections 111A, 112, 112A, and 50AA.

5. Income from Other Sources (IFOS) (Sec 56-59)

Residuary head. Captures dividends, bank interest, family pension, gifts under Sec 56(2)(x), and lottery/gaming winnings (Sec 115BB).

Section 115BAC Default

Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)

From FY 2023-24 onwards, the New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC is the default regime. Taxpayers must explicitly choose to opt into the Old Tax Regime. The Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 further restructured the new regime slabs to provide deeper tax relief.

Section 87A Tax Rebate

New Regime: Rebate up to ₹25,000 for total taxable income up to ₹7,00,000. Net tax payable is ZERO for income up to ₹7 Lakhs.
Old Regime: Rebate up to ₹12,500 for total taxable income up to ₹5,00,000.

Salaried Threshold: ₹7.75 Lakhs Zero Tax

With the enhanced standard deduction of ₹75,000 under Finance Act 2024, a salaried individual earning a gross salary of up to ₹7,75,000 pays ₹0 tax under Section 115BAC (₹7,75,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹7,00,000 taxable → 87A rebate wipes out full tax).

Deductions Status under New Regime (Sec 115BAC):
❌ Foregone under New Regime:
  • Chapter VI-A (80C, 80CCC, 80D, 80E, 80G, 80TTA, 80TTB)
  • Section 80CCD(1B) additional ₹50k NPS
  • Section 24(b) interest on self-occupied house loan (₹2L)
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA) & Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)
✅ Specifically Retained under New Regime:
  • Standard deduction of ₹75,000 for salaried / ₹25,000 for family pension
  • Section 80CCD(2): Employer contribution to NPS (up to 14% of Salary)
  • Section 80JJAA (Deduction for new employment)
  • Section 80CCH(2) (Agnipath scheme contributions)
Section 7.6

Deductions under Chapter VI-A (Applicable for Old Tax Regime)

Chapter VI-A deductions allow eligible tax deductions from Gross Total Income to compute taxable income:

Section 80C, 80CCC & 80CCD(1)

Overall aggregate cap under Section 80CCE is ₹1,50,000. Covers EPF, PPF, ELSS, Life Insurance premiums (sum assured ≥ 10x), principal repayment of home loan, tuition fees, and NPS employee contributions.

Section 80CCD(1B) ⭐

Exclusive additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 for self-contributions to NPS Tier I. This is over and above the ₹1.5 Lakh limit under Section 80CCE (total NPS deduction possible = ₹2,00,000).

Section 80D: Health Insurance

Self/Spouse/Children: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if senior citizen). Parents: additional ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if senior citizens). Maximum deduction: ₹1,00,000 if both assessee and parents are ≥60 years. Includes ₹5,000 for preventive health check-up.

Section 80E: Higher Education Loan

Deduction for interest paid on loans borrowed for higher education for self, spouse, or children. No upper monetary ceiling. Available for a maximum period of 8 consecutive years.

Section 80TTA vs Section 80TTB

Sec 80TTA: Up to ₹10,000 on savings account interest for individuals <60 years.
Sec 80TTB: Up to ₹50,000 for resident senior citizens (≥60 years) on interest from all bank/post-office deposits (savings, FDs, RDs).

Section 7.7

Surcharge, Health & Education Cess, and Marginal Relief

Higher income earners pay a surcharge on base income tax, followed by a mandatory cess:

Surcharge Slabs for Individuals

  • • Total Income > ₹50 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore: 10%
  • • Total Income > ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crores: 15%
  • • Total Income > ₹2 Crores to ₹5 Crores: 25%
  • • Total Income > ₹5 Crores (Old Regime only): 37%
  • • Under New Regime (Sec 115BAC): Surcharge is CAPPED at 25% for all income exceeding ₹2 Crores!

Cess & Marginal Relief

Health & Education Cess: Levied at a uniform 4% on the sum of base income tax plus surcharge.

Marginal Relief: Granted when the tax payable (including surcharge) exceeds the tax payable on the threshold income by an amount greater than the excess income earned beyond the threshold.

Advance Tax Installments & Penal Interest (Sections 208, 234A, 234B, 234C)

Under Section 208, every person whose estimated tax liability for the financial year (after reducing TDS/TCS) is ₹10,000 or more must pay advance tax in four quarterly installments.

Senior Citizen Exemption: A resident individual aged 60 years or more who does NOT have any income from business or profession is completely exempt from paying advance tax.
Due Date of Installment Cumulative Minimum Percentage Payable Shortfall Trigger for Sec 234C
On or before 15th June Not less than 15% Interest if paid < 12% of total tax
On or before 15th September Not less than 45% Interest if paid < 36% of total tax
On or before 15th December Not less than 75% Interest if paid < 75% of total tax
On or before 15th March 100% of tax liability Interest if paid < 100% of total tax
Section 234A Delay in Filing Return: Interest @ 1% per month or part thereof on unpaid tax from the due date of filing ITR until actual filing date.
Section 234B Default in Paying Advance Tax: Triggered if advance tax paid is less than 90% of assessed tax. Interest @ 1% per month from 1st April of AY.
Section 234C Deferment of Installments: Interest @ 1% per month for 3 months (1 month for March installment) on shortfall in individual quarterly tranches.
