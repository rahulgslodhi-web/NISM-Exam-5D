Section 8.1 • Fast-Track Refresher ⚡
Fund-Level Tax Status

Tax Status of Mutual Funds & Section 10(23D) Pass-Through

Mutual funds in India are constituted as trusts under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. Under the Income Tax Act, 1961, mutual funds enjoy an overarching tax-exempt pass-through status designed to prevent double taxation of collective investments.

Section 10(23D) Complete Exemption

Any income earned by a mutual fund registered with SEBI (or set up by a public sector bank, public financial institution, or authorized by the RBI) is 100% exempt from income tax.

The fund pays zero tax on interest income, dividend earnings, or realized capital gains from trading portfolio assets.

Abolition of DDT (Finance Act 2020)

Prior to April 1, 2020, mutual funds were liable to pay Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) at the scheme level before distributing income.

Finance Act, 2020 completely abolished DDT. Mutual funds now distribute 100% gross IDCW payouts, and all income distribution is taxed directly in the hands of unitholders.

Statutory Classification of Mutual Funds for Taxation

The Income Tax Act classifies mutual funds into specific categories based strictly on their underlying portfolio allocation in domestic equity shares:

Tax Category Statutory Portfolio Criteria Typical Scheme Examples
Equity-Oriented Fund ≥ 65% of total proceeds invested in equity shares of domestic listed companies.
FoF Equity Condition: Minimum 90% invested in units of another domestic equity fund traded on a recognized exchange.
Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Flexi Cap, ELSS, Aggressive Hybrid Schemes (≥65% equity).
Specified Mutual Fund (Sec 50AA) Not more than 35% of total proceeds invested in equity shares of domestic companies, acquired on or after April 1, 2023. Pure Debt Funds, Liquid, Money Market, Overnight, Conservative Hybrid (≤35% equity), International FoFs.
Other Non-Equity Funds Investments where domestic equity is between >35% and <65%, or funds investing predominantly in commodities. Gold ETFs/Funds, Silver ETFs/Funds, Balanced Hybrid, Dynamic Asset Allocation (if equity 35%-65%).

Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024

Capital Gains Taxation Architecture (Budget 2024 Overhaul)

The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 (Budget 2024) introduced a landmark rationalization of capital gains tax rates, holding periods, and exemption thresholds effective from July 23, 2024. Questions on these exact revised rates and categories are guaranteed in the exam.

Category 1: Equity Funds (Sec 111A & 112A)

  • Holding Period: 12 months is the dividing line. Up to 12 months is STCG; more than 12 months is LTCG.
  • STCG (Sec 111A): Tax rate hiked from 15% to 20% (plus 4% cess and applicable surcharge).
  • LTCG (Sec 112A): Tax rate hiked from 10% to 12.5%.
  • Annual Exemption Limit: Increased from ₹1,00,000 to ₹1,25,000 per financial year. The 12.5% tax applies only to aggregate net equity capital gains in excess of ₹1.25 Lakhs.
  • Grandfathering Clause: Capital gains up to January 31, 2018 remain grandfathered (exempt) using the fair market value formula.

Category 2: Specified Debt Funds (Sec 50AA)

  • Applicability: Funds where domestic equity is ≤ 35%, acquired on or after April 1, 2023.
  • Deemed Short-Term: Any gain arising from transfer or redemption is deemed to be short-term capital gains, irrespective of whether the unitholder held the fund for 6 months or 7 years.
  • Tax Rate: Added to unitholder's total income and taxed at the applicable marginal slab rate.
  • Indexation Abolished: Cost Inflation Index (CII) benefit was eliminated for these funds.
  • Grandfathered Debt Units: Units acquired before April 1, 2023 retain LTCG status if held > 36 months (taxed at 20% with indexation or 12.5% without indexation).

Step-by-Step Practical Tax Calculations

Numerical Example 1: Equity Fund Capital Gains Computation Post-Budget 2024 Rules

An investor buys 10,000 units of an Equity Diversified Fund on 10-Aug-2023 at NAV of ₹50 (Total Cost = ₹5,00,000).
• Sale 1 (15-Jun-2024): Redeems 5,000 units at NAV ₹65 (Holding: ~10 months → STCG).
• Sale 2 (20-Oct-2024): Redeems remaining 5,000 units at NAV ₹85 (Holding: ~14 months → LTCG).

Step 1: Compute STCG (Sale 1 - Holding ≤ 12 months):

Proceeds: 5,000 × ₹65 = ₹3,25,000 | Cost: 5,000 × ₹50 = ₹2,50,000
Net STCG = ₹3,25,000 - ₹2,50,000 = ₹75,000
Tax under Section 111A @ 20% = ₹75,000 × 20% = ₹15,000

Step 2: Compute LTCG (Sale 2 - Holding > 12 months):

Proceeds: 5,000 × ₹85 = ₹4,25,000 | Cost: 5,000 × ₹50 = ₹2,50,000
Gross LTCG = ₹4,25,000 - ₹2,50,000 = ₹1,75,000
Less: Annual Exemption u/s 112A = ₹1,25,000
Taxable LTCG = ₹1,75,000 - ₹1,25,000 = ₹50,000
Tax under Section 112A @ 12.5% = ₹50,000 × 12.5% = ₹6,250

Step 3: Total Base Tax Liability:

Total Base Tax = ₹15,000 (STCG) + ₹6,250 (LTCG) = ₹21,250 (plus 4% Health & Education Cess = ₹850 → Total ₹22,100).

Numerical Example 2: Debt Fund (Sec 50AA) vs Equity Fund Tax Disparity High Slab Impact

Investor "Ramesh" is in the 30% tax bracket. He holds two investments for 3 years, each generating a net capital gain of ₹2,00,000 upon redemption in FY 2024-25.

Case A: Equity Fund (>12m)

Gain: ₹2,00,000
Exemption: ₹1,25,000
Taxable Gain: ₹75,000
Tax @ 12.5%: ₹9,375
Add Cess (4%): ₹375
Total Tax: ₹9,750 (Effective Rate: 4.88%)

Case B: Specified Debt Fund (Sec 50AA)

Gain: ₹2,00,000
Exemption: ₹0 (No exemption)
Taxable Gain: ₹2,00,000
Tax @ 30% Slab: ₹60,000
Add Cess (4%): ₹2,400
Total Tax: ₹62,400 (Effective Rate: 31.2%)

Takeaway for Distributors: Due to Section 50AA, high-tax-bracket investors face significantly higher tax liability in debt funds compared to equity funds for identical capital gains.

Dividend & TDS Norms

Taxation of IDCW (Dividends) & TDS under Section 194K

Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (IDCW) represents dividend payouts declared by mutual fund schemes from distributable reserves. Since the abolition of scheme-level DDT, IDCW taxation is entirely transferred to the investor's tax computation.

Taxability in Hands of Unitholder

All IDCW payouts (whether from equity, debt, or hybrid schemes) are taxed under the head "Income from Other Sources" at the investor's applicable marginal income tax slab rate.

Expense Deduction (Section 57): Only interest expense incurred for borrowing money to invest in units is deductible, capped at a maximum of 20% of gross dividend income. No other expense (advisory, depository charges) is deductible.

TDS Architecture under Section 194K

When an AMC pays or credits IDCW to a resident unitholder, it must withhold Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 194K:

10% TDS if IDCW exceeds ₹5,000 per financial year per AMC
  • Section 206AA: If PAN is not provided (or is invalid/inoperative due to non-linking with Aadhaar), TDS is deducted at 20%.
  • Form 15G / 15H: Resident investors whose estimated total income is below the taxable exemption limit can submit Form 15G (aged <60) or Form 15H (senior citizens aged ≥60) to receive IDCW without TDS.
  • NRI Investors: Governed by Section 195. TDS is deducted at 20% (plus applicable surcharge & 4% cess). NRIs can claim Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) treaty rates by providing a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) and Form 10F.

Why Growth Option is Financially Superior to IDCW

Exam questions frequently test the comparative tax advantages of the Growth Option over the IDCW Option:

Parameter Growth Option IDCW (Payout / Reinvestment)
Tax Incidence Timing Deferred until actual redemption Immediate every time IDCW is paid

STT, Stamp Duty & 80C

Securities Transaction Tax (STT), Stamp Duty & ELSS (Sec 80C)

Securities Transaction Tax

Levied strictly on equity-oriented mutual funds.

• Purchase from AMC: NIL STT
• Redemption / Repurchase: 0.001% payable by seller
• ETF on Stock Exchange: 0.001% on sale (delivery)
• Debt & Other Funds: Zero STT
Indian Stamp Duty

Uniform pan-India stamp duty of 0.005% governed by Indian Stamp Act, 1899.

• Charged On: All unit issuances (Lumpsum, SIP, STP-in, Reinvested IDCW)
• Deducted: Upfront from investment before allotment
• Redemption: NIL Stamp Duty
ELSS under Section 80C

Tax deduction up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year under Section 80C.

• Lock-in Period: Mandatory 3 years from allotment
• SIP Lock-in Rule: Each SIP instalment has its own distinct 3-year lock-in
• Tax Regime: Available ONLY under Old Tax Regime; NOT under New Regime (Sec 115BAC)

Anti-Avoidance & Set-Off

Bonus Stripping (Sec 94(8)) & Loss Set-Off Framework

The Income Tax Act contains strict anti-avoidance provisions to prevent taxpayers from engineering artificial capital losses to offset taxable gains, paired with rigid set-off rules.

Bonus Stripping (Section 94(8)) Rules & Disallowance

Section 94(8) targets taxpayers who buy units cum-bonus to artificially create a capital loss upon post-bonus NAV reduction. Bonus stripping triggers if ALL THREE conditions are satisfied:

Condition 1: Purchase

Investor acquires units within 3 months prior to the record date of bonus issue.

Condition 2: Allotment

Investor is allotted bonus units without payment on the record date.

Condition 3: Sale

Investor sells or redeems original units within 9 months after record date while retaining bonus units.

Legal Consequence of Bonus Stripping:

1. The capital loss arising on the sale of the original units is completely disallowed and cannot be set off against any capital gains.
2. The disallowed loss is added to the cost of acquisition of the bonus units retained by the unitholder, adjusting their future capital gains liability.

Numerical Example 3: Step-by-Step Bonus Stripping Mechanics Exam Scenario

Case Facts:
• 1-May: Investor buys 1,000 units at NAV ₹100 (Total investment: ₹1,00,000).
• 1-Jun: Fund announces 1:1 bonus issue (Record date: 15-Jun → Bought within 3 months).
• 15-Jun: Investor receives 1,000 bonus units at zero cost (Total units = 2,000; NAV adjusts to ₹50).
• 1-Aug: Investor sells the original 1,000 units at NAV ₹50 (Sale within 9 months).

Proceeds from original 1,000 units = 1,000 × ₹50 = ₹50,000
Cost of original 1,000 units = 1,000 × ₹100 = ₹1,00,000
Apparent Short-Term Capital Loss = ₹50,000 - ₹1,00,000 = -₹50,000
→ Under Section 94(8), this ₹50,000 loss is DISALLOWED for set-off!
→ Cost of acquisition of the 1,000 bonus units becomes: ₹0 + ₹50,000 = ₹50,000 (i.e. ₹50/unit).

Capital Loss Set-Off & Carry Forward Rules (Sections 70, 71 & 74)

Intra-Head Set-Off (Section 70)

  • Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL): Can be set off against both Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG).
  • Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL): Can ONLY be set off against Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). It can NEVER be set off against STCG.

Inter-Head Set-Off & Carry Forward (Sec 71 & 74)

  • Inter-Head Ban (Sec 71): Capital losses cannot be set off against any other income head (Salary, Business Income, House Property, or Other Sources).
  • 8-Year Carry Forward (Sec 74): Unabsorbed losses can be carried forward for up to 8 consecutive assessment years.
  • Mandatory ITR Filing: Return of income must be filed on or before the due date under Section 139(1) to carry forward capital losses.
