Basic Concept

Investors and their Financial Goals

1. "Start with Why" Advisory begins with understanding the investor's purpose (Why invest?), not product pushing.
2. Objective vs. Goal Objective: Wish to buy a home.
Goal: Objective + Target Amount + Timeline.
3. The Pool Approach Flaw Putting all funds into one unsegregated pool hides the time horizon for each goal, leading to wrong asset choices.

Stephen Covey’s Quadrant II Focus & Goal Inflation:

• Quadrant II (Important, Not Urgent): Long-term goals like Retirement & Children's College. If delayed, they become urgent crises.
• Goal Inflation: Future cost of goals expands over time: A = P × (1 + r)n (where P = current cost, r = inflation rate, n = years).

Savings vs. Investments

Saving focuses on capital safety and deferring consumption (root: safe). Investing puts saved capital to work for profit under a risk-return trade-off. Remember: Saving always precedes investing.

Section 1.4

Investment Risks & Return Concepts

1. Inflation Risk (Purchasing Power)

Inflation erodes purchasing power over time. If inflation is 8% p.a., what costs ₹10,000 today will cost ₹21,589 in 10 years and ₹1,00,627 in 30 years.

Real Rate of Return = Nominal Rate − Inflation Rate

2. Liquidity Risk

Risk of inability to liquidate an asset quickly at fair market value. Pre-mature withdrawal penalties lower returns; locked-in instruments (like PPF) have restricted liquidity.

3. Credit Risk (Default Risk)

Possibility of default or delay in payment of interest or repayment of principal by the borrower. Governed by the borrower’s ability and intention. Government securities (G-Secs) carry the highest safety with virtually zero credit risk.

4. Market Risk & Systematic vs Unsystematic Risk

• Unsystematic (Firm-Specific / Diversifiable): Labour strike, CEO exit. Can be eliminated by diversification.
• Systematic (Market-wide / Non-diversifiable): Wars, GDP growth, interest rate revisions. Cannot be eliminated through diversification.

5. Interest Rate Risk in Bonds (Inverse Relationship ⭐):

When interest rates rise in the economy, prices of existing fixed-rate bonds decrease (because newer bonds offer higher rates). When interest rates fall, bond prices increase. Bonds with longer maturities experience higher price volatility.

3 Core Strategies

Risk Management Strategies

1. Avoid Avoid instruments you do not understand (gives up opportunity but eliminates specific risk).
2. Take Position Dynamic bets in anticipation of events (e.g., buying long bonds before rate cuts; high skill required).
3. Diversify Spreads risk across asset classes and securities. Eliminates unsystematic risk.

Behavioural Biases in Investment Decision Making

Investors are frequently guided by emotions (fear, greed, hope) rather than pure logic. Recognizing these psychological biases is critical for distributors and advisors:

1. Availability Heuristic

Relying predominantly on immediate examples or recent experiences that come to mind quickly when evaluating options, skipping thorough analysis.

2. Confirmation Bias

Seeking only data and opinions that confirm pre-existing beliefs while ignoring contrary evidence or critical risk warnings.

3. Familiarity Bias

Preferring the familiar over the novel ("A known devil is better than an unknown angel"), leading to over-concentrating in home countries or known companies.

4. Herd Mentality

Following the crowd into trending investments out of fear of missing out (FOMO), even when valuations are unjustifiably elevated.

5. Loss Aversion (Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky) ⭐

The psychological pain of losing an amount (e.g., ₹5,000) is felt much more intensely than the pleasure of gaining the equivalent amount (₹5,000). This causes investors to avoid profitable opportunities due to disproportionate fear of loss.

6. Overconfidence Bias

Overestimating one’s own analytical skills or forecasting ability, leading to inadequate risk management and reckless portfolio exposure.

7. Recency Bias

Extrapolating recent market events into the far future — assuming a bull market will never end, or that a market crash will continue forever.

8. Investor Interest Bias (Sector Bias)

Building portfolios skewed entirely toward the investor’s own industry (e.g., an IT engineer only holding Tech stocks), creating massive concentration risk.

Sections 1.7 & 1.8

Risk Profiling & Asset Allocation Strategies

The 3 Pillars of Risk Profiling

1. Need to take risk Dictated by return target required to achieve the financial goals.
2. Ability to take risk Determined by financial surplus, wealth, income stability, and investment time horizon.
3. Willingness to take risk Psychological & emotional capacity to withstand market fluctuations.

Strategic (SAA) vs. Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA)

Strategic (SAA)

Target long-term mix (e.g., 60% Equity / 40% Debt) aligned with the investor’s goals, horizon, and risk profile. Fixed baseline meant to be maintained across market cycles.

Tactical (TAA) / Dynamic

Active deviations from the target mix to exploit temporary short-term market mispricings and improve risk-adjusted returns. Suitable for seasoned investors.

Rebalancing Principle & SEBI Mandate ⭐:

• Why Rebalance: As equity grows faster than debt, portfolio drifts (e.g. 50:50 becomes 60:40). Selling appreciated equity to buy debt automatically enforces the discipline of "Buy Low, Sell High" without market forecasting.
• SEBI Mandated Rebalancing Timeline: In case of passive breaches in asset allocation specified in SID, AMCs must rebalance the portfolio within 30 business days (for all schemes other than Index Funds & ETFs). Note: Mandated rebalancing period is NOT applicable to Overnight Funds.

Basic Concept

Do-It-Yourself (DIY) vs. Taking Professional Help

Evaluating whether an investor should manage money independently or utilize professional mutual funds / registered advisors boils down to three key questions:

1. Can one do the job? Requires analytical competence, financial markets knowledge, accounting diligence, and dedicated time.
2. Does one want to do it? Many investors lack interest in tracking balance sheets or rebalancing sheets, preferring to focus on career/family.
3. Can one afford it? Comparing fund expenses against the hidden opportunity cost of one's time and expensive behavioural mistakes.
