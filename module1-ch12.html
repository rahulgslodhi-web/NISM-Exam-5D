Asset Allocation

Strategic vs. Tactical vs. Dynamic Asset Allocation

Empirical financial research (Brinson, Hood & Beebower) confirms that over 90% of portfolio return variability over time is determined by broad asset allocation rather than individual security selection or market timing.

Attribute Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA) Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA) Dynamic Asset Allocation (DAA)
Primary Objective Long-term wealth creation aligned to investor life goals and risk profile. Short/medium-term alpha generation and opportunistic mispricing capture. Systematic downside mitigation and rule-based counter-cyclical exposure.
Time Horizon Long-term (5 to 30+ years). Short to intermediate (3 months to 2 years). Continuous / algorithmically rebalanced daily/monthly.
Decision Driver Investor risk capacity, time horizon, and return target. Macro trends, valuation metrics (P/E, P/B, yield gap), and sentiment. Pre-defined quantitative models (e.g. Balanced Advantage Funds / BAF).
Rebalancing Trigger Fixed calendar intervals (e.g. annual) or corridor threshold drift (±5% or 10%). Market turning points, sudden valuation distortions, or policy shifts. Automated signals (e.g., trailing P/E ratios, moving averages).
Market Dependency Remains constant through economic bull and bear cycles. Deviates intentionally from the baseline strategic target. Dynamically shifts equity between 30% and 80% based on model inputs.
Practical Scenario: SAA Rebalancing vs. TAA Tactical Move

Investor Sunita (Age 42): Strategic Baseline (SAA) is set at 65% Equity : 35% Debt for her retirement fund.

SAA Threshold Rebalancing

Following an equity bull run, equity surges to 76% and debt drops to 24%. Under SAA rules, the advisor trims 11% equity and allocates it to debt to restore the target 65:35 balance, locking in gains and reducing risk.

TAA Opportunistic Tilt

During a severe macroeconomic correction where equity valuations plunge to attractive levels (Nifty trailing P/E < 18), the advisor tactically overweights equity to 75% for 9 months, trimming back to 65% when valuations normalize.

Portfolio Construction

The Core and Satellite Portfolio Construction Strategy

The Core and Satellite framework is the industry-standard architecture for constructing robust, cost-effective mutual fund portfolios. It blends reliable passive beta with tactical active alpha generation.

Core Component (65% to 80%) Satellite Component (20% to 35%)
CORE: 75% (Stability & Broad Market Beta)
SATELLITE: 25% (Alpha)
Core Portfolio (65% – 80%)

The permanent structural foundation of the portfolio. Protects capital against catastrophic market events while capturing broad equity compounding.

Eligible Scheme Categories: Nifty 50 / BSE Sensex Index Funds, Large Cap Active Funds, Flexi Cap Funds, Sovereign G-Sec Funds, Banking & PSU Debt Funds.
Satellite Portfolio (20% – 35%)

Opportunistic high-conviction allocations designed to generate excess returns (alpha) across favorable economic cycles.

Eligible Scheme Categories: Mid Cap & Small Cap Funds, Sectoral / Thematic Funds (e.g. Infrastructure, Healthcare, Tech), Specialized Investment Funds (SIF), Credit Risk Funds.

Holding Period & Review Long-term buy-and-hold; reviewed annually for rebalancing. Medium-term cyclical holding; actively reviewed quarterly or semi-annually.

Equity Selection & PTR

Selecting Equity Schemes: The Quantitative & Qualitative Framework

Selecting mutual fund schemes requires looking beyond past 1-year trailing returns. Distributors must evaluate risk-adjusted ratios, portfolio churn, portfolio concentration, and fund manager continuity.

Portfolio Turnover Ratio (PTR) Formula & Computation Formula Mandate
PTR = Lower of [ Total Purchases OR Total Sales during the period ] ÷ Average Daily Net Assets (AUM)
⭐ Step-by-Step Practical Numerical Walkthrough:

Given Data for Scheme Alpha:
• Scheme Average Daily AUM over the last 12 months = ₹3,000 Crores
• Total equity securities purchased during the year = ₹950 Crores
• Total equity securities sold during the year = ₹600 Crores

1. Determine Numerator: Min(Purchases, Sales) = Min(₹950 Cr, ₹600 Cr) = ₹600 Crores
2. Determine Denominator: Average Daily Net Assets = ₹3,000 Crores
3. PTR Calculation: ₹600 Cr ÷ ₹3,000 Cr = 0.20 or 20%

Interpretation: A PTR of 20% indicates that only one-fifth of the scheme portfolio was replaced over the year, demonstrating a disciplined, high-conviction buy-and-hold strategy with minimal transaction brokerage costs.

Low PTR (< 30%) Characteristics
  • High conviction, patient long-term compounding.
  • Lower brokerage, exchange fees, and STT costs.
  • Reduced short-term capital gains tax friction.
High PTR (> 100%) Characteristics
  • Aggressive momentum trading and frequent sector rotation.
  • High transaction drag impacting net investor NAV.
  • Indicates lack of long-term conviction or strategy drift.
Concentration Risk

Examine the Top 10 Stocks and Top 3 Sectors exposure:

  • Broadly Diversified: Top 10 stocks hold < 35% of AUM.
  • Concentrated / Focused: Top 10 stocks exceed 50–65% of AUM (SEBI limits Focused Funds to max 30 stocks).
  • Excessive sector exposure (> 35% in BFSI or Tech) heightens cyclical volatility.
Fund Size / AUM Impact

AUM impact differs sharply across market-cap categories:

  • Large-Cap Funds: Giant AUM (> ₹40,000 Cr) is manageable due to high liquidity of Nifty 100 stocks.
  • Small-Cap Funds: Very large AUM (> ₹20,000 Cr) causes liquidity drag, market impact cost, or forces cash hoarding.
Manager Tenure & AMC

Qualitative governance pillars:

  • Manager Tenure: Minimum 3 to 5 years continuity across complete bull and bear market cycles.
  • Process-Driven AMC: Investment philosophy governed by strong internal research committees, not individual star managers.

Debt Selection & Duration Matching

Debt Scheme Selection: Macaulay Duration Matching & The Immunization Principle

In fixed-income investing, capital safety is achieved not by chasing yields, but through strict alignment of the investor's planned investment horizon with the fund's Macaulay Duration.

⭐ The Immunization Principle: How Duration Matching Neutralizes Interest Rate Shocks

Every debt fund faces two opposing, offsetting forces when benchmark interest rates change:

1. Price Risk (Capital Value)

When interest rates rise, existing bond prices drop, causing a negative capital impact on the scheme NAV.

2. Reinvestment Risk (Coupon Compounding)

When interest rates rise, periodic coupon cash flows and maturing bond proceeds are reinvested at higher prevailing yields.

The Mathematical Equilibrium: At the exact point where Investor Holding Period = Macaulay Duration, the capital loss from rising rates is identically offset by the compounded gains from reinvesting coupons at higher rates. The investor is fully immunized against rate volatility!

Yield to Maturity (YTM) vs. Net Yield
Expected Net Yield ≈ Portfolio YTM − Total Expense Ratio (TER)

Crucial Exam Caveat: YTM is not a guaranteed return! It is an internal rate of return (IRR) that holds true ONLY if:

  • All underlying securities are held until final maturity.
  • Every issuer pays coupons and principal without any credit default.
  • All intermediate coupon inflows are reinvested at the exact same YTM rate.
Modified Duration (MD) Sensitivity Rule
% Change in Bond Price ≈ − Modified Duration × ΔYield (%)

Practical Calculation Example:
A Medium Duration Fund has a Modified Duration of 4.2 years. If RBI cuts the repo rate and market yields drop by 0.50% (-50 bps):

% Price Gain ≈ − 4.2 × (−0.50%) = +2.10% Capital Appreciation

Investor Profiling

Investor Risk Profiling: Risk Capacity vs. Risk Appetite

SEBI mandates that every mutual fund distributor carry out an objective risk assessment of the client before recommending any financial product.

Risk Capacity (Financial Ability) Objective

The investor's mathematical and financial ability to absorb portfolio losses without altering their standard of living or jeopardizing essential life goals (e.g. child education, retirement).

Governed by: Dependable regular income, net worth, existing liabilities/EMIs, number of financial dependents, investment time horizon, and emergency liquidity reserve.
Risk Appetite / Tolerance Subjective / Psychological

The investor's emotional and behavioral willingness to endure market volatility and portfolio drawdowns without panicking or liquidating investments at market bottoms.

Governed by: Personality traits, emotional resilience, financial literacy, prior market trauma, and behavioral biases (e.g. severe loss aversion).
⭐ Cardinal Exam Rule: Risk Capacity Strictly Overrides Risk Appetite!

If a client has a High Risk Appetite (eager to gamble in aggressive small-cap/thematic funds) but has Low Risk Capacity (low income, high debt, retiring in 18 months), the distributor must NOT recommend high-risk equity products. The financial ability to survive losses always takes precedence over psychological desire!

Retirement Distribution Phase: Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) vs. IDCW (Payout)
IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal)
  • Dividends are declared at AMC discretion; cash flow is unpredictable.
  • Tax Drag: 100% of the dividend received is taxed as ordinary income at the investor's applicable slab rates (up to 39%).
SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan) - Recommended
  • Predictable, customized cash flow chosen by the investor.
  • High Tax Efficiency: Each redemption comprises principal return + capital gain. Tax is levied ONLY on the capital gain component, not the entire withdrawal!

Code of Conduct

Mutual Fund Distributor Code of Conduct & Regulatory Compliance

SEBI and AMFI enforce strict fiduciary standards on distributors. Violations attract immediate suspension, ARN cancellation, commission clawback, and regulatory debarment.

Strict Regulatory Prohibitions (Absolute "DON'TS")

1. Portfolio Churning

Advising or inducing clients to frequently redeem and reinvest between schemes to generate transaction volumes or earn fresh trail commissions.

2. Commission Rebating / Pass-Back

Passing back, sharing, or rebating any part of distributor commission to the investor in cash, gifts, discounts, or indirect benefits. Strictly illegal.

3. Guaranteed Return Claims

Promising, assuring, or indicating fixed returns or guaranteed capital safety on any open-ended mutual fund scheme.

4. Handling Investor Cash / Cheques

Accepting cheques in distributor's personal or firm name. Cheques must be drawn strictly in favor of the scheme/AMC. Cash is capped at ₹50,000/year.

Mandatory Affirmative Duties (Absolute "DO'S")

Product Suitability

Ensure the recommended scheme aligns strictly with the client's financial goals, horizon, and objective risk capacity.

Commission Disclosure

Disclose all trail commission rates received across competing mutual fund houses to the investor upfront.

EUIN Quoting

Mandatorily quote the Employee Unique Identification Number (EUIN) on every application to maintain personal advisory accountability.
