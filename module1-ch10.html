Section 10.1 • Core Foundations
Risk Architecture

Systematic (Market) Risk vs. Unsystematic (Specific) Risk

Investment risk in mutual funds represents the probability that the actual realized return will differ from the expected return, including the possibility of capital loss. Total risk consists of two distinct components.

Systematic Risk (Market Risk) Non-Diversifiable

Inherent to the entire macro-economy and financial system. It affects all securities simultaneously to varying degrees. It CANNOT be eliminated or diversified away by adding more securities to a portfolio.

Key Sub-Categories:
  • Interest Rate Risk: When market interest rates rise, bond prices fall across all fixed income instruments (inverse relationship).
  • Inflation Risk (Purchasing Power Risk): Rising price levels erode the real purchasing power of future portfolio cash flows.
  • Reinvestment Risk: Risk that interim coupon cash flows will have to be reinvested at lower prevailing yields.
  • Exchange Rate / Currency Risk: Depreciating domestic currency impacts international funds, ADRs, and import-dependent businesses.
  • Socio-Political & Geopolitical Risk: General elections, wars, supply shocks, fiscal policy shifts affecting the whole nation.
Statistical Metric: Measured by Beta (β) and Market Variance (σm2).
Unsystematic Risk (Specific Risk) Diversifiable

Specific to an individual corporate issuer, company management, or a narrow industry sector. It CAN be virtually eliminated through proper portfolio diversification across 25 to 30 non-correlated securities.

Key Sub-Categories:
  • Credit / Default Risk: Risk that a bond issuer fails to make timely coupon or principal repayments.
  • Credit Downgrade Risk: A rating agency downgrades an issuer (e.g., AA to BBB), widening credit spreads and immediately reducing bond price.
  • Business / Operational Risk: Product obsolescence, raw material shortages, labor strikes, loss of key corporate customers.
  • Financial Risk (Leverage): High debt burdens causing insolvency distress when interest coverage ratios fall.
  • Liquidity Risk: Inability to sell an asset swiftly at fair market value due to low trading volumes (high impact cost).
Risk Control: SEBI prudential exposure caps (e.g., max 10% in single stock, max 20% in single sector).

Scheme-Specific Risk Profiles Across Categories

Equity Funds

Subject to equity market volatility, small/mid-cap illiquidity risk, valuation multiple contraction, and sector concentration risk.

Debt Funds

Exposed to interest rate risk (price drop when yields rise), credit rating downgrade risk, default risk, and reinvestment risk.

Liquid / Overnight Funds

Subject to sudden institutional redemption pressure, reinvestment rate decline, and short-term commercial paper credit events.

Arbitrage / Hybrid Funds

Exposed to basis risk (spread between cash equity and stock futures narrowing/inverting) and dynamic rebalancing execution costs.

International Funds

Subject to currency/forex fluctuations (INR vs. USD/EUR), cross-border taxation, geopolitical embargoes, and country risk.

Gold & Commodity Funds

Exposed to international commodity spot price swings, physical storage/custody tracking costs, and tracking error.

Section 10.2 • Statistical Measures
Risk Measurement

Quantitative Risk Metrics & The SEBI Risk-o-meter

Measuring risk allows investors and distributors to evaluate performance on a risk-adjusted basis rather than looking at raw returns in isolation.

Standard Deviation (σ)
Total Risk Measure

Measures the degree of dispersion or fluctuation of historical returns around their mean (average). A higher standard deviation indicates greater volatility and higher total risk (both systematic and unsystematic).

Variance = σ²
Std Dev = √(σ²)
Beta (β)
Systematic Risk Measure

Measures the sensitivity of a scheme's returns relative to benchmark market fluctuations:

  • β = 1.0: Fund moves in tandem with benchmark.
  • β > 1.0: Aggressive (e.g. β = 1.20 means 20% more volatile than market).
  • β < 1.0: Defensive (e.g. β = 0.80 means 20% less volatile).
Modified Duration
Bond Price Sensitivity

Measures percentage change in a bond's price for a 100 bps (1%) change in market yield:

Δ Price ≈ -Modified Duration × Δ Yield

Example: If Modified Duration is 4.0 years and yield rises by 50 bps (+0.5%), bond price falls by approx 2.0%.

SEBI Dynamic 6-Level Risk-o-meter
Monthly Recalculation

SEBI mandates a visual 6-level Risk-o-meter across all mutual fund schemes: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High, and Very High.

1. Low
2. Low-Mod
3. Moderate
4. Mod-High
5. High
6. Very High

• Evaluation Frequency: Calculated monthly based on actual month-end portfolio holdings (liquidity, credit rating, and duration for debt; market-cap, volatility, and impact cost for equity).

• Disclosure: Published within 10 days from the close of each month on AMC and AMFI websites. Any change in the Risk-o-meter must be communicated to investors via email/SMS.

Section 10.3 • Super High Exam Yield ⭐
Calculation Standards

Performance Measurement: Simple, Annualized, CAGR & XIRR

NISM examinations rigorously test the mathematical formulas, regulatory horizons, and sign conventions for mutual fund returns.

1. Simple (Absolute) Return / Holding Period Return (HPR) Absolute Gain

Measures the percentage change in NAV plus any distributed dividends (IDCW) relative to the initial purchase NAV. It completely ignores the time element.

$$\text{Simple Return (\%)} = \left[ \frac{\text{NAV}_{\text{End}} - \text{NAV}_{\text{Begin}} + \text{Dividends Received}}{\text{NAV}_{\text{Begin}}} \right] \times 100$$
Numerical Example:

An investor purchases units at NAV of ₹40.00, receives ₹2.50 dividend during the period, and sells units at ₹46.50.

$$\text{Simple Return} = \left[ \frac{46.50 - 40.00 + 2.50}{40.00} \right] \times 100 = \left[ \frac{9.00}{40.00} \right] \times 100 = 22.50\%$$

2. Simple Annualized Return (Mandatory for Periods < 1 Year) ⭐ SEBI Mandate
CRITICAL SEBI REGULATION: For holding periods of less than 1 year (e.g. 7 days, 15 days, 30 days, 91 days, 182 days), performance CANNOT be compounded (CAGR is strictly prohibited). It MUST be presented as a Simple Annualized Return.
$$\text{Annualized Return (\%)} = \text{Simple Return (\%)} \times \left( \frac{365}{\text{Number of Days}} \right)$$
Example A (Liquid Fund 7-Day Return):

NAV rises from ₹1,000.00 to ₹1,001.35 in 7 days.

$$\text{Simple Return} = \frac{1.35}{1000} \times 100 = 0.135\%$$

$$\text{Annualized} = 0.135\% \times \frac{365}{7} = 7.039\%$$

Example B (Ultra Short Fund 73-Day Return):

NAV rises from ₹100.00 to ₹108.00 in 73 days.

$$\text{Simple Return} = \frac{8.00}{100} \times 100 = 8.00\%$$

$$\text{Annualized} = 8.0\% \times \frac{365}{73} = 8.0\% \times 5 = 40.00\%$$

3. Compounded Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) (Mandatory for Periods > 1 Year) ⭐ Lumpsum > 1 Year

CAGR measures the smooth annual geometric growth rate over multi-year holding periods (1, 3, 5, 10 years, and since inception), assuming reinvestment and compounding of all returns.

$$\text{CAGR (\%)} = \left[ \left( \frac{\text{NAV}_{\text{End}}}{\text{NAV}_{\text{Begin}}} \right)^{\frac{1}{N}} - 1 \right] \times 100 \quad \text{where } N = \text{years} = \frac{\text{Days}}{365}$$
Exam Numerical 1 (2-Year Holding):

Buy NAV ₹20.00; Sold at ₹28.80 after 2 years.

$$\text{Ratio} = 28.80 / 20.00 = 1.44$$

$$\text{CAGR} = (1.44)^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1 = \sqrt{1.44} - 1 = 1.20 - 1 = 0.20$$

$$\text{CAGR} = 20.00\%$$

Note: Absolute return is 44%, simple average is 22%, but CAGR accounts for compounding and is 20%.

Exam Numerical 2 (3-Year Holding):

Buy NAV ₹50.00; Sold at ₹86.40 after 3 years.

$$\text{Ratio} = 86.40 / 50.00 = 1.728$$

$$\text{CAGR} = (1.728)^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1 = 1.20 - 1 = 0.20$$

$$\text{CAGR} = 20.00\%$$

4. Extended Internal Rate of Return (XIRR) ⭐ SIP & Multiple Cashflows

CAGR fails when an investor executes multiple periodic transactions (Systematic Investment Plans - SIP, Systematic Transfer Plans - STP, dividend payouts, or staggered redemptions) because each installment stays invested for a different duration. XIRR is the annualized internal rate of return for non-periodic, multiple dated cash flows.

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{C_i}{(1 + r)^{\frac{d_i - d_0}{365}}} = 0$$
Where: $C_i$ = Cash flow on date $d_i$, $d_0$ = Initial date, $r$ = XIRR (discount rate making NPV = 0).
Sign Conventions for SIP XIRR in Financial Systems:
Outflows (Investments):

Every SIP installment is entered with a NEGATIVE (-) sign (cash leaving the investor's pocket).

Inflows (Valuation / Redemption):

The terminal portfolio market value or redemption proceeds is entered with a POSITIVE (+) sign.

Section 10.4 • Evaluation Quality
Consistency Metric

Rolling Returns vs. Point-to-Point (Trailing) Bias

Distributors and investors often fall victim to marketing brochures advertising attractive 3-year or 5-year returns that are heavily distorted by point-to-point end-date bias.

Point-to-Point (Trailing) Returns End-Date Bias

Calculates performance strictly between two fixed calendar dates (e.g., 1-Jan-2021 to 1-Jan-2024).

Severe Analytical Limitations:
  • Extreme Point-to-Point Bias: Highly sensitive to market conditions on the specific start and end dates.
  • If the start date coincides with a major crash (e.g., March 2020), 3-year trailing returns appear artificially spectacular.
  • If the end date hits a market slump, trailing returns severely understate the fund manager's long-term capability.
  • Can be easily cherry-picked in advertisements to mislead unsophisticated investors.
Rolling Returns (Gold Standard) Unbiased

Calculates annualized CAGR across overlapping continuous time windows on a daily or weekly frequency across market cycles.

Why Rolling Returns are Superior:
  • Completely Eliminates End-Date Bias: Evaluates fund performance across thousands of discrete market entry points.
  • Reveals True Consistency: Shows what percentage of rolling periods outperformed the benchmark index.
  • Measures Downside Risk: Shows the minimum, maximum, and median returns observed, highlighting capital preservation during bear markets.
  • A fund delivering 14% rolling return in 90% of 3-year windows is far superior to a volatile fund with high trailing return from a lucky exit peak.
How 3-Year Rolling Returns Work Conceptually:

01-Jan-21 to 01-Jan-24
Window 2

Window N
Daily Rolled Forward

Over a 10-year span, calculating 3-year rolling returns on a daily frequency yields ~1,700 distinct data points, providing statistically robust insights into consistency and drawdown resilience.

How AMFI Measures Credit Risk Value (CRV)

The scheme's Credit Risk Value (CRV) is the weighted average credit score of its debt holdings, where instrument weights are assigned based on credit ratings:

Sovereign / G-Sec / AAA: Score = 13
AA+ Rating: Score = 12
AA Rating: Score = 11
AA- Rating: Score = 10
A+ Rating: Score = 9
A Rating: Score = 8
BBB+ Rating: Score = 6
Unrated / Lower: Score < 4
Ceiling Rule for Fund Managers

The declared PRC cell sets the maximum risk boundary. A fund manager is allowed to run lower duration or higher credit quality (i.e. safer), but can NEVER exceed the maximum duration or lower credit quality of the cell.

Shifting Cell = Fundamental Attribute Change

Any change in the PRC matrix cell that increases the scheme's potential risk ceiling constitutes a Fundamental Attribute Change under SEBI regulations. It requires Trustee approval, SEBI approval, and a 30-day exit window without exit load for existing unitholders.

Section 10.6 • Super High Exam Yield ⭐
Liquidity Controls

Restriction on Redemptions (Gating Framework)

To prevent runs on the fund and avoid distress fire-sales during systemic black swan crises, SEBI allows AMCs to restrict redemptions under stringent regulatory safeguards.

Permissible Situations (Systemic Only) Exceptional

Gating can ONLY be invoked during extraordinary, market-wide systemic distress:

  • Liquidity Crisis: Market-wide liquidity crunch affecting the financial system as a whole (not just an individual scheme).
  • Market Disruptions: Stock exchanges, bond trading platforms, or clearing corporations shutting down or suspending operations.
  • Operational Blackouts / Force Majeure: Acts of war, pandemic lockouts, clearing grid breakdown, catastrophic cyber disruption.
Strictly Prohibited Grounds Illegal

SEBI explicitly prohibits AMCs from restricting redemptions for reasons attributable to the AMC or scheme itself:

  • CANNOT be imposed due to poor performance of the fund manager.
  • CANNOT be imposed due to scheme-specific asset-liability mismatches.
  • CANNOT be imposed to prevent redemptions following credit rating downgrades in the portfolio.
Mandatory Approval & Maximum Duration
  • Must obtain prior approval from the Board of the AMC.
  • Must obtain prior approval from the Board of Trustees.
  • Must be immediately communicated to SEBI with full justification.
  • Statutory Duration Cap: Permitted for a maximum of 10 working days in any 90-day period.
Mandatory Retail Exemption Threshold

To safeguard retail investors from being locked out of their emergency liquidity:

NO restriction can be placed on redemptions up to ₹2,00,000 per folio!

For redemption requests exceeding ₹2 Lakhs, the first ₹2 Lakhs is processed immediately without restriction, and gating applies strictly to the remaining balance.

Worked Exam Scenario (Application of ₹2 Lakh Rule):

During a systemic liquidity event where gating is approved, an investor submits a redemption request for ₹12,00,000 from their folio. How is this processed?

• First ₹2,00,000: Redeemed immediately at applicable NAV without gating.
• Remaining ₹10,00,000: Subject to gating / redemption restriction.
Section 10.7 • Super High Exam Yield ⭐
Credit Distress Mechanism

Segregated Portfolio (Side-Pocketing) Framework

Side-pocketing was instituted by SEBI following major corporate debt defaults (IL&FS, DHFL) to prevent runs on debt schemes and ensure fair treatment between existing unitholders, redeeming unitholders, and incoming investors.

Trigger 1: Rated Debt Securities CRA Downgrade

Credit event is triggered when a rated debt or money market instrument is downgraded to below investment grade by a SEBI-registered Credit Rating Agency (CRA).

Downgrade to BB+ or lower (including default rating 'D').

Note: A downgrade from AAA to AA or AA to BBB is NOT a trigger, because BBB- is still investment grade.

Trigger 2: Unrated Debt Securities Actual Default

Since unrated debt instruments have no formal credit rating to downgrade, the trigger is:

Actual default in payment of interest or principal on scheduled due date.

Upon default, AMC must immediately inform AMFI and SEBI and proceed with segregation procedures.

Step-by-Step Creation & Operational Workflow
1. Enabling Provision

The scheme must have an enabling side-pocketing clause in its Scheme Information Document (SID).

2. 2-Day Approvals

AMC must decide to segregate within 2 working days and obtain Trustee Board approval within 2 working days.

3. Portfolio Split

Scheme is split into Main Portfolio (good assets) and Segregated Portfolio (distressed paper). Pro-rata units allotted to existing unitholders.

4. 10-Day Listing

Units of segregated portfolio must be listed on a recognized stock exchange within 10 working days for secondary liquidity.

No Subscriptions / Redemptions

The segregated portfolio is completely closed for purchases and redemptions. Investors who want to exit must sell their units on the stock exchange.

0% AMC Management Fees

The AMC is strictly prohibited from charging any investment management fee on the segregated portfolio. TER is capped strictly to legal/recovery costs.

100% Pro-Rata Recovery

Any money recovered later (through coupon payment, sale of collateral, or NCLT resolution) is distributed pro-rata to unitholders of the segregated portfolio.
