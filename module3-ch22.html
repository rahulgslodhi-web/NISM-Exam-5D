Core Foundations

Market Participants in Interest Rate Derivatives

The participants in Exchange Traded Interest Rate Derivatives (ETIRD) are broadly classified into three categories based on their commercial exposure, risk profile, and trading objectives:

1. Hedgers

Real Commercial Exposure: Own balance sheet exposure to interest rate fluctuations (e.g. mutual funds holding bonds, banks, primary dealers, insurers, or corporate debt issuers).

Primary Objective: Neutralize or transfer price risk to lock in portfolio valuation or fix future borrowing / investment yields.

2. Speculators / Traders

No Commercial Exposure: Assume interest rate risk voluntarily by taking directional views on yield curve movements.

Vital Market Function: Act as counterparties to hedgers and inject essential liquidity and trading depth into the exchange order books.

3. Arbitrageurs

Lock in Risk-Free Spreads: Continuously identify price discrepancies between cash and derivatives markets, or across contracts.

Market Function: Simultaneously enter offsetting positions to capture mispricings without directional risk, enforcing price discovery equilibrium.

22.2.1

Short Hedge: Protecting Existing Bond Portfolios Against Rising Yields

A fixed-income portfolio holder is long on debt securities and naturally short on interest rates. When interest rates rise, bond prices fall, triggering mark-to-market (MTM) losses. To protect against this price decline, the investor executes a Short Hedge by selling bond futures contracts. Any MTM decline on the physical bonds is cushioned by gains on the short futures position.

22.2.2

Portfolio-Based Hedging & Duration-Based Hedge Ratio ⭐

In reality, institutional investors manage diversified bond portfolios consisting of multiple securities with varying maturities and coupons. Standardized IRF contracts are available only on select benchmark G-Secs. To immunize the portfolio or alter its duration, managers calculate the Duration-Based Hedge Ratio:

Mathematical Foundation & Formulas NISM Workbook Core Equation

To neutralize portfolio sensitivity to small yield changes ($\Delta V_P + N \cdot \Delta V_F = 0$), where $\Delta V \approx -MD \cdot V \cdot \Delta y$:

$$HR = \frac{MD_{\text{portfolio}} \times V_{\text{portfolio}}}{MD_{\text{ctd}} \times P_{\text{ctd}}}$$
Formula for Number of Futures Contracts (N):
$$N = \frac{MD_P \times V_P}{MD_F \times P_F \times \text{Lot Size}}$$

Where \(MD_P\) = Portfolio Modified Duration, \(V_P\) = Portfolio Market Value, \(MD_F\) = Futures Modified Duration, \(P_F\) = Futures Price per unit, and \(\text{Lot Size} = 2,000\) units (Face value ₹2 Lakhs per contract).

Textbook Numerical Problem (Exam Favorite) Step-by-Step Solution

Question: An investor holds a ₹26.00 Crore portfolio of GOI bonds with a portfolio modified duration of 6.1. The 1-month GOI bond futures contract duration is 4.7 and trades at ₹98.50 (Lot size = 2,000 units). How many futures contracts are needed to fully hedge the portfolio?

Step 1: Calculate Numerator (Dollar Duration of Portfolio)
= 26,00,00,000 × 6.1 = 1,58,60,00,000
Step 2: Calculate Denominator (Dollar Duration of 1 Futures Contract)
= 98.50 × 4.7 × 2,000 = 9,25,900
Step 3: Solve for N = \(\frac{1,586,000,000}{925,900} = 1,712.92 \approx\) 1,713 Lots
Target Duration Formula (Altering Duration):

If instead of full immunization (target duration = 0), the manager wants to adjust portfolio duration from \(MD_P\) to a target \(MD_T\):

$$N = \frac{(MD_T - MD_P) \times V_P}{MD_F \times P_F \times \text{Lot Size}}$$

If \(MD_T < MD_P\), \(N\) is negative (sell futures to shorten duration). If \(MD_T > MD_P\), \(N\) is positive (buy futures to lengthen duration).

Convexity Limitation: Duration is a linear first-order approximation valid strictly for marginal yield changes. For substantial yield shifts, the non-linear curvature (Convexity) causes actual bond prices to deviate from duration estimates, generating basis risk.

Hedging Future Borrowings

Corporate Need: A company plans to issue 10-year NCDs after 1 month at a spread of 50 bps over G-Secs. It faces the risk of rising interest rates before the issuance.

• Hedge Action: Sells IRF contracts on Aug 14 @ ₹95.60 (Yield 7.93%).
• At Settlement (Sep 24): FSP drops to ₹94.80 (Yield rises to 8.08%).
• Derivatives Gain = ₹0.80 per bond (95.60 - 94.80).

The ₹0.80 gain on IRF offsets the higher coupon payable on the newly issued NCDs, locking in the borrowing cost. (Note: Corporate credit spread widening remains an unhedged basis risk).

22.2.4

Long Hedge: Locking In Yields for Cash Inflows

Institutional Need: A life insurance company expects a ₹200 Crore premium inflow or bond maturity in late March. It fears interest rates will decline (bond prices surge) before the cash can be deployed.

• Hedge Action: Buys 10,000 lots of 6.45% GOI 2029 IRF @ ₹99.60 (Yield 6.51%).
• At Expiry: Bond price surges to ₹101.58 (Yield drops to 6.23%).
• Derivatives Profit = (101.58 - 99.60) × 10,000 × 2,000 = ₹3.96 Crore!

The ₹3.96 Cr profit cushions the lower yield at which the ₹200 Cr cash must now be invested in physical bonds. If rates rose instead (price fell to ₹99.00), futures loss would be ₹1.20 Cr (cost of protection).

Spreads & Exotic Payoffs

Option Trading Strategies on Bonds

Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options (ETIRO) allow tailored asymmetric payoff structures. Spreads combine options on the identical underlying GOI security: Vertical Spreads have the same expiry with different strikes; Horizontal (Calendar) Spreads have the same strike with different expiries; and Diagonal Spreads differ across both strikes and expiries.

Vertical Spreads Matrix (Calls & Puts)

Bullish Vertical Spread (Using Calls) Debit Spread

Legs: Buy Lower Strike Call (\(X_1\)) + Sell Higher Strike Call (\(X_2\)).

• Textbook Setup: Buy 98.75 Call @ 0.45, Sell 99.50 Call @ 0.20.
• Net Premium Paid (Max Loss): 0.45 - 0.20 = ₹0.25.
• Max Profit: (99.50 - 98.75) - 0.25 = ₹0.50 (at ≥ ₹99.50).
• BEP: Lower Strike + Net Debit = 98.75 + 0.25 = ₹99.00.
Bullish Vertical Spread (Using Puts) Credit Spread

Legs: Sell Higher Strike Put (\(X_2\)) + Buy Lower Strike Put (\(X_1\)).

• Textbook Setup: Sell 99.50 Put @ 0.40, Buy 99.00 Put @ 0.10.
• Net Premium Received (Max Profit): 0.40 - 0.10 = ₹0.30.
• Max Loss: (99.50 - 99.00) - 0.30 = ₹0.20 (at ≤ ₹99.00).
• BEP: Higher Strike - Net Credit = 99.50 - 0.30 = ₹99.20.
Bearish Vertical Spread (Using Calls) Credit Spread

Legs: Sell Lower Strike Call (\(X_1\)) + Buy Higher Strike Call (\(X_2\)).

• Textbook Setup: Sell 98.50 Call @ 0.60, Buy 99.25 Call @ 0.20.
• Net Premium Received (Max Profit): 0.60 - 0.20 = ₹0.40.
• Max Loss: (99.25 - 98.50) - 0.40 = ₹0.35 (at ≥ ₹99.25).
• BEP: Lower Strike + Net Credit = 98.50 + 0.40 = ₹98.90.
Bearish Vertical Spread (Using Puts) Debit Spread

Legs: Buy Higher Strike Put (\(X_2\)) + Sell Lower Strike Put (\(X_1\)).

• Textbook Setup: Buy 99.50 Put @ 0.40, Sell 99.00 Put @ 0.25.
• Net Premium Paid (Max Loss): 0.40 - 0.25 = ₹0.15.
• Max Profit: (99.50 - 99.00) - 0.15 = ₹0.35 (at ≤ ₹99.00).
• BEP: Higher Strike - Net Debit = 99.50 - 0.15 = ₹99.35.

Volatility Plays: Straddles & Strangles on Bonds

Straddle (Same Strike, Same Expiry)

Textbook Setup: Strike ₹99.00. Call Prem = 0.30, Put Prem = 0.20. Combined Prem = ₹0.50.

Long Straddle (Buy ATM Call + Buy ATM Put):
• Market Outlook: High volatility expected (major rate event).
• Max Loss: ₹0.50 (if bond expires exactly at ₹99.00).
• Max Profit: Unlimited in either direction.
• BEPs: Lower = \(99.00 - 0.50 = \mathbf{98.50}\); Upper = \(99.00 + 0.50 = \mathbf{99.50}\).
Short Straddle (Sell ATM Call + Sell ATM Put):
• Market Outlook: Stable / range-bound market (yields stay flat).
• Max Profit: ₹0.50 (if bond expires at ₹99.00).
• Max Loss: Unlimited in either direction beyond BEPs.
Strangle (Different Strikes, OTM, Same Expiry)

Textbook Setup: Buy 99.25 Call @ 0.20 + Buy 98.75 Put @ 0.15. Total Prem = ₹0.35.

Long Strangle (Buy OTM Call + Buy OTM Put):
• Market Outlook: Massive breakout expected at lower premium outlay than straddle.
• Max Loss: ₹0.35 (if bond expires anywhere between ₹98.75 and ₹99.25).
• BEPs: Lower = \(98.75 - 0.35 = \mathbf{98.40}\); Upper = \(99.25 + 0.35 = \mathbf{99.60}\).
Short Strangle (Sell OTM Call + Sell OTM Put):
• Market Outlook: Bond price remains safely within the 98.75 - 99.25 corridor.
• Max Profit: ₹0.35 (between 98.75 and 99.25).
• Max Loss: Unlimited beyond 98.40 or 99.60.

Synthetic Positions: Covered Call vs. Protective Put

Covered Call on Bonds Synthetic Short Put

Strategy: Buy/hold underlying GOI bond at ₹99.50 + Sell OTM Call at strike ₹100.00 @ ₹0.10 premium.

• Purpose: Generate additional yield on long-term bond holdings during flat/moderately bullish phases.
• Max Profit: (100.00 - 99.50) + 0.10 = ₹0.60 (upside is capped at ₹100.10).
• Downside Risk: Substantial if bond crashes (cushioned by ₹0.10 fee). BEP = ₹99.40.
• Arbitrage Implication: If a 100 Put trades at 0.50, trader creates synthetic short put for 0.60 and buys the cash put for 0.50, locking ₹0.10 risk-free profit!
Protective Put on Bonds Synthetic Long Call

Strategy: Buy/hold underlying GOI bond at ₹99.50 + Buy ATM Put at strike ₹99.50 @ ₹0.20 premium.

• Purpose: Put a firm floor under portfolio losses while maintaining 100% upside participation if rates fall.
• Max Loss: Strictly capped at the put premium = ₹0.20 (regardless of bond crash severity).
• Max Profit: Unlimited as bond price surges.
• BEP: 99.50 + 0.20 = ₹99.70.

Section 22.4 • Directional & Duration Management
Portfolio Structuring

Speculative Trading & Altering Portfolio Duration

Unlike hedgers who offset pre-existing balance sheet risks, speculators enter positions voluntarily to capitalize on expected interest rate fluctuations. Active fund managers also deploy bond futures to swiftly and cost-effectively adjust portfolio duration without disturbing underlying physical bond portfolios.

Bullish Directional Trading (Yields Falling)

When expecting interest rates to drop (bond prices to appreciate), a trader can choose between:

  • Buy Physical Bonds: Requires 100% upfront capital (₹5 Lakhs buys ₹5 Lakhs face value). Returns are capped to physical price increase + accrued coupon.
  • Buy IRF Contracts (20x Leverage): With a 5% margin, ₹5 Lakhs controls ₹1.00 Crore notional value, multiplying capital returns (e.g. ₹97,000 profit vs ₹6,600 on physical bond).
  • Buy IRF + Invest Surplus in CDs: Unleveraged approach where ₹5 Lakhs cash is parked in 24-day Certificates of Deposit (earning ₹2,300), generating total returns of ₹7,150.

Bearish View & The Physical Short-Sale Ban

In India, RBI regulations prohibit retail investors and most institutional participants from naked short selling of Government of India securities in the physical cash market.

The ETIRD Advantage: Exchange-traded bond derivatives provide the only seamless, transparent, and legally accessible vehicle to express a bearish view by either selling GOI Bond Futures or buying GOI Bond Put Options without needing physical bond borrowing.

Dynamic Portfolio Duration Alteration

Fund managers who develop strong convictions on monetary policy actions adjust their portfolio duration to maximize performance:

Lengthening Duration (Bullish on Yields)

When yields are expected to decline, the manager buys GOI bond futures. This magnifies the portfolio's capital gain without selling short-dated liquid paper or purchasing illiquid long-dated cash bonds.

Shortening Duration (Bearish on Yields)

When yields are expected to spike, the manager sells GOI bond futures. This compresses effective duration toward zero or desired benchmark levels, immunizing against price declines without paying secondary market illiquidity haircuts.

Cash-and-Carry & Synthetics

Arbitrage Transactions & Creating Synthetic Securities

Arbitrageurs enforce market efficiency by simultaneously buying and selling mispriced instruments across spot and derivative markets. Crucially, their profits are locked in at trade inception, carrying zero directional exposure to interest rate shifts.

22.5.1 Regular Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage

Futures Overpriced

Condition: Futures market price exceeds theoretical carry price (\(F > S_0 + \text{Carry} - \text{Coupon}\)).

• Action: Buy bond in spot market @ ₹102.60 + Borrow funds in Repo @ 5% + Sell IRF @ ₹102.62 (500 lots, ₹10 Cr FV).
Payoff at Expiry (Bond at ₹102.30 vs ₹102.80):
If Price = ₹102.30: Accrued ₹4.42L + IRF Gain ₹3.20L - Bond Loss ₹3.00L - Repo Cost ₹2.95L = +₹167,599
If Price = ₹102.80: Accrued ₹4.42L + Bond Gain ₹2.00L - IRF Loss ₹1.80L - Repo Cost ₹2.95L = +₹167,599

Net payoff is identically positive at ₹167,599 in both price outcomes!

22.5.2 Reverse Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage

Futures Underpriced

Condition: Futures market price trades below theoretical fair value (\(F < S_0 + \text{Carry} - \text{Coupon}\)).

• Action: Sell bond in spot market @ ₹102.60 + Lend money in Repo @ 5% + Buy IRF @ ₹102.40 (500 lots, ₹10 Cr FV).
Payoff at Expiry (Bond at ₹102.30 vs ₹102.80):
If Price = ₹102.30: Bond Profit ₹3.00L + Repo Interest ₹2.95L - Accrued ₹4.42L - IRF Loss ₹1.00L = +₹52,401
If Price = ₹102.80: IRF Profit ₹4.00L + Repo Interest ₹2.95L - Accrued ₹4.42L - Bond Loss ₹2.00L = +₹52,401

Net payoff is identically positive at ₹52,401 in both price outcomes!

Section 22.5.3 • Synthetic Money Market Securities High Exam Yield

Synthetic 3-Month T-Bills for Yield Enhancement

Consider an institutional investor who wishes to invest surplus liquidity for 3 months. Instead of buying a plain 3-month Treasury Bill in the secondary cash market:

Synthetic 3M T-Bill = Long Position in 15-Year GOI Bond + Short Position in 3-Month Bond Futures

How it works: The investor purchases a high-yielding 15-year GOI bond in the spot market and simultaneously shorts 3-month bond futures contracts on that bond. Because the futures contract locks in the precise selling price at the end of 3 months, the effective maturity of the 15-year bond collapses to exactly 3 months with zero residual price volatility.

Yield Pick-Up / Enhancement: Whenever the implied yield of the synthetic T-Bill exceeds the prevailing spot market yield of cash 91-Day T-Bills, fund managers exploit this structure to earn superior risk-free returns.

Real-World Arbitrage Risks & Frictions (Textbook Warning)

In theory, arbitrage is risk-free. In practice, arbitrageurs face substantial operational risks:

1. Leg Execution Risk: If one leg executes and the other experiences illiquidity or price movement, the trader gets stuck with naked exposure.
2. Regulatory Hurdles: Reverse cash-and-carry requires short selling physical bonds, which is prohibited for mutual funds and retail entities.
3. Transaction & Impact Costs: Brokerage, repo haircuts, margin costs, and NDS-OM market depth can easily consume small basis profits.
Section 22.6 • Advanced ETIRD Trading
Spread Mechanisms

Trading Spreads using ETIRD

Spread trading exploits relative price differences rather than outright market direction. Traders take simultaneous long and short positions across different contract months (time spreads) or different underlying bonds (yield curve spreads).

22.6.1 Basis Trading (Spot vs. Futures Basis)

Basis is defined as: \(\text{Basis} = \text{Spot Clean Price} - \text{Futures Clean Price}\). At expiry, the basis must mathematically converge to zero (\(\text{Basis}_T \to 0\)).

Long the Basis (Basis Widening Expected):

Buy underlying G-Sec in spot market + Sell Bond Futures. Used when futures are overpriced relative to cash. As basis widens or converges to fair carry, the trade generates profit.

Short the Basis (Basis Narrowing Expected):

Sell underlying G-Sec in spot market + Buy Bond Futures. Used when futures are underpriced relative to cash. Profits as the discounted futures price catches up to cash.

22.6.2 Calendar Spread (Time Spread)

Involves simultaneously buying a contract of one expiry month and selling the exact same contract of a different expiry month (e.g. Near-Month vs. Mid-Month) on the same underlying bond.

Textbook Example: Sell Sep 6.10% GOI 2031 IRF @ ₹99.00 and Buy Oct IRF @ ₹99.05 (initial spread = ₹0.05).
Later, Sep trades at ₹98.80 and Oct trades at ₹98.95 (spread widens to ₹0.15).
Net Gain = (0.15 - 0.05) × 2,000 = ₹200 per lot!

Exchange Benefits: Exchanges levy significantly lower calendar spread margins and offer a dedicated Spread Order Book that executes both legs simultaneously, eliminating execution risk.

22.6.3 Inter-Bond Spreads (Yield Curve Plays)

Simultaneous long and short futures positions on two different underlying GOI securities with the same contract expiry:

• Intra-Bucket Spread: Buy 6.10% GOI 2031 and Sell 5.85% GOI 2030 (captures on-the-run liquidity premium within the 10Y bucket).
• Inter-Bucket Spread (Yield Curve Trading):
Steepener Trade: Buy 5Y Bond Futures + Sell 10Y Bond Futures (profits when yield curve steepens & 5Y yields drop faster).
Flattener Trade: Buy 10Y Bond Futures + Sell 5Y Bond Futures (profits when yield curve flattens & long-term yields fall relative to short-term).
Section 22.7 • ⭐ Super High Exam Yield
Hedger Risks & Shortcomings

Limitations of ETIRD for Hedgers

While ETIRDs offer exchange-guaranteed settlement, transparency, and liquidity, hedgers face fundamental structural limitations that prevent perfect hedges:

1. Basis Risk (Core Tested Concept ⭐)

Standardized single-bond IRFs rarely move in perfect 1:1 correlation with an investor's custom debt portfolio. Fluctuations in the spot-futures basis and non-linear convexity divergence for large yield shifts create hedging slippage.

2. Maturity Date Mismatch

GOI Bond Futures contracts expire strictly on the last Thursday of the contract month. If the hedger's physical bond maturity, corporate cash outflow, or liability date occurs on any other calendar day, an unavoidable maturity timing mismatch arises.

3. Non-Availability on Corporate Debt & SDLs

ETIRDs exist almost exclusively on benchmark Central Government dated securities. Hedgers holding corporate debt, State Development Loans (SDLs), or retail housing loans cannot achieve a perfect hedge because corporate credit spreads fluctuate independently of sovereign benchmark yields.

4. Yield Curve Reshaping Risk

Duration-based hedging relies on the assumption of parallel yield curve shifts. In reality, yield curves experience non-parallel reshaping (steepening, flattening, or twists). A portfolio with varying tenors will suffer residual losses when curve slopes twist.

5. Cash Settlement Timing Slippage & Liquidity Divergence

ETIRDs in India are settled in cash based on the weighted average NDS-OM price of the last two trading hours. Execution price slippage and timing lags between unwinding exchange-traded contracts and executing bulk block trades in the OTC cash debt market can generate small value erosions.

Why Hedgers Still Rely on ETIRD Despite Limitations:
1. Zero Counterparty Credit Risk: Guaranteed novation and daily MTM settlement by the Exchange Clearing Corporation.
2. High Transparency & Discovery: Centralized nationwide electronic limit order books provide real-time price discovery.
3. Low Entry Barriers: Standardized small lot size (₹2 Lakhs face value) enables granular risk calibration.
