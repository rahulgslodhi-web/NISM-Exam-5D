Section 5.1 • Fast-Track Refresher
Legal Architecture

Mandatory Scheme Documents & Caveat Emptor

Mutual fund investments operate under the common law principle of Caveat Emptor ("Let the buyer beware"). Investors are legally presumed to have read, understood, and consented to the disclosures made in the scheme offer documents. In court or before ombudsmen, an investor cannot claim ignorance if a risk factor or fee was explicitly disclosed in these statutory documents.

1. SID

Scheme Information Document: Scheme-specific charter detailing investment objective, asset allocation, investment strategies, risk factors, fees, and fund managers.

2. SAI

Statement of Additional Info: Fund-level handbook with statutory disclosures common across all schemes: sponsors, trustees, AMC, custodians, legal disputes, and valuation policies.

3. KIM

Key Information Memorandum: Abridged summary of SID + SAI. By law under SEBI regulations, every application form must be accompanied by a valid KIM.

4. Addendum

Notice cum Addendum: Legal instrument used to formally notify unitholders of interim material changes between periodic statutory updation cycles of SID, SAI, and KIM.

Legal Equation: Complete Offer Document = SID + SAI + all applicable Addenda. KIM serves as the concise point-of-sale summary for prospective investors.

SEBI 21-Working-Day Filing Rule (Draft SID):

When a new scheme is conceived, the AMC files a Draft SID with SEBI along with a non-refundable filing fee. SEBI reviews the draft and provides written comments/observations within 21 working days. The AMC must incorporate all SEBI comments before filing the final SID and launching the New Fund Offer (NFO).

Due Diligence Certificate:

Along with the Draft SID, the AMC must submit a formal Due Diligence Certificate signed by the Compliance Officer, CEO, MD, or Executive Director certifying that all SEBI regulations, disclosures, guidelines, and legal prerequisites have been verified and strictly complied with.

Timelines & Milestones

Document Updation Timelines & Lifecycle

Regulatory disclosures must remain fresh, accurate, and reflect current market and regulatory realities. SEBI has established strict calendar-based deadlines for updating each document:

SID Updation Twice a Year

Updated within 1 month from the end of each half-year:

  • By April 30: Based on March 31 data
  • By October 31: Based on Sept 30 data
SAI Updation ⭐ Annual Cycle

Updated regularly within 3 months from the end of the financial year:

  • By June 30: Comprehensive annual statutory update
  • Ongoing basis: Immediate updates for material changes via addenda
KIM Updation Half-Yearly

Updated at least once in half-year within 1 month from end of the half-year:

  • By April 30 & October 31
  • Filed electronically with SEBI and uploaded on the AMC website
Memory Anchor for the Exam: Remember the "3-1-1 Rule":
• SAI: 3 months after financial year end (June 30).
• SID: 1 month after each half-year (April 30 and October 31).
• KIM: 1 month after each half-year (April 30 and October 31).

⭐ Reg 22(e) & Exit Load Rules

Fundamental Attribute Changes & 30-Day Exit Option

Under Regulation 22(e) of the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996, the fundamental attributes of a scheme represent its core investment contract. No change in the fundamental attributes can be executed without following strict statutory protocols and protecting unitholder rights.

What Constitutes a "Fundamental Attribute"?
1. Type of Scheme Open-ended, Close-ended, or Interval scheme; equity fund, debt fund, hybrid fund, or solution-oriented fund.
2. Investment Objective Capital appreciation, regular income; core asset allocation pattern (e.g., shifting equity allocation from 65-100% to 20-50%); core investment strategy.
3. Terms of Issue Liquidity provisions (listing, repurchase, redemption frequency); maximum aggregate recurring expenses (TER) charged to the scheme.

Statutory 5-Step Procedure for Making Fundamental Changes

1
Trustee & AMC Board Approval
2
Prior SEBI Clearance / No-Objection
3

⭐ 30 Calendar Days Exit Window
The 30 Calendar Days Free Exit Window (Crucial Exam Rule)

Whenever a fundamental attribute change is proposed, existing unitholders who disagree with the revision must be given an exit option of at least 30 calendar days to redeem their units at the prevailing Net Asset Value (NAV) WITHOUT payment of any exit load.

Exemption / Non-applicability: In case of scheme mergers, unitholders of the surviving/transferee scheme do not get an exit window if its own fundamental attributes remain unaltered. Unitholders of the merging/transferor scheme, however, always get the 30-day exit window.
Notice cum Addendum vs. Fundamental Attribute Change
Notice cum Addendum (Non-Fundamental) Used for routine modifications: change in fund manager, change in key personnel, opening/closing of official points of acceptance (OPAs), minor fee adjustments within permissible caps, change in benchmark index within the approved category. No 30-day exit window required.
Fundamental Attribute Change (Reg 22(e)) Modifies the core investment objective, asset allocation bands, fund category, or liquidity structure. Mandatory 30-calendar-day exit window at NAV with zero exit load. Prior SEBI approval required.

How the Portfolio Risk Score is Calculated

Debt Portfolio Risk Calculation

Debt securities risk score is calculated as the simple average of three risk parameters:

  • Credit Risk Value (CRV): AAA = 1, AA+ = 2, down to Below BBB- / Unrated = 12.
  • Interest Rate Risk (Duration): Macaulay duration ≤ 0.5 yr = 1, up to > 4 yrs = 6.
  • Liquidity Risk: Scored based on listing, credit rating, issue size, and secondary market turnover.
Debt Score = (Credit Score + Duration Score + Liquidity Score) / 3
Equity Portfolio Risk Calculation

Equity securities risk score is calculated as the simple average of three risk parameters:

  • Market Capitalization: Large cap = 5, Mid cap = 7, Small cap = 9.
  • Volatility: 2-year daily annualized volatility (≤15% = 5, up to >45% = 9).
  • Impact Cost (Liquidity): Average impact cost (≤1% = 5, 1-2% = 7, >2% = 9).
Equity Score = (Market Cap Score + Volatility Score + Impact Cost Score) / 3
Final Scheme Risk Score & Cash Allocation:

The overall scheme score is the weighted average of the risk scores of all individual assets (debt, equity, derivatives, cash). Cash and cash equivalents (TREPS, reverse repo, bank balances) are assigned the lowest risk score of 1.0.

⭐ 3x3 Debt Matrix

Potential Risk Class (PRC) Matrix for Debt Schemes

Introduced by SEBI (effective December 1, 2021), the Potential Risk Class (PRC) Matrix defines the maximum risk boundaries within which a debt mutual fund scheme is permitted to operate. While the Risk-o-meter shows current portfolio risk, the PRC matrix shows the maximum potential risk the fund manager is legally allowed to take.

Strict Rules Regarding PRC Matrix Compliance:
  • Mandatory Disclosure: The PRC cell must be prominently displayed in the SID, KIM, Factsheet, and Common Application Form.
  • Hard Boundary: The actual portfolio duration and credit risk score can never exceed the maximum ceiling defined by the cell.
  • Breach Curing Period: If a portfolio inadvertently breaches its PRC cell, the AMC has a 30-day curing period to restore compliance.
  • Change in PRC Cell = Fundamental Attribute Change: If an AMC wishes to permanently alter its PRC cell, it is treated as a Fundamental Attribute Change under Regulation 22(e), requiring prior SEBI approval, Trustee approval, and a 30-calendar-day exit window at NAV with zero exit load!

Alternative Vehicles

Specialized Investment Funds (SIF) 5-Level Risk-Band

Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs) invest in non-traditional asset classes, utilize complex financial derivatives, maintain short positions, or employ leverage. Applying the retail 6-level Risk-o-meter to SIFs could confuse retail unitholders. Therefore, SEBI mandated a distinct 5-Level Risk-Band for SIFs.

Assignment & Disclosure Timelines:

Assigned at the time of New Fund Offer (NFO). Evaluated on a monthly basis and published on the AMC website within 10 days from the close of each month, as well as on March 31.

Portfolio Disclosures for SIFs:

Published on the AMC and AMFI websites for every alternate month (May, July, September, November, January, March) within 10 days of the month-end.

The Fund Factsheet: Non-Statutory Voluntary Best Practice ⭐

The monthly Fund Factsheet is NOT a statutory or mandatory document under SEBI regulations. It is published voluntarily by mutual funds as an industry best practice under AMFI guidelines, typically within 10 days of month-end.

Equity Scheme Metrics: Portfolio P/E, Price to Book (P/B), Dividend Yield, Top 10 holdings, Sector weights, Standard Deviation, Beta, Sharpe Ratio.
Debt Scheme Metrics: Yield to Maturity (YTM), Modified Duration, Macaulay Duration, Average Maturity, Credit Rating Profile, PRC Matrix cell.
