Options Basics

Option Contracts Fundamentals & Payoffs

Understanding asymmetry: Rights vs. Obligations, European exercise style, and the four fundamental payoff profiles.

Call Option (Right to Buy)

Grants the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to purchase the underlying asset at the predetermined Strike Price (X) on or before expiry.

Buyer's Bias: Bullish. Max Loss = Premium Paid. Max Profit = Unlimited.
Put Option (Right to Sell)

Grants the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying asset at the predetermined Strike Price (X) on or before expiry.

Buyer's Bias: Bearish. Max Loss = Premium Paid. Max Profit = Strike Price - Premium.
Indian Exchange Regulatory Specifications (SEBI / NSE / BSE)
European Exercise Only All exchange-traded equity & index options in India are European Style (designated as CE for Call European and PE for Put European). Exercisable only on expiration date.
Index Options Settlement Nifty 50, Nifty Bank, and other index options settle strictly in Cash against the closing index price on expiry day.
Stock Options Settlement All in-the-money (ITM) single-stock option contracts open at expiry are subject to Compulsory Physical Delivery of actual underlying equity shares.

The 4 Foundational Payoff Profiles

Position Market View Max Profit Max Loss Breakeven Point Payoff Formula
Long Call (Buyer) Bullish (↑) Unlimited Premium Paid Strike + Premium (X + C) max(0, ST - X) - C
Short Call (Writer) Bearish / Neutral (↓) Premium Received Unlimited Strike + Premium (X + C) C - max(0, ST - X)
Long Put (Buyer) Bearish (↓) Strike - Premium (X - P) Premium Paid Strike - Premium (X - P) max(0, X - ST) - P

The Moneyness Matrix: ITM, ATM, and OTM

Moneyness reflects the economic payoff if the option were exercised immediately at prevailing spot market prices.

Moneyness State Call Option (Right to Buy) Put Option (Right to Sell) Intrinsic Value Delta (Δ) Profile
In-the-Money (ITM)
Spot > Strike (S > X) Spot < Strike (S < X) Positive (> 0) Call: +0.50 to +1.0
Put: -0.50 to -1.0
At-the-Money (ATM)
Spot = Strike (S = X) Spot = Strike (S = X) Zero (0) Call: ≈ +0.50
Put: ≈ -0.50

Spot < Strike (S < X) Spot > Strike (S > X) Zero (0) Call: 0.0 to +0.50

Mathematical Formulation

Intrinsic Value vs. Time Value (Extrinsic Value)

Decomposing the market price of an option into its intrinsic worth and time decay component.

Fundamental Pricing Identity
Total Option Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value (Extrinsic Value)
Call Intrinsic Value Formula:
IVCall = max(0, Spot - Strike) = max(0, S - X)

If S ≤ X, Intrinsic Value is strictly ₹0.00. It can NEVER be negative!

Put Intrinsic Value Formula:
IVPut = max(0, Strike - Spot) = max(0, X - S)

If S ≥ X, Intrinsic Value is strictly ₹0.00. It can NEVER be negative!

Worked Numerical Problems (Exam Question Formats)

Example 1: Call Option Valuation

Reliance Industries spot is trading at ₹2,950. The 2,900 Call option is quoted at a premium of ₹85.

• Intrinsic Value = max(0, S - X)
= max(0, 2,950 - 2,900) = ₹50.00
• Time Value = Premium - Intrinsic Value
= 85 - 50 = ₹35.00

✓ Option is ITM with ₹50 tangible value and ₹35 extrinsic value.

Example 2: Put Option Valuation

TCS spot is trading at ₹3,800. The 3,900 Put option is quoted at a premium of ₹140.

• Intrinsic Value = max(0, X - S)
= max(0, 3,900 - 3,800) = ₹100.00
• Time Value = Premium - Intrinsic Value
= 140 - 100 = ₹40.00

✓ Option is ITM with ₹100 tangible value and ₹40 extrinsic value.

Exam Trap Alert: Out-of-the-Money Options

If Infosys spot is at ₹1,750, a 1,800 Call trading at ₹18 has an Intrinsic Value of ₹0.00 (not -₹50). Its Time Value is the entire premium of ₹18.00. The premium of any OTM or ATM option consists 100% of Time Value!

BSM & Volatility

Determinants of Option Prices, BSM Model & Volatility

The Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) analytical model, directional sensitivity, and volatility dynamics.

Directional Impact of Key Input Variables

Input Variable Change in Variable Impact on Call Price Impact on Put Price Financial Rationale
Underlying Spot Price (S) Increases (↑) Increases (↑) Decreases (↓) Calls gain intrinsic value; Puts lose intrinsic value.
Strike Price (X) Increases (↑) Decreases (↓) Increases (↑) Higher strike reduces call payoff, enhances put payoff.
Time to Expiration (T) Increases (↑) Increases (↑) Increases (↑) More time provides greater opportunity for favorable spot movements.
Implied Volatility (σ) Increases (↑) Increases (↑) Increases (↑) Higher volatility widens expected price distribution. Benefits both!
Risk-Free Rate (r) Increases (↑) Increases (↑) Decreases (↓) Cost of carry: Call delays capital outlay; Put delays receipt of proceeds.
Dividend Yield (q) Increases (↑) Decreases (↓) Increases (↑) Stock price drops ex-dividend on payout date.
Historical vs. Implied Volatility (IV)
  • • Historical Volatility: Backward-looking standard deviation of past stock price returns over a fixed window (e.g. 30 days).
  • • Implied Volatility (IV): Forward-looking volatility expectation priced into current option premiums, extracted using BSM.
  • • India VIX: The volatility index calculated by NSE from the order book of NIFTY options, indicating expected annualized market volatility over the next 30 calendar days.
Volatility Skew (Smirk) vs. Volatility Smile
  • • Volatility Smile: Symmetric U-shape where deep OTM Calls and Puts have higher IV than ATM options (frequently observed in currency options).
  • • Volatility Skew (Smirk): Asymmetric curve common in equity indices (NIFTY) where lower strike OTM Puts have much higher IV than higher strike OTM Calls.
  • • Why Equity Skew Exists: Institutional "crashophobia" driving sustained demand for downside protective puts (tail risk hedging).

Delta (Δ): Direction & Probability Hedge Ratio
  • • Rate of Change: Change in option premium for every ₹1 change in underlying spot price.
  • • Hedge Ratio: To hedge 1 long call with Δ = 0.60 on a 50-share lot, an investor shorts \(0.60 \times 50 = 30\) shares of the underlying stock (Delta-neutral portfolio).
  • • Probability Proxy: An option's absolute delta roughly approximates the probability of the option expiring In-the-Money (ITM). An ATM call (Δ ≈ 0.50) has ≈ 50% probability of expiring ITM.
Gamma (Γ): Acceleration & Curvature Δ Sensitivity
  • • Rate of Change: Change in Delta for every ₹1 change in the underlying spot price.
  • • Gamma Explosion: For ATM options nearing expiration, Gamma spikes dramatically. A tiny spot price fluctuation can rapidly flip Delta from 0.10 to 0.90.
  • • Risk for Writers: Short option sellers face huge negative Gamma near expiry, requiring frequent portfolio re-hedging.
Theta (Θ): The Enemy of Option Buyers Time Decay
  • • Daily Decay: Loss in option premium resulting purely from the passage of one calendar day (holding price and volatility constant).
  • • Non-linear Acceleration: Time decay is relatively slow when months remain until expiry, but accelerates exponentially in the final 30 calendar days.
  • • Calendar Day Effect: Theta erodes over weekends and market holidays too; buyers lose value each passing non-trading day.
Vega (ν): Sensitivity to Volatility Shifts 1% IV Shift
  • • Volatility Impact: Change in option premium for every 1 percentage point (1.0%) change in Implied Volatility (IV).
  • • Long Maturity Bias: Vega is significantly higher for longer-dated options because volatility has more time to expand terminal payoffs.
  • • Volatility Crush: Right after high-profile corporate earnings or Union Budget announcements, IV plunges sharply, causing option buyers to suffer losses even if direction was right.

Arbitrage & Synthetics

Put-Call Parity Theorem & Synthetic Strategies

The fundamental equilibrium relationship connecting Calls, Puts, the underlying Stock, and risk-free Bonds.

The Put-Call Parity Equation

For European options on a non-dividend paying stock with identical strike price (\(X\)) and expiration date (\(T\)):

C + X × e-rT = P + S

In discrete form: C + X / (1 + r·T) = P + S  |  With dividends (PV = D): C + X·e-rT = P + S - D

Theoretical Proof (Portfolio A vs Portfolio B):

• Portfolio A (Fiduciary Call): Buy 1 Call (\(C\)) + Invest \(X\cdot e^{-rT}\) in Zero-Coupon Bond. At expiry, if \(S_T > X\), payoff = \((S_T - X) + X = S_T\). If \(S_T \le X\), payoff = \(0 + X = X\). Terminal value = \(\max(S_T, X)\).

• Portfolio B (Protective Put): Buy 1 Put (\(P\)) + Buy 1 Share of Stock (\(S\)). At expiry, if \(S_T > X\), payoff = \(0 + S_T = S_T\). If \(S_T \le X\), payoff = \((X - S_T) + S_T = X\). Terminal value = \(\max(S_T, X)\).

Since both portfolios produce IDENTICAL terminal payoffs under all possible market conditions, they must have identical initial prices!

Synthetic Positions Generated via Put-Call Parity

Rearranging the parity equation \(S = C - P + X\cdot e^{-rT}\) enables the creation of synthetic equivalents:

Target Synthetic Position Component 1 Component 2 Economic Payoff Profile Exam Application
Synthetic Long Stock Long Call (+C) Short Put (-P) Identical to holding 1 share of cash stock. Creating long equity exposure without paying 100% cash up front.
Synthetic Short Stock Short Call (-C) Long Put (+P) Identical to shorting 1 share of cash stock. Shorting without securities borrowing or delivery friction.
Synthetic Long Call Long Stock (+S) Long Put (+P) Unlimited upside with protected downside (Protective Put). Replicating call payoff when call strikes lack liquidity.
Synthetic Long Put Long Call (+C) Short Stock (-S) Downside profit with limited upside risk. Replicating put protection when borrowing shares.
Conversion Arbitrage (Call Overpriced)

When \(C + X\cdot e^{-rT} > P + S\):

• Sell Call (-C)
• Buy Put (+P)
• Buy Spot Stock (+S)
• Borrow PV(X) at risk-free rate

Locks in a zero-risk arbitrage profit regardless of expiry price.

Reverse Conversion Arbitrage (Put Overpriced)

When \(P + S > C + X\cdot e^{-rT}\):

• Buy Call (+C)
• Sell Put (-P)
• Short Spot Stock (-S)
• Lend proceeds at risk-free rate

Locks in a zero-risk arbitrage profit by buying underpriced synthetics.
