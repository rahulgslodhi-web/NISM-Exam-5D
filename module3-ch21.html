Contract Specifications

Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options (ETIRO) Specifications

Regulatory & Market Background

Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) introduced Exchange Traded Interest Rate Options (ETIRO) on recognized stock exchanges (NSE and BSE) to provide institutional debt market participants and mutual funds with an efficient, transparent, and non-linear risk hedging mechanism.

Contract Parameter ETIRO Regulatory Specification (NSE / BSE) Exam Relevance & Notes
Underlying Asset 10-Year Government of India Benchmark Dated Security or 10-Year GoI IRF Matches the most liquid sovereign benchmark yield curve.
Contract Lot Size 2,000 underlying bonds (₹2,00,000 Face Value) Each bond has a nominal face value of ₹100 (2,000 × ₹100 = ₹2,00,000).
Option Types Call Option (CE) and Put Option (PE) Call grants right to buy underlying bond; Put grants right to sell.
Exercise Style European Style (Exercised only on expiration day) No early exercise permitted; eliminates early assignment risk for option sellers.
Quotation Basis Clean Price per ₹100 Face Value Quoted exclusive of accrued interest (e.g., premium quoted as ₹0.8525).
Minimum Tick Size ₹0.0025 (0.25 paise or 1/4th of a paisa) Identical to the tick size of 10-Year GoI IRF contracts.
Minimum Tick Value 2,000 × ₹0.0025 = ₹5.00 per contract Every 1 tick movement creates a ₹5 profit/loss per lot.
Strike Price Interval ₹0.25 (25 paise) or ₹0.50 intervals Exchange introduces a minimum of 5 In-the-Money, 1 At-the-Money, and 5 Out-of-the-Money strikes.
Contract Tenor & Cycle 3 Serial Monthly Contracts Near-month, next-month, and far-month trading cycles.
Expiration Day Last Thursday of the contract month If the last Thursday is a trading holiday, expires on the preceding business day.
Trading Hours 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM (Monday to Friday) Alings with the wholesale debt market (NDS-OM) trading hours.
Settlement Mechanism 100% Cash Settled in Indian Rupees (INR) Zero physical delivery of GoI dated securities. Cash settled on T+1 day.
Final Settlement Price (FSP) Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP) on NDS-OM Computed from wholesale NDS-OM trades between 3:00 PM and 5:00 PM on expiry, or FBIL valuation.
Margining Architecture
  • Option Buyers: Pay 100% of the option premium upfront at trade inception. No mark-to-market (MTM) margin or daily margin calls.
  • Option Writers (Sellers): Subject to portfolio-based SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) Initial Margin, Exposure Margin, and Extreme Loss Margin.
  • Mark-to-Market losses for writers are debited on a daily basis.
Regulatory Position Limits
  • Trading Member: 15% of total open interest or ₹1,000 Crores face value, whichever is higher.
  • Client Level: 3% of total open interest or ₹200 Crores face value, whichever is higher.
  • Mutual Funds: Scheme-level limits apply; gross derivative exposure must not exceed 100% of net assets of the scheme.

Pricing Framework

The Fischer Black (1976) Pricing Model

Standard equity options use the Black-Scholes-Merton (1973) model, which values options on spot equity shares. However, options on fixed-income futures, forward bonds, and interest rate derivatives are valued using the Fischer Black (1976) model (often called the Black-76 model).

Why Black-76 instead of Black-Scholes-Merton? (Exam Conceptual Test)

In equity options, the holder invests upfront in the spot asset, incurring financing costs while receiving continuous dividend yield. In debt options on futures or forward-starting bonds, the underlying is the Forward/Futures price (\(F\)). The forward price already reflects the cost of carry (repo financing cost minus accrued coupon). Thus, no continuous carry adjustment is needed, and the forward price is modeled as a lognormal martingale discounted at the risk-free rate.

Fischer Black (1976) Pricing Equations:
Call Option Premium (C):
C = e-rT × [F × N(d1) - X × N(d2)]
Put Option Premium (P):
P = e-rT × [X × N(-d2) - F × N(-d1)]
d1 = [ ln(F / X) + (σ2 / 2) × T ] / [ σ × √T ]
d2 = d1 - [ σ × √T ]
• F: Forward/Futures bond price
• X: Strike clean price
• T: Time to expiry (in years)
• r: Risk-free interest rate
• σ: Volatility of bond price/yield
• N(•): Cumulative normal distribution
• Put-Call Parity: C - P = e-rT × (F - X)
Variable Change in Variable Impact on Call (C) Impact on Put (P) Economic Explanation
Forward Bond Price (F) F ↑ (Yield ↓) ↑ Increases ↓ Decreases Higher underlying price widens call payoff, diminishes put payoff.
Strike Price (X) X ↑ ↓ Decreases ↑ Increases Higher strike means buyer pays more to exercise call or receives more for put.
Volatility (σ) σ ↑ ↑ Increases ↑ Increases Option holders benefit from upside tail risk while downside is capped at premium.
Time to Expiry (T) T ↑ ↑ Increases ↑ Increases More time creates greater probability for the option to finish deep in-the-money.

Greeks Nuance

Option Greeks: Price Delta (ΔP) vs. Yield Delta (Δy)

One of the most heavily tested, high-frequency traps in the NISM Series V-D exam is the critical distinction between Price Delta and Yield Delta. Candidates routinely lose marks by assuming all Call deltas are positive and all Put deltas are negative!

CRITICAL EXAM TRAP: The Signs of Yield Delta (Δy)

In fixed-income derivatives, underlying bond prices move inversely to interest rates and yields. Therefore:

Call Option Yield Delta is NEGATIVE!

When interest rate yields rise → Bond price falls → Call option value drops! (∂C / ∂y < 0).

Put Option Yield Delta is POSITIVE!

When interest rate yields rise → Bond price falls → Put option value increases! (∂P / ∂y > 0).

Mathematical Link via the Chain Rule:

Yield Delta (Δy) measures the sensitivity of the option premium (\(V\)) to a unit change in the underlying bond's Yield to Maturity (\(y\)):

Δy = ∂V / ∂y = ( ∂V / ∂P ) × ( ∂P / ∂y ) = ΔP × ( ∂P / ∂y )
From bond mathematics, Modified Duration (\(MD\)) dictates that: ∂P / ∂y ≈ - (P × MD) < 0 (strictly negative).
Call Yield Delta: Δy, Call = (+ ΔP) × (- P × MD) = NEGATIVE (−)
Put Yield Delta: Δy, Put = (- |ΔP|) × (- P × MD) = POSITIVE (+)
Greek Metric Definition & Partial Derivative Call Option Sign Put Option Sign Exam Focus Note
Price Delta (ΔP) ∂V / ∂Pbond Positive (+0.0 to +1.0) Negative (-1.0 to 0.0) ATM Call ΔP ≈ +0.50; ATM Put ΔP ≈ -0.50.
Yield Delta (Δy) ⭐ ∂V / ∂y NEGATIVE (−) POSITIVE (+) Highest frequency exam question! Inverse relationship in play.
Gamma (Γ) ∂2V / ∂P2 Positive (> 0) Positive (> 0) Measures curvature/convexity of option price; highest for ATM options near expiry.
Vega (ν) ∂V / ∂σ Positive (> 0) Positive (> 0) Both Call and Put buyers gain when interest rate volatility rises (RBI policy uncertainty).
Theta (Θ) ∂V / ∂t Negative (−) Negative (−) Time decay erodes option buyer value; decay accelerates exponentially in final 30 days.
Rho (ρ) ∂V / ∂r Negative / Modest Negative / Modest Discounts the future cash payoff via e-rT in the Black-76 model.
Portfolio DV01 and Duration Management via ETIRO

Fund managers dynamically adjust portfolio duration without executing physical cash market trades using Option DV01:

Option DV01 = Underlying Bond DV01 × |ΔP|
Portfolio DV01New = Portfolio DV01Original + [ Number of Contracts × 2,000 × Option DV01 ]

Buying Put options reduces the net portfolio duration and DV01, shielding the fund against monetary tightening.

Moneyness

Moneyness of Interest Rate Options: Price vs. Yield Perspectives

ETIRO can be analyzed either through the clean bond price or through the underlying yield to maturity. Understanding both perspectives is mandatory for the exam:

Call Option Moneyness CE
In-the-Money (ITM) Call:

• Bond Price > Strike Price (\(P > X\))

• Market Yield < Strike Yield (\(y < y_X\))

At-the-Money (ATM) Call:

• Bond Price = Strike Price (\(P = X\))

• Market Yield = Strike Yield (\(y = y_X\))

Out-of-the-Money (OTM) Call:

• Bond Price < Strike Price (\(P < X\))

• Market Yield > Strike Yield (\(y > y_X\))

Put Option Moneyness PE
In-the-Money (ITM) Put:

• Bond Price < Strike Price (\(P < X\))

• Market Yield > Strike Yield (\(y > y_X\))

At-the-Money (ATM) Put:

• Bond Price = Strike Price (\(P = X\))

• Market Yield = Strike Yield (\(y = y_X\))

Out-of-the-Money (OTM) Put:

• Bond Price > Strike Price (\(P > X\))

• Market Yield < Strike Yield (\(y < y_X\))

Intrinsic Value vs. Time Value (Numerical Example)

The market premium of an interest rate option consists of two distinct components:

Total Option Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value (Extrinsic Value)
Call Intrinsic Value = Max(0, Current Bond Clean Price - Strike Price)
Put Intrinsic Value = Max(0, Strike Price - Current Bond Clean Price)
Step-by-Step Practical Problem:

The 10-year benchmark GoI security is trading at a clean price of ₹101.50. A 30-day European Call option with Strike ₹100.00 is quoted at ₹2.10. A 30-day European Put option with Strike ₹102.50 is quoted at ₹1.80. Calculate the Intrinsic Value, Time Value, and Total Cash Outlay per lot (2,000 bonds).

Call Option (Strike ₹100.00):
Intrinsic Value = 101.50 - 100.00 = ₹1.50
Time Value = 2.10 - 1.50 = ₹0.60
Outlay per Lot = 2,000 × ₹2.10 = ₹4,200
Put Option (Strike ₹102.50):
Intrinsic Value = 102.50 - 101.50 = ₹1.00
Time Value = 1.80 - 1.00 = ₹0.80
Outlay per Lot = 2,000 × ₹1.80 = ₹3,600

Asymmetric Hedging

Hedging with IRO Put vs. Short IRF: The Asymmetry Advantage

Debt portfolio managers face a fundamental strategic choice when hedging against interest rate hikes: whether to sell linear Interest Rate Futures (IRF) or buy non-linear Interest Rate Options (IRO Put).

3-Scenario Comparative Case Study Portfolio: ₹10 Crore 10-Year G-Sec @ ₹100.00

Assume the current clean price of the bond portfolio is ₹100.00. The fund manager can either:
1. Hedge with IRF: Sell IRF at ₹100.00 (Zero upfront cash expense; linear symmetric hedge).
2. Hedge with IRO Put: Buy ATM Put Options at Strike ₹100.00 at an upfront premium of ₹1.50 per bond (₹15 Lakhs total premium for ₹10 Cr portfolio).

Institutional Strategies & SEBI

Institutional Option Strategies & SEBI Regulatory Mandates

Mutual funds, Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs), pension funds, and insurance companies deploy structured option strategies within rigorous regulatory constraints prescribed by SEBI and RBI:

Covered Call Writing Income

Structure: Long Physical G-Sec Portfolio + Sell Out-of-the-Money Call Options.

Objective: Deployed in a neutral or range-bound interest rate environment to harvest option premium and generate incremental yield on dormant holdings.

Trade-off: Upside is capped at the strike price. If yields fall sharply and bond prices surge, the fund does not participate beyond the strike.
Protective Put Insurance

Structure: Long Physical G-Sec Portfolio + Buy Out-of-the-Money / ATM Put Options.

Objective: Guards against catastrophic yield shocks (e.g. unexpected repo rate hikes, credit rating downgrades, sovereign debt supply spikes).

Trade-off: Incurs non-refundable premium drag. Acts like an insurance premium policy.
Zero-Cost Collar Costless Band

Structure: Long Portfolio + Buy OTM Put (Floor) + Sell OTM Call (Cap).

Objective: The premium earned from selling the Call exactly offsets the premium paid to purchase the Put, establishing a zero-cost protective corridor [XPut, XCall].

Trade-off: Caps upside at the Call strike in exchange for a free downside floor at the Put strike.
SEBI Regulations on Mutual Fund Participation in ETIRO
Hedging Intent Restriction:

Mutual funds are permitted to participate in exchange-traded derivatives primarily for hedging and portfolio duration rebalancing. Speculative naked option writing is strictly prohibited.

Covered Call Writing Permission:

Mutual funds can write call options only if they hold the equivalent quantity of the underlying government security in the physical portfolio (fully covered).

100% Gross Exposure Cap:

The cumulative gross exposure across physical debt securities, money market instruments, IRF, and option premiums/exposures cannot exceed 100% of the scheme's net assets (NAV).

Duration Category Compliance:

Derivative positions must not breach the Macaulay Duration boundaries prescribed under SEBI mutual fund scheme categorization guidelines (e.g. Short Duration, Medium Duration).
