Section 11.1 • Fast-Track Refresher
Benchmarking Principles

Benchmarking & Total Return Index (TRI) Mandate

Understanding the shift from Price Return Index (PRI) to Total Return Index (TRI) and why SEBI made it compulsory.

A benchmark is an unmanaged, standardized basket of securities that serves as an objective baseline against which a mutual fund scheme's risk, return, and portfolio manager skill are judged. A fund's absolute return is meaningless in isolation: generating 15% when the broader market gained 25% is underperformance, while generating 8% when the broader market dropped 5% demonstrates superior management.

Price Return Index (PRI) — Discontinued Pre-Feb 2018

Measures only capital price movements of constituent stocks. Completely ignores dividends distributed by companies in the index.

Flaw / Distortion: Mutual fund NAVs automatically receive and reinvest dividends from portfolio holdings. Comparing a dividend-accumulating NAV against a price-only PRI created an artificial optical alpha of 1.5% to 2.5% p.a., flattering active managers unfairly.
Total Return Index (TRI) — Mandatory SEBI Mandate

Measures both capital gains AND dividend reinvestment. Assumes gross dividends declared by index constituents are immediately reinvested back into the index on the ex-dividend date.

Fair & Transparent: SEBI mandated effective February 1, 2018, that all equity and hybrid mutual funds must benchmark performance exclusively against the Total Return Index (TRI), eliminating phantom alpha.
Attribute Price Return Index (PRI) Total Return Index (TRI)
Components Included Stock price fluctuations only (Capital appreciation/depreciation) Stock price movements + All corporate dividend cash flows
Parity with Mutual Fund NAV Mismatched (Fund NAV holds dividends, PRI does not) Perfect Parity (Both fund NAV and TRI capture dividends)
Historical Return Difference Lower by roughly 1.5% to 2.5% per annum Higher by roughly 1.5% to 2.5% per annum due to compounding
SEBI Regulatory Status Disallowed for performance benchmarking Mandatory across all equity & hybrid schemes

SEBI Two-Tier Framework

Two-Tier Benchmark Architecture & AMFI Category Indices

To eliminate "benchmark gaming", SEBI instituted a standardized two-tier framework across all scheme categories.

Historically, AMCs frequently changed benchmarks or picked easier indices (e.g., a mid-cap fund comparing itself against Nifty 50) to make fund managers look skillful. Effective April 1, 2022, SEBI created a standardized Two-Tier Benchmarking Framework:

Tier-1 Benchmark (Compulsory) Mandatory

Reflects the broad category to which the scheme belongs. AMFI issues a single, standardized list of Tier-1 indices for all schemes. Every fund in a category must use an AMFI-prescribed Tier-1 benchmark.

  • Ensures an apple-to-apple comparison among peers in the same category.
  • AMC cannot arbitrarily select a narrow or easier index for Tier-1.
Tier-2 Benchmark (Optional) AMC Discretion

Reflects the bespoke investment style, sector, or thematic strategy within the broad category.

  • Optional for broad funds; highly recommended for thematic/sectoral funds.
  • Allows fund managers to demonstrate skill against their specialized strategy.

Additional Benchmark (Standard Universal Benchmark)

In addition to Tier-1 and Tier-2, SEBI mandates disclosure of an Additional Standard Benchmark across all fund factsheets and advertisements. For equity funds, this is universally Nifty 50 TRI or S&P BSE Sensex TRI. For debt funds, it is the CRISIL 10 Year Gilt Index (10-Year GoI Bond).

Core Performance Ratios

Risk-Adjusted Ratios: Sharpe, Treynor & Sortino

Evaluating excess return generated per unit of risk assumed. Higher is always better.

Raw returns are deeply deceptive. A fund delivering 18% return by taking extreme, gut-wrenching volatility is inferior to a fund delivering 16% return with minimal volatility. Modern Portfolio Theory provides three foundational ratios to evaluate risk-adjusted return:

Sharpe Ratio Total Risk
Sharpe = (Rp - Rf) / σp

• Numerator: Excess return over risk-free rate.
• Denominator: Standard Deviation (σp) = Total Risk (Systematic + Unsystematic).
• Best Used For: Evaluating a fund that constitutes the investor's entire portfolio.

Treynor Ratio Systematic Risk
Treynor = (Rp - Rf) / βp

• Numerator: Excess return over risk-free rate.
• Denominator: Beta (βp) = Systematic (Market) Risk only.
• Best Used For: Evaluating a fund as an addition to a large, well-diversified portfolio where specific risk is eliminated.

Sortino Ratio Downside Risk
Sortino = (Rp - Rf) / σd

• Numerator: Excess return over risk-free or MAR.
• Denominator: Downside Deviation (σd) = Downside Volatility only.
• Best Used For: Investors who welcome upside volatility but dread loss of capital and deep drawdowns.

Ratio Formula Risk Metric (Denominator) Theoretical Context Primary Exam Trap
Sharpe Ratio (Rp - Rf) / σp Standard Deviation (σ) Assumes investor holds only this scheme (full standalone risk matters). Penalizes positive upside surges as "volatility".
Treynor Ratio (Rp - Rf) / βp Beta (β) Assumes unsystematic risk is completely diversified away; only market beta remains. Misleading if the fund is poorly diversified (ignores specific risk).
Sortino Ratio (Rp - MAR) / σd Downside Deviation (σd) Asymmetric risk preference: investors only care about negative swings below a hurdle rate. Requires large sample size of negative return periods to be statistically sound.
Practical Solved Numerical: Comparing Two Schemes

Consider two schemes evaluated in the market when the 91-day T-bill risk-free rate (Rf) is 6%:

Scheme Blue (Aggressive Multi Cap)

• Annualized Return (Rp): 18%
• Standard Deviation (σ): 16%
• Portfolio Beta (β): 1.20
• Downside Deviation (σd): 6%

Sharpe = (18 - 6) / 16 = 0.75
Treynor = (18 - 6) / 1.20 = 10.00%
Sortino = (18 - 6) / 6 = 2.00
Scheme Green (Disciplined Large Cap)

• Annualized Return (Rp): 15%
• Standard Deviation (σ): 10%
• Portfolio Beta (β): 0.75
• Downside Deviation (σd): 5%

Sharpe = (15 - 6) / 10 = 0.90 (Superior Standalone)
Treynor = (15 - 6) / 0.75 = 12.00% (Superior Addition)
Sortino = (15 - 6) / 5 = 1.80
Exam Takeaway: Even though Scheme Blue generated 3% higher raw return (18% vs 15%), Scheme Green delivered significantly higher Sharpe (0.90 vs 0.75) and higher Treynor (12% vs 10%) because it assumed vastly lower volatility and market risk.

CAPM & Active Skill

Jensen's Alpha (α) & Information Ratio (IR)

Measuring pure active manager outperformance and tracking risk efficiency.

1. Jensen's Alpha (α) — CAPM Outperformance

Derived from the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM). It measures the excess return generated by the fund manager above the expected return required for the systematic risk (β) assumed.

Expected Return E(Rp) = Rf + βp × (Rm - Rf)
Jensen's Alpha (α) = Actual Rp - E(Rp)
  • Positive Alpha (α > 0): The manager generated superior returns via security selection / market timing (value added).
  • Zero Alpha (α = 0): Return exactly matches the CAPM expectation for the beta taken.
  • Negative Alpha (α < 0): The manager destroyed value, underperforming the risk-adjusted benchmark (dragged by expenses or poor stock picks).
2. Information Ratio (IR) — Active Return vs Active Risk

Measures the consistency with which an active manager generates excess return relative to the benchmark index, normalized by active risk (Tracking Error).

Active Return = Rp - Rb
Information Ratio = (Rp - Rb) / Tracking Error
  • SEBI Mandate: Daily disclosure of Information Ratio on AMC websites and AMFI for all active schemes.
  • Benchmark IR: >0.50 is good; >0.75 is very strong; >1.00 represents exceptional, persistent manager skill.
Exam Numerical Walkthrough: Calculating Jensen's Alpha & Information Ratio

Problem: A mutual fund delivers an actual return of 16%. The risk-free rate is 6%, the benchmark market return is 12%, the fund's Beta is 1.20, and its Tracking Error is 4%.

Step 1: Calculate Market Risk Premium (Rm - Rf) = 12% - 6% = 6.0%
Step 2: Calculate CAPM Expected Return E(Rp) = Rf + β × (Rm - Rf) = 6% + 1.20 × 6% = 6% + 7.2% = 13.2%
Step 3: Calculate Jensen's Alpha (α) = Actual Rp - Expected Return = 16% - 13.2% = +2.8%
Step 4: Calculate Information Ratio (IR) = (Rp - Rb) / Tracking Error = (16% - 12%) / 4% = 4% / 4% = 1.00

Interpretation: The fund manager generated 2.8% pure alpha above CAPM expectations, and an Information Ratio of 1.00 confirms exceptional active consistency.

Passive Fund Replication

Tracking Error vs. Tracking Difference for Index Funds & ETFs

Understanding the critical metrics used to evaluate passive index replication efficiency.

In passive management (Index Funds and ETFs), the fund manager does not attempt to beat the benchmark; their sole objective is to replicate the benchmark index as faithfully as possible. Two distinct metrics evaluate this replication quality:

Attribute Tracking Difference (TD) Tracking Error (TE)
Mathematical Definition Rscheme - Rbenchmark σ(Rscheme, t - Rbenchmark, t) × √250
What it Measures The simple arithmetic quantum of under/outperformance over a specified period. The annualized volatility/dispersion of daily return differences (consistency of tracking).
Typical Value / Sign Usually negative (due to Total Expense Ratio and cash drag). Always positive (standard deviation cannot be negative). Desired close to 0%.
Reporting Horizon Disclosed for 1-yr, 3-yr, 5-yr, 10-yr, and since inception. Calculated over past 3-year trailing daily returns (or since inception).
SEBI Regulatory Limit No fixed numerical ceiling (governed by TER limits). Must NOT exceed 2.00% for Index Funds and ETFs.
Primary Causes of Tracking Difference and Tracking Error:
1. Total Expense Ratio (TER)

The index has 0% cost, while the fund deducts daily recurring TER from NAV.

2. Cash Drag

Funds hold 0.5% - 2% in liquid cash for daily redemptions, diluting equity gains.

3. Dividend Reinvestment Lag

Index reinvests dividends on ex-date; fund receives cash weeks later.

4. Rebalancing & Brokerage

Brokerage, STT, and market impact cost incurred during index rebalancing.

5. Corporate Actions

Rights issues, bonus shares, spin-offs, and stock splits timing mismatch.

6. Circuit Filters & Illiquidity

Constituent stocks hitting upper/lower circuits prevent immediate execution.

SEBI Compliance Rule: If Tracking Error for any Index Fund or ETF exceeds 2%, the AMC must immediately report this to the Board of Trustees and prominently publish the breach on its website.

SEBI Advertising Code

SEBI Performance Advertising & Factsheet Standards

Strict regulatory rules governing public performance claims, period horizons, and disclosures.

SEBI's Advertising Code (Sixth Schedule to SEBI Mutual Fund Regulations) protects investors against selective, cherry-picked, or misleading performance claims:

Mandatory Time Periods (CAGR)

For all mutual fund schemes in existence for more than 1 year, performance must be presented in Compounded Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) for:

• Past 1 Year • Past 3 Years • Past 5 Years • Since Inception

If the scheme has been in existence for less than 5 years but more than 3 years: 1-yr, 3-yr, and inception returns must be given.

Standard Initial Investment of ₹10,000

Along with percentage CAGR returns, advertisements must illustrate the rupee growth of a standard initial investment of ₹10,000 across:

1. Scheme NAV • 2. Tier-1 Benchmark • 3. Additional Benchmark

Investors can clearly see how ₹10,000 grew in the scheme vs the category index vs broad market index.

Fund Manager Multi-Scheme Disclosure Rule (Anti-Cherry-Picking)

If an AMC publishes an advertisement highlighting the superior performance of any one scheme, it is mandatory to disclose the performance of ALL other schemes managed by the same fund manager in the same advertisement!

What if the fund manager manages more than 6 schemes?
The AMC must disclose the performance of at least the top 3 and bottom 3 schemes managed by that fund manager based on trailing 1-year returns, preventing the fund house from hiding poor-performing schemes.
Period Restrictions for Schemes in Existence for Less Than 1 Year
  • Schemes < 6 months old: Strictly PROHIBITED from advertising any performance figures to the public.
  • Schemes > 6 months but < 1 year old: Cannot advertise annualized returns (except for money market schemes, liquid schemes, and overnight schemes).
  • Liquid / Overnight / Money Market Schemes: Permitted to disclose simple annualized returns for short standard windows: 7 days, 15 days, 30 days, 3 months (90 days), and 6 months (180 days).
Monthly Factsheet Publishing Mandate

AMCs must publish monthly scheme factsheets on their websites within 10 calendar days of each month-end. Factsheets must disclose: Scheme AUM, top 10 portfolio holdings, sector allocation, Total Expense Ratio (TER), Potential Risk Class (PRC) Matrix for debt, Risk-o-meter (scheme and Tier-1 benchmark), Sharpe Ratio, Beta, Tracking Error, and Information Ratio.

March 2026 SIF Curriculum

Performance Dynamics for Specialized Investment Funds (SIF)

Advanced metrics and evaluation frameworks for high-conviction, non-linear strategies.

Under the March 2026 NISM Series V-D curriculum, Specialized Investment Funds (SIF) bridge traditional mutual funds and private alternative funds (minimum ticket size: ₹10 Lakhs). Because SIFs can deploy unconstrained long-short equity, derivative overlays, and structured credit strategies, traditional performance metrics must be adapted:

1. Sortino & Tail-Risk Metrics over Sharpe

Because derivative strategies create asymmetric (non-normal) return distributions, the Sharpe ratio's assumption of bell-curve normality fails. SIF distributors must evaluate Sortino Ratio, Maximum Drawdown (MDD), and Value at Risk (VaR) to protect against catastrophic tail losses.

2. Hurdle Rates & High-Water Marks

SIFs that incorporate performance-linked incentive fees must operate under a strict High-Water Mark (HWM) principle and hurdle rate (e.g., T-Bill + 400 bps). Performance fees cannot be levied until all previous historical losses are fully recouped.
