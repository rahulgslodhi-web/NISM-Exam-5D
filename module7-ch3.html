Section 3.1 • Health Insurance Landscape Product Architecture

Health Insurance: Indemnity vs. Fixed-Benefit Policies

Health insurance covers medical costs arising from disease, illness, or accidental bodily injury. The core divide in health coverage is between Indemnity and Fixed-Benefit structures.

Indemnity Plans (Mediclaim) Expense Reimbursement

Reimburses actual medical expenses incurred during hospitalization (inpatient treatment, ICU, daycare surgeries) up to the Sum Insured.

  • Requires hospital bills, discharge summary, and pharmacy receipts.
  • Subject to Principle of Indemnity (insured cannot claim more than actual expenses).
  • Available as Individual or Family Floater covers.
Fixed-Benefit Plans (Critical Illness) Lump Sum Payout

Pays a predetermined lump-sum cash amount upon the confirmed first diagnosis or occurrence of a specified health event, regardless of hospital costs.

  • Critical Illness: Pays 100% sum insured on diagnosis of cancer, stroke, kidney failure (subject to 28-30 day survival).
  • Personal Accident (PA): Death, permanent total disability (PTD), permanent partial disability (PPD).
  • Exempt from indemnity; client can claim full amount across multiple policies!

Individual Cover vs. Family Floater Dynamics

Individual Health Policy: Each family member has their own dedicated sum insured (e.g. ₹5 Lakhs each). One member exhausting their cover does not affect others. Ideal when family members have huge age gaps or pre-existing diseases.
Family Floater Policy: Single umbrella sum insured (e.g. ₹10 Lakhs) shared by spouse and children. Premium is priced on the age of the oldest insured member. Cost-effective for young, healthy families. Feature: Auto-Restoration / Reload restores the sum insured if exhausted mid-year for subsequent unrelated illnesses.
Section 3.2 • Cost Sharing & Waiting Clauses High Yield

Deductibles, Co-Pay, Room Rent & IRDAI 2024 Waiting Periods

Understanding cost-sharing terms and statutory waiting periods is crucial for evaluating health proposals.

Top-Up vs. Super Top-Up Health Insurance ⭐

Tested in Every Exam

Both plans provide high health insurance coverage at a fraction of standard base policy costs by applying a Deductible (threshold). However, the way the deductible is triggered is completely different:

Feature Regular Top-Up Plan Super Top-Up Plan ⭐
Deductible Application Applied to EACH SINGLE hospitalization claim independently. Applied to the AGGREGATE of all claims across the full policy year!
Scenario: Deductible ₹5L. Claim 1: ₹3.5L. Claim 2: ₹4.0L. Pays ₹0 on Claim 1 (below ₹5L).
Pays ₹0 on Claim 2 (below ₹5L).
Total Paid = ₹0! 		Total Claims = ₹3.5L + ₹4.0L = ₹7.5L.
Deductible = ₹5.0L.
Super Top-Up Pays = ₹2.5 Lakhs!
Adviser Recommendation Risky; leaves policyholder exposed to multiple moderate claims. Highly recommended to supercharge base cover cheaply.

Room Rent Capping & Proportionate Deduction Formula ⭐

Penalty Clause

Standard policies cap room rent at 1% of Sum Insured per day (and 2% for ICU). If an insured occupies a room charging higher than their eligible limit, the insurer applies a Proportionate Deduction to all associated medical treatment costs (doctor fees, surgery, nursing, operation theatre)!

Proportionate Claim Payable Formula:
Eligible Associated Expense = Actual Incurred Expense × [ Eligible Room Rent Limit / Actual Room Rent Occupied ]
Numerical Case: Amit has a ₹5 Lakh policy with a 1% room rent cap (₹5,000/day). He stays in a deluxe room at ₹10,000/day for 4 days. His hospital bill is: Room Rent = ₹40,000; Doctor & Surgery charges = ₹2,00,000.
• Room rent paid = 4 × ₹5,000 = ₹20,000.
• Surgery/Doctor paid = ₹2,00,000 × (₹5,000 / ₹10,000) = ₹1,00,000 only!
• Amit must pay the remaining ₹1,20,000 out of pocket due to proportionate deduction!

Statutory Waiting Periods (IRDAI 2024 Master Circular Reforms) ⭐

Initial Waiting Period 30 Days from policy commencement. No claims admissible except emergency accidental injuries.
Specific Illness Waiting 12 to 24 Months for slow-developing conditions (cataract, hernia, joint replacement, piles, kidney stones).
PED Waiting Period ⭐ Reduced to a maximum of 36 Months (3 Years) under IRDAI 2024 regulations (earlier 48 months).
Moratorium Clause ⭐ Reduced to 5 Years (60 Months) continuous coverage. Thereafter, claim cannot be contested except for established fraud.
Section 3.3 • Motor Vehicles Insurance Motor Vehicles Act Mandates

Motor Insurance: Third-Party (TP), Own Damage (OD) & IDV

Motor insurance is governed under the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 (amended in 2019).

Statutory Mandate

Third-Party (TP) Liability ⭐

Compulsory by law for all vehicles. Covers legal liability to third parties:

  • Death / Bodily Injury: UNLIMITED financial liability! Awarded by Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT).
  • Third-Party Property Damage (TPPD): Capped at ₹7,50,000.
Optional Cover

Own Damage (OD) Cover

Covers loss or damage to the insured vehicle itself due to accidental external collision, fire, self-ignition, lightning, theft, flood, earthquake, or transit damage.

Mandatory Add-On

Compulsory Personal Accident (CPA)

Mandatory personal accident cover of ₹15 Lakhs for the registered owner-driver covering death and permanent total disability. (Exempt if owner holds existing standalone PA policy of ₹15L+).

Insured's Declared Value (IDV) & Constructive Total Loss (CTL) ⭐

Valuation Engine

The IDV is the agreed Sum Insured for the vehicle, representing the current market value. It is calculated by taking the manufacturer's listed selling price and subtracting standard age depreciation:

Age of Vehicle Depreciation % for IDV Age of Vehicle Depreciation % for IDV
Not exceeding 6 months 5% Exceeding 2 years but ≤ 3 years 30%
Exceeding 6 months but ≤ 1 year 15% Exceeding 3 years but ≤ 4 years 40%
Exceeding 1 year but ≤ 2 years 20% Exceeding 4 years but ≤ 5 years 50%
Constructive Total Loss (CTL) Threshold: A vehicle is treated as a Constructive Total Loss when the aggregate cost of retrieval and repair exceeds 75% of the vehicle's IDV. The insurer pays the full IDV minus wreck salvage value.
Section 3.4 • Property & Home Insurance Under-Insurance Penalty

Home Insurance (Bharat Griha Raksha) & Principle of Average ⭐

Standardized home insurance regulations introduced by IRDAI and the penalty mechanics of under-insurance.

Bharat Griha Raksha (Standard Home Policy)

Introduced by IRDAI to provide a standardized, transparent home building and contents insurance product covering 14 natural and man-made perils (fire, cyclone, flood, earthquake, landslide, terrorism, bursting of water pipes).

  • Automatic General Contents Cover: Automatically covers general home contents for 20% of the building sum insured (maximum ₹10 Lakhs) without requiring the insured to declare individual item lists.
  • Waiver of Under-Insurance: Under Bharat Griha Raksha, if under-insurance is within 15% of true reinstatement value, no under-insurance penalty is applied!

The Principle of Average (Under-Insurance Penalty Formula) ⭐

Exam Formula

In commercial fire and property policies, if the insured chooses to insure their asset for an amount less than its actual value at the time of loss (to save premium), they are deemed to be their own insurer for the difference and must bear a proportionate fraction of any partial loss.

Claim Payable = Total Loss Amount × [ Sum Insured / Actual Value of Property at Time of Loss ]
Worked Numerical Problem:
A warehouse with goods actually valued at ₹50,00,000 is insured under a Standard Fire policy with a Sum Insured of only ₹30,00,000 (60% coverage). A fire causes partial damage of ₹10,00,000.
• Claim Payable = ₹10,00,000 × (₹30,00,000 / ₹50,00,000) = ₹10,00,000 × 0.60 = ₹6,00,000.
• The warehouse owner must bear the remaining ₹4,00,000 loss personally due to the average clause!
