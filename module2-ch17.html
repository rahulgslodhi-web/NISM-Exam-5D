Hedging Math & Arbitrage

Portfolio Beta Hedging with Index Futures

Equity mutual fund managers hold diversified equity portfolios to eliminate unsystematic (company-specific) risk. However, the portfolio remains exposed to systematic (market) risk, quantified by its Beta (β). Mutual fund managers use Index Futures (e.g., Nifty 50 or BSE Sensex futures) to hedge against broad market downturns or systematically modify the portfolio's overall market sensitivity without liquidating underlying cash shares.

Master Beta Hedging Formula Core Exam Formula
N = (βtarget - βportfolio) × Portfolio Value (Vp)Futures Price (F) × Lot Size (Contract Value)
Complete Hedge (β* = 0): Result is negative (−). Manager must sell / short index futures to fully eliminate market exposure.
Reduce Beta (β* < βp): Result is negative (−). Manager sells / shorts index futures to insulate against anticipated market drops.
Increase Beta (β* > βp): Result is positive (+). Manager buys / longs index futures to enhance upside participation without purchasing shares.

Worked Numerical Scenarios (Tested Regularly in NISM V-D)

Example 1: Complete Portfolio Hedge (βtarget = 0) Full Neutralization

A mutual fund holds an equity portfolio worth ₹50 Crores (₹50,00,00,000) with an estimated portfolio Beta (βp) of 1.20. Nifty 50 Futures is quoting at ₹25,000 with a market lot size of 25 units. How many Nifty Futures contracts must the fund manager trade to completely eliminate systematic risk?

• Contract Value of Nifty Futures = 25,000 × 25 = ₹6,25,000
• Target Beta (βtarget) = 0
• N = [(0 - 1.20) × 50,00,00,000] / 6,25,000 = -60,00,00,000 / 6,25,000 = -960 Contracts
Action: Short (Sell) exactly 960 Nifty Futures contracts. If the broad market falls by 10%, the portfolio loses approx ₹6.00 Crores (10% × 1.20 × ₹50 Cr), which is offset by a ₹6.00 Crore profit on the short futures position.
Example 2: Partial Beta Reduction (e.g., from 1.40 down to 0.80) Defensive Adjustment

A fund manager holding an aggressive equity portfolio of ₹20 Crores with a high Beta of 1.40 expects temporary market volatility. The manager wishes to de-risk the portfolio by reducing effective Beta to 0.80. Nifty Futures is trading at ₹24,000 with a lot size of 25 units.

• Contract Value = 24,000 × 25 = ₹6,00,000
• N = [(0.80 - 1.40) × 20,00,00,000] / 6,00,000 = [-0.60 × 20,00,00,000] / 6,00,000
• N = -12,00,00,000 / 6,00,000 = -200 Contracts
Action: Short (Sell) 200 Nifty Futures contracts to lower effective sensitivity from 1.40 to 0.80.
Example 3: Increasing Portfolio Beta (e.g., from 0.90 up to 1.30) Aggressive Bullish Tilt

A fund manager holds ₹15 Crores of low-beta stocks (β = 0.90). Expecting an aggressive post-election rally, the manager wants to scale portfolio beta up to 1.30. Nifty Futures trades at ₹25,000 (Lot size = 25).

• Contract Value = 25,000 × 25 = ₹6,25,000
• N = [(1.30 - 0.90) × 15,00,00,000] / 6,25,000 = [+0.40 × 15,00,00,000] / 6,25,000 = +96 Contracts
Action: Long (Buy) 96 Nifty Futures contracts to boost overall market participation.
Cash-Futures Arbitrage & Basis Risk

Basis is the difference between the Cash Spot Price and the Futures Price (Basis = Spot - Futures). At expiration, basis must converge to zero.

  • Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage: If Futures trades at a substantial premium over theoretical fair value (Cost of Carry), arbitrageurs buy cash shares and sell overvalued futures, locking in risk-free yield.
  • Reverse Cash-and-Carry: If Futures trades at an unwarranted discount, traders short cash shares and buy undervalued futures.
  • Basis Risk: Occurs when a hedge is closed out before expiry, as spot and futures price fluctuations do not move in absolute 100% lockstep.
Futures Roll-Over Dynamics

Because equity futures contracts have finite lives (expiring on the last Thursday of the month), institutional hedgers must roll over positions into the next month's contract.

  • Mechanics: Simultaneously squaring off current near-month contract and opening a matching position in the next-month contract.
  • Roll Spread: The difference between next-month futures and near-month futures.
  • High Roll-Over % with Positive Basis: Signals aggressive institutional bullishness being carried forward into the upcoming series.
  • High Roll-Over % with Negative Basis (Discount): Signals aggressive short build-up being carried forward.

Hedging Combinations

Covered Call & Protective Put Strategies

These classic hybrid strategies combine physical cash equity holdings with options to alter the risk-return profile.

1. Covered Call (Long Stock + Short OTM Call) Income Generation

An investor holding stock writes (sells) an Out-of-the-Money Call option against their shares. Ideal for range-bound, stagnant, or mildly bullish market views to generate recurring cash flow.

Covered Call Payoff Profile
• Max Profit: (X - S0) + Call Premium (C)
• Max Loss: S0 - C (Severe if stock plummets to ₹0)
• Breakeven: S0 - C
• Synthetic Equivalent: Short Put position

₹900-₹100+₹30-₹70
₹970 (BE)-₹30+₹30₹0
₹1,000₹0+₹30+₹30
₹1,050 (Strike)+₹50+₹30+₹80 (Max)

⚠️ Exam Trap: A covered call does not provide significant downside insurance! It only buffers drops by the small premium received. In return, the trader surrenders all upside above the strike price.
2. Protective Put (Long Stock + Long Put) Capital Insurance

An equity holder purchases a Put option on the stock as an insurance policy. It protects against market crashes while preserving 100% of unlimited upside potential.

Protective Put Payoff Profile
• Max Profit: Unlimited as stock price surges
• Max Loss: (S0 - X) + Put Premium (P) (Guaranteed Floor)
• Breakeven: S0 + P
• Synthetic Equivalent: Long Call position

💡 Exam Formula Rule: Cost of protection is the put premium \(P\). If stock crashes to zero, the put option allows the holder to sell at Strike \(X\), capping the loss strictly at \((S_0 - X) + P\).

Directional Vertical Spreads

Vertical Spreads: Bull Call & Bear Put Spreads

A vertical spread involves taking a simultaneous long and short position in options of the same class (both Calls or both Puts) and same expiry date, but at different strike prices.

Bull Call Spread (Net Debit Spread) Moderately Bullish

Constructed by Buying a Lower Strike Call (X1) at premium C1 and Selling a Higher Strike Call (X2) at premium C2.

Bull Call Spread Payoff
• Net Cost (Debit): C1 - C2
• Max Profit: (X2 - X1) - Net Debit
• Max Loss: Net Debit Paid
• Breakeven Point: X1 + Net Debit
Worked Example:
Buy 1,000 Call @ ₹45; Sell 1,050 Call @ ₹18.
Net Debit = 45 - 18 = ₹27.00.
Max Profit = (1050 - 1000) - 27 = ₹23.00.
Breakeven = 1000 + 27 = ₹1,027.00.

Selling the higher strike call finances part of the lower strike call's cost, lowering both breakeven and risk.

Bear Put Spread (Net Debit Spread) Moderately Bearish

Constructed by Buying a Higher Strike Put (X2) at premium P2 and Selling a Lower Strike Put (X1) at premium P1.

Bear Put Spread Payoff
• Net Cost (Debit): P2 - P1
• Max Profit: (X2 - X1) - Net Debit
• Max Loss: Net Debit Paid
• Breakeven Point: X2 - Net Debit
Worked Example:
Buy 1,000 Put @ ₹40; Sell 950 Put @ ₹15.
Net Debit = 40 - 15 = ₹25.00.
Max Profit = (1000 - 950) - 25 = ₹25.00.
Breakeven = 1000 - 25 = ₹975.00.

Buying the ATM put gives bearish exposure while selling the OTM put offsets time decay and premium cost.

Spread Strategy Outlook Options Legs Used Cashflow Max Profit Max Loss Breakeven
Bull Call Spread Moderately Bullish Buy Call X1 + Sell Call X2 (X1 < X2) Net Debit (X2 - X1) - Debit Net Debit X1 + Debit
Bear Put Spread Moderately Bearish Buy Put X2 + Sell Put X1 (X2 > X1) Net Debit (X2 - X1) - Debit Net Debit X2 - Debit

Bear Call Spread Mildly Bearish / Neutral Sell Call X1 + Buy Call X2 (X1 < X2) Net Credit Net Credit Received (X2 - X1) - Credit X1 + Credit

Volatility Plays

Straddles, Strangles & The Zero-Cost Collar

When a trader expects severe price turbulence or market calm without having a clear directional bias, combination strategies across both Calls and Puts are employed.

1. Long Straddle High Volatility

Simultaneously Buy ATM Call (X) + Buy ATM Put (X) with the same strike and expiration date.

• Outlook: Expects extreme breakout in either direction (e.g. Union Budget, earnings, court verdict).
• Max Profit: Unlimited on either side.
• Max Loss: Total Premium Paid (C + P).
• Breakevens: X ± (Call Premium + Put Premium).
Short Straddle: Selling ATM Call + ATM Put. Profits if price stays locked exactly at Strike X. Catastrophic unlimited risk if market breaks out!
2. Long Strangle Cheaper Volatility

Simultaneously Buy OTM Put (X1) + Buy OTM Call (X2) where X1 < X2.

• Outlook: Expects massive price explosion; cheaper initial capital outlay than a Straddle.
• Max Profit: Unlimited on either side.
• Max Loss: Total Premium Paid (C2 + P1).
• Breakevens: Upper = X2 + Total Premium; Lower = X1 - Total Premium.
Requires a wider price displacement than a straddle before turning profitable because options are both Out-of-the-Money.
3. Zero-Cost Collar Free Downside Floor

Long Underlying Stock + Buy OTM Put (X1) + Sell OTM Call (X2).

• Zero-Cost Construction: The premium received from writing the OTM Call exactly funds the purchase of the OTM Put (\(C \approx P\)).
• Downside Floor: Locked at Put Strike X1.
• Upside Cap: Capped at Call Strike X2.
Institutional fund managers love Collars because they provide guaranteed downside capital preservation without requiring net cash budget.

4-Leg Range Play

Butterfly Spread (Neutral Range Strategy)

A Butterfly Spread is an advanced, strictly defined-risk strategy designed to capitalize on low volatility and pinpoint consolidation within an expected range.

Long Call Butterfly Spread Construction 4 Options • 3 Strikes

A Butterfly uses 4 contracts across 3 equidistant strike prices (\(X_1 < X_2 < X_3\) such that \(X_2 - X_1 = X_3 - X_2\)):

Buy 1 ITM Call (X1) + Sell 2 ATM Calls (X2) + Buy 1 OTM Call (X3)
(Can also be constructed identically using Puts)
Butterfly Spread "Tent" Payoff Profile
Key Formulas:
• Net Cost: C(X1) - 2C(X2) + C(X3) (Net Debit)
• Max Profit: (X2 - X1) - Net Debit (Occurs exactly at ST = X2)
• Max Loss: Net Debit Paid (Occurs if ST ≤ X1 or ST ≥ X3)
• Lower Breakeven: X1 + Net Debit
• Upper Breakeven: X3 - Net Debit
Worked Numerical Example:
Underlying = ₹1,000. Strikes: 950, 1000, 1050.
Buy 950 Call @ ₹65
Sell 2 × 1000 Calls @ ₹30 (Rec: ₹60)
Buy 1050 Call @ ₹12
Net Debit = 65 - 60 + 12 = ₹17.00
Max Profit = (1000 - 950) - 17 = ₹33.00
Breakevens: 967 and 1,033

Put-Call Ratio (PCR) & Price-OI Interpretation Matrix

Market technicians and derivatives analysts monitor Open Interest (OI), Volume, and the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) to decode institutional positioning and anticipate market turning points.

Put-Call Ratio (PCR) Mechanics Contrarian Tool

In the Indian markets, retail traders predominantly buy calls/puts, while well-capitalized institutional players (DIIs, FPIs, prop desks) write (sell) options. Therefore, high put writing indicates strong floor support, while high call writing indicates heavy ceiling resistance.

PCR (OI) = Total Put Open Interest / Total Call Open Interest
Standard PCR Benchmark Thresholds:
  • PCR > 1.30 to 1.50 (Oversold): Extreme put writing. The market has become excessively one-sided and bearish. Contrarians view this as an imminent bullish bounce / bottom reversal.
  • PCR < 0.60 to 0.70 (Overbought): Extreme call writing and complacency. Contrarians view this as an imminent bearish pullback / top reversal.
  • PCR between 0.90 and 1.10: Neutral baseline balance between buyers and sellers.
