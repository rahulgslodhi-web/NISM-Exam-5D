Section 2.1 • Life Insurance Need Assessment High Yield

Methods for Estimating Life Insurance Coverage

How much insurance is enough? In advisory practice and Level 2 case studies, two analytical methodologies predominate: the Human Life Value (HLV) method and the Expense Protection / Needs Analysis method.

1 The Human Life Value (HLV) Method (Huebner Model) ⭐

Formula Required

Formulated by Prof. Solomon S. Huebner, HLV capitalizes the individual's future economic earning capacity dedicated to dependents. It represents the Present Value of the person's future net earnings surplus after deducting personal income taxes and personal maintenance expenses until retirement.

Theoretical HLV Formula:
HLV = Σt=1n [ (Et - Tt - Ct) / (1 + r)t ]

Where: Et = Gross Annual Earnings in year t; Tt = Income Taxes; Ct = Personal Consumption/Living expenses of the earner; r = Post-tax discount rate; n = Remaining working years until retirement.

Level 2 Exam Calculation Example:

Client Profile: Rajesh, age 35, plans to retire at age 60 (remaining working life = 25 years). Current annual income is ₹18,00,000. Taxes are ₹3,00,000 p.a. His personal consumption is ₹3,00,000 p.a. The appropriate discount rate is 6% p.a. Calculate his HLV.

1. Net Annual Surplus to Family = ₹18,00,000 - ₹3,00,000 (Tax) - ₹3,00,000 (Personal) = ₹12,00,000 p.a.
2. Period (n) = 60 - 35 = 25 years
3. Discount Rate (r) = 6% = 0.06
4. Annuity PV Factor = [1 - (1 + 0.06)-25] / 0.06 = [1 - 0.233] / 0.06 ≈ 12.783
5. HLV = ₹12,00,000 × 12.783 ≈ ₹1,53,39,600 (₹1.53 Crores)

2 Expense Protection / Needs Analysis Method ⭐

Balance Sheet Approach

Rather than looking at earning capacity, the Needs Approach calculates the exact capital required by surviving family members to sustain their standard of living, pay off liabilities, and meet future milestone goals.

Required Life Insurance Cover = Total Family Financial Needs - Available Realisable Assets

Total Needs =
• Immediate Clearance Fund: Outstanding Home Loan, Car Loan, Personal Debt + Funeral / Final Medical costs
• Income Replacement Fund: Capitalized PV of family monthly household expenses until spouse life expectancy
• Specific Lump-Sum Goals: Child Higher Education Fund + Marriage Fund + Emergency Reserve
Less Available Liquid Assets =
• Fixed Deposits, Mutual Funds, Equity Shares, Existing Life Policies (Excluding primary self-occupied home!)
Section 2.2 • Product Architectures Product Classification

Traditional Life Insurance Products & Comparison

Understanding the structural mechanics, cost profiles, and cash-value accumulation across traditional plans.

Pure Term Assurance Recommended

Provides high financial protection at lowest cost. Pays Sum Assured only upon death during policy term. Zero maturity benefit.

TROP Variant: Term with Return of Premium charges 2x to 3x higher premium just to refund nominal premiums at maturity. Yields a sub-par ~2% IRR; RIA recommends pure term + investing difference in PPF/MF.
Whole Life Assurance Age 99 / 100

Provides permanent risk cover for the insured's entire lifetime (typically matures at age 99 or 100). Guaranteed death benefit payout whenever death occurs, or endowment payout if client survives to age 100.

Frequently used for estate liquidity to pay inheritance liabilities or equal distribution of assets among heirs.
Endowment Assurance Savings Combo

Pays Sum Assured + Accrued Bonuses on either premature death during term OR upon survival to maturity date.

Low transparency; effective annualized return (IRR) is historically modest at ~4.5% to 5.5% p.a.
Money-Back Policy Periodic Payouts

Pays periodic survival benefits (e.g. 20% of Sum Assured every 5 years) and remaining balance + bonuses at term end.

⭐ Critical Rule: In the event of death during the policy term, FULL Sum Assured is paid without deducting any previous survival payouts!
Section 2.3 • Unit Linked Insurance Plans High Yield

ULIPs: Fee Structure, Lock-in & Budget 2021 Taxation ⭐

ULIPs combine market investment with insurance cover. Units are credited to the investor's fund based on Net Asset Value (NAV).

Charge Category Deduction Method Regulatory Limit / IRDAI Mandate
Premium Allocation Charge (PAC) Deducted directly from gross premium before units are allocated. Covers initial marketing and distributor commissions. Tends to front-load in year 1.
Mortality Charge Monthly cancellation of units based on client age and Sum at Risk (SAR). Sum at Risk = Higher of (Sum Assured - Fund Value, 0). As fund value grows, SAR falls!
Fund Management Charge (FMC) ⭐ Adjusted daily in NAV computation before publishing unit price. Strictly capped at a maximum of 1.35% p.a. by IRDAI regulations.
Surrender / Discontinuance Charge Deducted if policy is surrendered within the initial 5-year lock-in. Graduated caps mandated by IRDAI (max ₹6,000 in Year 1 down to ₹2,000 in Year 4; NIL from Year 5).

Mandatory 5-Year Lock-In & DPF

ULIPs have a statutory lock-in period of 5 complete policy years. If the policyholder stops paying premiums or surrenders during the first 5 years, the funds are shifted into the Discontinued Policy Fund (DPF), earning a guaranteed minimum interest rate of 4.0% p.a. Proceeds can only be withdrawn after the completion of Year 5.

Budget 2021 Taxation (₹2.5 Lakh Limit) ⭐

For ULIPs purchased on or after 1 February 2021, if the aggregate annual premium payable on all ULIP policies exceeds ₹2,50,000 in any year, the maturity proceeds lose exemption under Section 10(10D) and are taxed as capital gains identical to equity mutual funds! (Death benefit, however, remains 100% tax-exempt).

Section 2.4 • Policy Servicing & Formulas Numerical Mechanics

Paid-Up Value vs. Surrender Value Calculations

When a policyholder cannot continue paying premiums on an endowment or whole life policy after paying for a minimum period (usually 2 to 3 years), the policy does not lapse completely. It either converts into a Paid-Up Policy or can be Surrendered for cash:

Formula: Paid-Up Sum Assured ⭐

Pro-Rata Formula
Paid-Up Sum Assured = [ (Number of Premiums Paid) / (Total Number of Premiums Payable) ] × Original Sum Assured

Treatment of Bonuses: Bonuses accrued prior to the date the policy became paid-up remain attached and will be paid at maturity or death. However, once paid-up, the policy ceases to participate in future bonus declarations!

Worked Example: A 20-year endowment policy of ₹10,00,000 has annual premiums. The policyholder pays premiums regularly for 8 years and then stops. Accrued bonuses till year 8 equal ₹1,60,000.
• Paid-Up Sum Assured = (8 / 20) × ₹10,00,000 = ₹4,00,000.
• Total Maturity / Death Payable = ₹4,00,000 + ₹1,60,000 (accrued bonus) = ₹5,60,000.
Guaranteed Surrender Value (GSV): Calculated as a statutory percentage of total premiums paid (excluding 1st year premium, rider premiums, and taxes). The GSV factor scales upwards with policy duration.
Special Surrender Value (SSV): Usually higher than GSV. Calculated actuarially: SSV = (Paid-up Value + Accrued Bonuses) × SSV Discount Factor. Under IRDAI Master Circular 2024, surrender payouts must be fair and equitable even in early durations.
Section 2.5 • Policy Enhancement Add-On Riders

Life Insurance Riders & Regulatory Limits

Accidental Death Benefit (ADB) Pays an additional lump sum (usually equal to 100% of base Sum Assured) if death occurs due to an accident within 90 or 180 days of the injury.
Waiver of Premium (WOP) Rider ⭐ In the event of disability or death of the proposer (e.g. father on child policy), all future premiums are waived while the policy continues with full maturity benefits.
Accidental Total & Permanent Disability (ATPD) Pays monthly income or lump sum if accident results in total permanent loss of limbs or sight, preventing any occupational employment.
Critical Illness (CI) Rider Provides lump sum payment upon first diagnosis of specified major illnesses (stroke, cancer, kidney failure), subject to surviving a 28-30 day survival window.
IRDAI Rider Premium Cap: Total premium for all non-health life riders cannot exceed 30% of the base policy premium. Health-related riders cannot exceed 100% of base policy premium.
Section 2.6 • Statutory Asset Protection
Heavily Tested Topic

Married Women's Property Act 1874 (Section 6) ⭐⭐⭐

Section 6 of the MWP Act provides the most impenetrable asset-protection structure available under Indian law for business owners, corporate executives, doctors, and high-net-worth individuals.

Creation of an Irrevocable Statutory Trust

When a married man effects an insurance policy on his own life and declares on the proposal that the policy is taken under Section 6 of the Married Women's Property Act 1874 for the benefit of his wife, or wife and children, or children alone, the policy immediately creates an automatic statutory trust.

The Legal Shield from Creditors & Court Attachments:

Section 6 explicitly provides: "The money so insured shall not, so long as any object of the trust remains, be subject to the control of the husband, or to his creditors, or form part of his estate."

Irrevocability & No Policy Loan

The husband/policyholder cannot alter or delete beneficiaries once registered. He cannot take a loan, surrender, or assign the policy without the express written consent of the beneficiaries/trustee.

Beyond Reach of Creditors & Taxmen

Insolvency liquidators, Official Assignees under the Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code, civil court attachment warrants, and Income Tax attachment orders CANNOT attach MWP Act policy proceeds!

Does NOT Pass Under Will

The policy proceeds do not form part of the husband's estate. Even if he writes a Will bequeathing the insurance proceeds to someone else, the MWP Act trust overrides the Will completely!

Proposal Stage Mandate

The MWP Act endorsement must be executed at the time of proposal inception. An existing regular life insurance policy cannot be converted into an MWP policy at a later date.
