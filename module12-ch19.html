Performance Cheat Codes

• Sharpe Ratio: Denominator is Total Risk (σ). Use for complete standalone portfolios.
• Treynor Ratio: Denominator is Systematic Risk (β). Use for evaluating sub-funds within diversified portfolios.
• Sortino: Penalizes only harmful downside volatility below MAR.

Section 19.1 • Product Spectrum
SEBI Regulatory Ticket Thresholds ⭐

Comparison of Investment Vehicles: Mutual Funds vs. PMS vs. AIFs

Investment Advisers must understand the structural, operational, and tax differences across collective investment vehicles to recommend suitable products for retail, affluent, and high-net-worth (HNI) clients:

Attribute Mutual Funds (MFs) Portfolio Management (PMS) Alternative Investment Funds (AIF)
Governing Regulation SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996 SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020 SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012
Minimum Ticket Size ⭐ ₹100 (SIP) / ₹500 (Lump-sum) ₹50 Lakhs (Mandatory SEBI Floor) ₹1 Crore (₹25L for fund employees)
Legal Structure & Ownership Public Trust; investor holds scheme units. No direct share title. Contractual agreement; individual securities held in client's own demat account. Trust, Company, or LLP; investor holds unit tranches/commitments.
Portfolio Customization Nil (Standardized pooled portfolio for all unit holders). High (Discretionary, Non-Discretionary, or Advisory with model mandates). Negotiable for large commitments; defined by PPM strategy.
Category Spectrum Equity (11), Debt (16), Hybrid (6), Solution & Other (5). Discretionary vs. Non-Discretionary vs. Advisory. Cat I (VC, Angel, Infra); Cat II (PE, Debt); Cat III (Hedge / Long-Short).
Fee Structure & Water Mark Total Expense Ratio (TER) capped by SEBI Regulation 52 (No performance fee). Fixed Fee + Performance Fee with Hurdle Rate and High-Water Mark (HWM). Management Fee (1.5-2.5%) + Carried Interest (15-20%) above Hurdle Rate.
Taxation Pass-Through ⭐ Exempt under Sec 10(23D); taxed only upon unit redemption by investor. No pool tax. Every portfolio buy/sell triggers immediate capital gain for client! Pass-through under Sec 115UB for Cat I & II; Cat III taxed at fund level at MMR.
Key Exam Concept: High-Water Mark (HWM) Principle in PMS

Under SEBI PMS regulations, a performance fee can only be charged if the portfolio NAV exceeds the highest previous peak (High-Water Mark). If a portfolio drops from ₹60 Lakhs to ₹50 Lakhs and subsequently recovers to ₹58 Lakhs, the manager cannot charge a performance fee on the ₹8 Lakh gain because the portfolio is still below its historic high of ₹60 Lakhs!

Section 19.2 • Alternative Assets
Gold Instruments Comparison ⭐

Gold Investment Products: SGBs vs. Gold ETFs vs. Physical Gold

Gold acts as a strategic inflation hedge and crisis asset. Advisers must compare post-tax net realization across available instruments:

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB) Most Tax-Efficient
  • • Issuer: Reserve Bank of India on behalf of GoI.
  • • Interest Income: 2.50% p.a. paid semi-annually on nominal issue price (taxable at investor slab).
  • • Tenor: 8 years (early encashment window after year 5).
  • • Taxation on Maturity: 100% EXEMPT from Capital Gains Tax if redeemed with RBI after 8 years!
  • • Secondary Sale: LTCG at 12.5% if sold on exchange prior to maturity.
Gold ETFs / Gold Mutual Funds High Liquidity
  • • Underlying: Physical gold of 99.5% purity held in vaults.
  • • Expense Ratio: 0.30% to 0.60% p.a. (drag on net gold return).
  • • Liquidity: Real-time exchange trading during market hours.
  • • Taxation (Post Budget 2024): LTCG at 12.5% if held > 12 months (or 24 months for unlisted FoFs). No indexation benefit.
  • • Interest: Zero coupon / zero ongoing cash flow.
Physical Jewelry / Coins Least Efficient
  • • Friction Costs: 3% GST on purchase + 10% to 25% making/melting charges lost on resale.
  • • Storage Costs: Bank locker charges and theft insurance.
  • • Purity Risk: Risk of impurity and caratage haircut during resale.
  • • Advisory Verdict: Strictly unsuitable for pure financial investment; recommend only for cultural consumption.
Section 19.3 • Quantitative Attribution
Core Numerical Formulas ⭐⭐⭐

Risk-Adjusted Performance Attribution Formulas

Raw returns tell only half the story. Portfolio managers must be evaluated based on the degree of risk undertaken to generate that return.

1. Sharpe Ratio (Total Risk)

William Sharpe
Sharpe Ratio = (Rp - Rf) / σp

• Rp = Portfolio Return, Rf = Risk-Free Rate, σp = Standard Deviation (Total Risk).
• Application Rule: Use Sharpe when evaluating a fund as a standalone, unconstrained investment or when assessing an entire overall portfolio.

2. Treynor Ratio (Systematic Risk)

Jack Treynor
Treynor Ratio = (Rp - Rf) / βp

• βp = Portfolio Beta (Systematic / Market Risk).
• Application Rule: Use Treynor when evaluating a scheme to be added to an already well-diversified portfolio, where unsystematic risk has been diversified away.

3. Sortino Ratio (Downside Risk)

Frank Sortino
Sortino Ratio = (Rp - MAR) / σd

• MAR = Minimum Acceptable Return (hurdle rate), σd = Downside Deviation.
• Advantage: Unlike Sharpe, Sortino does NOT penalize positive upside volatility. Investors love upside surprises!

4. Jensen's Alpha (α)

Michael Jensen
α = Rp - [ Rf + βp(Rm - Rf) ]

• Measures return in excess of the CAPM theoretical benchmark.
• Interpretation: α > 0 indicates the manager added value through stock picking/timing. α < 0 indicates manager underperformed required risk return.

5. Information Ratio (IR) & Tracking Error (TE)

Active Consistency
Information Ratio (IR) = (Rp - Rb) / Tracking Error

• Tracking Error (TE): The annualized standard deviation of excess returns: TE = σ(Rp - Rb).

• Benchmarking Standards: IR measures consistency of alpha generation per unit of active deviation from benchmark. An IR of 0.50 is good, 0.75 is very good, and ≥1.0 is exceptional.

Exam Alert: The R-Squared (R2) Reliability Threshold ⭐

R-Squared (Coefficient of Determination) measures what percentage of a fund's performance is explained by the benchmark index. If R2 is less than 0.70 (70%), the fund's Beta and Treynor ratio become statistically unreliable and invalid! In such cases, Sharpe ratio must be utilized instead.

Section 19.4 • Practical Application
Solved Exam Model ⭐

Comprehensive Solved Performance Attribution Problem

Consider the following market scenario tested in NISM Series X-B:

Portfolio / Benchmark Annual Return (R) Std Dev (σ) Beta (β) Downside Dev (σd) Tracking Error (TE)
Scheme A (Large Cap Active) 16.0% 18.0% 1.10 10.0% 3.5%
Scheme B (Flexi Cap Active) 18.5% 22.0% 1.35 12.5% 5.0%
Nifty 50 TRI (Market Benchmark) 13.0% 15.0% 1.00 - -
Risk-Free Rate (Rf / 91-DTB) 6.5% 0.0% 0.00 - -

Scheme A Computations:

• Sharpe: (16.0% - 6.5%) / 18.0% = 9.5 / 18 = 0.528
• Treynor: (16.0% - 6.5%) / 1.10 = 9.5 / 1.10 = 8.636%
• Sortino: (16.0% - 6.5%) / 10.0% = 9.5 / 10 = 0.950
• CAPM Required Return: 6.5% + 1.10 × (13.0% - 6.5%) = 6.5% + 7.15% = 13.65%
• Jensen's Alpha (α): 16.0% - 13.65% = +2.35%
• Information Ratio: (16.0% - 13.0%) / 3.5% = 3.0 / 3.5 = 0.857

Scheme B Computations:

• Sharpe: (18.5% - 6.5%) / 22.0% = 12.0 / 22 = 0.545
• Treynor: (18.5% - 6.5%) / 1.35 = 12.0 / 1.35 = 8.889%
• Sortino: (18.5% - 6.5%) / 12.5% = 12.0 / 12.5 = 0.960
• CAPM Required Return: 6.5% + 1.35 × (13.0% - 6.5%) = 6.5% + 8.775% = 15.275%
• Jensen's Alpha (α): 18.5% - 15.275% = +3.225%
• Information Ratio: (18.5% - 13.0%) / 5.0% = 5.5 / 5.0 = 1.100
Advisory Synthesis: While Scheme B exhibits higher raw volatility (22% vs 18%), it generated superior risk-adjusted performance across ALL metrics: higher Sharpe (0.545 vs 0.528), higher Treynor (8.89% vs 8.64%), higher Jensen Alpha (+3.225% vs +2.35%), and an exceptional Information Ratio of 1.10, confirming high active management consistency.
Section 19.5 • Portfolio Maintenance
Rebalancing & Tax Friction ⭐

Portfolio Rebalancing Triggers & Tax Drag Optimization

Market movements inevitably distort a client's baseline Strategic Asset Allocation. Rebalancing restores the risk profile, but incurs transaction brokerage, exit loads, and tax drag.

1. Rebalancing Trigger Methodologies

• Calendar Rebalancing: Rebalancing at predetermined calendar intervals (e.g. annually on April 1). Simple to administer, but can trigger unnecessary trading in sideways markets.

• Percentage-of-Portfolio Corridor Rebalancing ⭐: Establishing tolerance corridors around SAA targets (e.g., 60% Equity ± 5%, i.e. 55% to 65%). Rebalance ONLY when weight breaches the corridor. This drastically curtails tax drag and trading friction.

• Volatility / CPPI Triggers: Dynamic shifts based on market risk multipliers.

2. Tax Optimization Rules (Post Budget 2024)

• Equity LTCG (Sec 112A): 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year (Holding > 12 months).

• Equity STCG (Sec 111A): 20% on gains (Holding ≤ 12 months).

• Specified Mutual Funds (Sec 50AA): Debt funds with equity ≤ 35% taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period.

• Cash-Flow Rebalancing Strategy: Rebalance by routing fresh SIPs/dividends into underweighted assets rather than selling appreciated assets, completely eliminating tax liability!
