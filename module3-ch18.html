Section 18.1 • Fast-Track Refresher & Taxonomy
Debt Taxonomy

Fixed Income Market Instruments & Classification

Fixed income securities represent a contractual borrower-lender relationship where the issuer promises periodic interest (coupon) and principal repayment at maturity.

Sovereign (Central G-Secs)

Issued by RBI on behalf of GoI. Tenors up to 40-50 years. Zero credit risk (sovereign). Semi-annual coupon. 10-Year Benchmark G-Sec serves as risk-free rate anchor for the economy.

State Development Loans

Issued by 28 State Governments & UTs via RBI auctions (Sec 21A, RBI Act). Sovereign status, SLR eligible, trading at a 25-60 bps spread over Central G-Secs due to lower liquidity.

Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

Short-term GoI debt in 3 tenors: 91-day, 182-day, 364-day. Zero coupon; issued at discount and redeemed at par (₹100). States cannot issue T-Bills.

Corporate Bonds / NCDs

Issued by corporates & NBFCs. Credit rating mandatory. Private placements (> ₹50 Cr) execute on Electronic Bidding Platforms (EBP). Priced at G-Sec yield + Credit Spread.

Commercial Papers (CPs)

Short-term unsecured promissory notes. Tenor: 7 days to 1 year. Minimum denomination: ₹5 Lakh (multiples thereof). Rated A3 minimum (typically A1+).

Certificates of Deposit (CDs)

Issued by Scheduled Commercial Banks (7 days to 1 year) & select AIFIs (1 to 3 years). Minimum denomination: ₹1 Lakh. Demat form, negotiable, SLR/CRR applicable.

Specialized Debt Structures Tested in NISM Series V-D:
Floating Rate Bonds (FRBs):

Coupons reset periodically based on an external benchmark (e.g., 182-day T-Bill yield) + fixed spread. Minimal price risk / modified duration near zero.

Zero-Coupon Bonds (ZCBs):

No interim cash flows. Issued at deep discount, redeemed at par. Crucial rule: Macaulay Duration is always exactly equal to term to maturity.

Perpetual / AT-1 Bonds:

Additional Tier-1 bank capital under Basel III. No maturity date (call options only). Discretionary coupon; loss absorption via write-down or equity conversion upon PONV.

Section 18.2 • Market Architecture & Settlement
Infrastructure

Indian Debt Market Architecture: NDS-OM, E-Kuber & CCIL

The Indian fixed income ecosystem separates primary issuance, anonymous secondary order matching, and central counterparty settlement guarantee.

1
RBI E-Kuber (Primary)

RBI's Core Banking Solution executing sovereign auctions for G-Secs, SDLs, and T-Bills:

  • Competitive Bids: Primary Dealers, Banks, MFs bid on yield/price.
  • Non-Competitive Bids: Up to 5% reserved for retail and mid-tier entities without bidding rate.
  • Retail Direct: Individual investors participate directly without broker commissions.
2
NDS-OM (Secondary)

Negotiated Dealing System - Order Matching, launched in August 2005:

  • Anonymous Order Driven: Screen-based matching based on price-time priority.
  • Standard Lot: ₹5 Crore lot size for Central Government Securities.
  • Odd-Lot Segment: Enables trading from ₹10,000 to < ₹5 Crore.
  • When-Issued (WI): Facilitates forward trading in new bonds prior to auction.
3
CCIL (Settlement & Novation)

Clearing Corporation of India Limited acts as the central clearing house:

  • Central Novation: CCIL interposes as buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, zeroing counterparty risk.
  • DvP-III Mode: Net settlement of both securities and funds across members.
  • Settlement Cycle: Standard T+1 settlement in G-Sec outright secondary market.
Corporate Debt Market Infrastructure (SEBI Framework):

Secondary corporate bond trading is conducted via stock exchange Request for Quote (RFQ) platforms (NSE RFQ and BSE RFQ). SEBI mandates mutual funds execute a minimum percentage of secondary debt trades via RFQ to improve transparency. Bilateral OTC corporate bond trades must be reported on CBRICS (NSE) or BSE within 15 minutes of trade agreement.

Section 18.3 • Super High Exam Yield ⭐
Valuation & Day Counts

Clean Price vs. Dirty Price & Day Count Conventions

In fixed income markets, interest accrues continuously on a daily basis between coupon payment dates. Trading terminals quote Clean Price to reflect pure economic value without distortion, whereas actual funds settlement is executed at Dirty Price to fairly compensate the seller.

Core Valuation Architecture
Mandatory Exam Numeracy
Primary Pricing Identity
$$\text{Dirty Price} = \text{Clean Price} + \text{Accrued Interest}$$

Also termed the Cash Price or Gross Invoice Price transferred at settlement.

Accrued Interest Formula
$$\text{Accrued Interest } (AI) = \text{Face Value} \times \text{Coupon Rate} \times \left(\frac{\text{Days Elapsed}}{\text{Day Count Base}}\right)$$

Allocates the periodic coupon proportionally between the outgoing seller and incoming buyer.

Clean Price (Trading Screen Quote)

The quoted market price on trading platforms (NDS-OM, NSE/BSE RFQ). It strips out accrued interest so market movements reflect pure interest rate and credit spread shifts, eliminating artificial "saw-tooth" drops on coupon payout dates.

Dirty Price (Actual Cash Remitted)

The total invoice amount the buyer remits to the seller on settlement date ($T+1$). Compensates the seller for the exact number of days they held the bond and bore issuer risk since the last coupon.

Institutional Day Count Conventions in India

3 Established Standards
30 / 360 Corporate Basis

Standard for Indian Corporate Bonds & NCDs

Every full calendar month is treated as exactly 30 days, and the year as 360 days (12 × 30).

Counting Rule:

Days = \(360(Y_2 - Y_1) + 30(M_2 - M_1) + (D_2 - D_1)\)

If month end is 31st, it is counted as day 30.

Actual / Actual Sovereign ICMA

Standard for Central G-Secs & SDLs

Uses the exact calendar days elapsed divided by the exact calendar days in the current semi-annual coupon period (typically 181 to 184 days).

Accrual Factor:

\(\text{Factor} = \frac{\text{Actual Days Elapsed}}{\text{Actual Days in Half-Year}}\)

Accounts for leap years and 31-day months.

Actual / 365 Money Market

Standard for T-Bills, Commercial Papers & CDs

Uses the exact calendar days elapsed divided by a fixed base of 365 days (leap years ignore Feb 29 for denominator).

Accrual Factor:

\(\text{Factor} = \frac{\text{Actual Days Elapsed}}{365}\)

Fixed convention across short-term discount paper.

Section 18.4 • Yield Measures & Relationships
Yield Concepts

Yield Measures: Nominal, Current & Yield to Maturity (YTM)

Yield is the annualized percentage return on investment. The three core yield metrics evaluate income, market price, and total cash flow return over holding life.

1. Nominal Yield (Coupon)

The stated contractual annual interest rate paid by the issuer as a percentage of the Face Value:

$$\text{Nominal Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Coupon}}{\text{Face Value}}$$

Remains strictly constant for fixed-rate bonds regardless of fluctuating market prices.

2. Current Yield

Relates annual coupon income directly to the current market price of the bond:

$$\text{Current Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Coupon}}{\text{Market Price}}$$

Major Limitation: Completely ignores capital gains/losses realized at maturity and coupon reinvestment compounding!

3. Yield to Maturity (YTM)

The Internal Rate of Return (IRR) that discounts all future coupon payments and maturity face value to equate the current Dirty Price:

$$\text{Dirty Price} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + \text{YTM})^t}$$

True economic yield metric used across global institutional debt markets.

The Two Mandatory Assumptions of YTM (Frequently Tested)

An investor realizes the exact YTM quoted at the time of purchase ONLY IF both conditions hold:

1. Held to Full Maturity:

The bond is held until its redemption date without premature sale or issuer credit default.

2. Constant Reinvestment Rate:

Every intermediate coupon cash flow is successfully reinvested at the exact same YTM. If reinvestment occurs at lower yields, realized return falls below YTM (Reinvestment Risk).

Coupon Rate vs. Current Yield vs. YTM Matrix:
Bond Trading Condition Price vs. Face Value Yield Relationship Order Economic Explanation
Discount Bond Price < Face Value (e.g. ₹95 vs ₹100) Coupon < Current Yield < YTM Investor earns annual coupon plus capital accretion to par at maturity.
Par Bond Price = Face Value (₹100 = ₹100) Coupon = Current Yield = YTM No capital gain or loss at maturity; return is purely coupon driven.
Premium Bond Price > Face Value (e.g. ₹105 vs ₹100) Coupon > Current Yield > YTM Annual coupon is high, but investor suffers capital amortization down to par.

Duration Mechanics

Macaulay Duration vs. Modified Duration

Maturity measures only when the final principal is repaid. Duration measures the true economic life and interest rate sensitivity of a bond.

Macaulay Duration (Measured in Years) F. Macaulay, 1938

The weighted average time (in years) required for an investor to recoup the bond's purchase price through all discounted future coupon and principal cash flows.

$$D_{\text{mac}} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} \left[ t \times \text{PV}(\text{CF}_t) \right]}{\text{Bond Price}}$$
Golden Examination Rules:
  • For a Zero-Coupon Bond, Macaulay Duration = Term to Maturity!
  • For a Coupon Bond, Macaulay Duration is strictly less than Maturity.
  • Higher Coupon → Lower Duration (cash recovered faster).
  • Higher YTM → Lower Duration (later cash flows discounted more).
Modified Duration (Price Sensitivity) % Sensitivity

Measures the percentage price change of a fixed income security for a 100 bps (1.00%) parallel change in market yield:

$$\text{MD} = \frac{D_{\text{mac}}}{1 + \frac{\text{YTM}}{m}}$$
$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -\text{MD} \times \Delta y$$
Key Formula Components:

• m: Compounding frequency per year (m = 2 for semi-annual bonds).

• Negative Sign: Captures the inverse relationship between yield and price. As yield rises (Δy > 0), price drops (ΔP < 0).

Risk Metrics & Curvature

Price Value of a Basis Point (PV01) & Convexity

While Modified Duration estimates percentage shifts, institutional dealers manage books in absolute rupee terms using PV01, and adjust for curve non-linearity using Convexity.

PV01 (DV01 / Dollar Value of 01) Rupee Risk Metric

The absolute rupee change in the price of a bond or portfolio for a 1 basis point (0.01% or 0.0001) shift in yield:

$$\text{PV01} = \text{Modified Duration} \times \text{Market Value} \times 0.0001$$
Institutional Application:

• Used daily by mutual fund debt managers and treasury dealers to set risk limits.

• A portfolio with PV01 of ₹5,00,000 gains ₹5 Lakhs if yields drop by 1 bps, and loses ₹5 Lakhs if yields rise by 1 bps.

Convexity: The Curvature Effect Second Derivative

Duration is a linear tangent approximation of a non-linear curved relationship. Convexity measures the rate of change of duration with respect to yield:

$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -\text{MD} \times \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Convexity} \times (\Delta y)^2$$
Positive Convexity Advantage:

• When yields drop: Bond price rises by more than predicted by duration alone.

• When yields rise: Bond price falls by less than predicted by duration alone.

Why Positive Convexity Benefits the Bondholder:
Yield Decline (Rally)

Duration gives a baseline gain. The convexity term \(\frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2\) is positive and ADDS to the capital gain. The price accelerates upwards!

Yield Increase (Sell-off)

Duration gives a baseline loss. But \(\frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2\) remains positive and CUSHIONS the drop, reducing the actual net loss!
