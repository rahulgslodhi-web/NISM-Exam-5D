Foundations & ALM

Concept & Economic Functions of Interest Rate Derivatives (IRD)

An Interest Rate Derivative (IRD) is a financial instrument whose payoff depends on the level of interest rates, benchmark yield curves, or the prices of debt securities (such as Government Securities or Treasury Bills). Unlike equity derivatives where the underlying asset is a physical stock or index, the underlying of an IRD is a pure interest rate benchmark or borrowing yield.

1. Asset-Liability Management (ALM) in Banks

Commercial banks face an inherent structural maturity mismatch:

  • Assets (Lending): Long-term fixed-rate loans (home loans, project finance, 10–20 years).
  • Liabilities (Funding): Short-term floating deposits (savings accounts, CASA, 1–3 year fixed deposits).
  • The Risk (Gap Risk): If interest rates rise, deposit funding costs surge immediately while asset coupon income remains fixed, compressing the bank's Net Interest Margin (NIM).
  • Derivatives Solution: The bank enters a Payer Interest Rate Swap (Pays Fixed, Receives Floating). The floating cash inflows offset rising deposit costs, neutralizing duration mismatch without modifying customer loan contracts.
2. Portfolio Duration Management in Mutual Funds

Debt mutual fund managers dynamically alter portfolio duration to manage interest rate cycles:

  • Anticipating Rate Hikes: A fund manager expecting RBI rate increases wants to cut portfolio duration (e.g., from 6.0 years to 2.5 years) to prevent capital depreciation.
  • Friction of Physical Selling: Liquidating physical corporate bonds incurs wide bid-ask spreads, illiquidity discounts, and capital gains tax liabilities.
  • Derivatives Advantage: Entering a payer swap or shorting Interest Rate Futures (IRF) synthetically shortens duration within minutes at minimal transaction cost.
  • Liability Immunization: Pension funds and insurers lock in exact reinvestment yields to match defined future annuity outflows.
Exam Watchpoint: NISM frequently asks why banks use derivatives instead of adjusting loans directly. The answer is: derivatives permit off-balance sheet synthetic restructuring of interest rate sensitivity without disrupting customer loan agreements or taking on physical bond liquidation costs.

Forward Rate Agreements (FRAs) & Settlement Discounting

A Forward Rate Agreement (FRA) is an Over-the-Counter (OTC) forward contract in which two counterparties agree to lock in an interest rate to be paid or received on a notional principal amount over a future borrowing/lending period.

Buyer of FRA (Long Position) Borrower
  • Obligation: Agrees to PAY the fixed FRA rate and RECEIVE the floating reference rate (e.g., MIBOR).
  • Objective: Hedging against RISING interest rates.
  • Payoff: If Reference Rate > FRA Rate → Buyer receives cash settlement payout.
Seller of FRA (Short Position) Lender / Investor
  • Obligation: Agrees to RECEIVE the fixed FRA rate and PAY the floating reference rate.
  • Objective: Hedging against FALLING interest rates.
  • Payoff: If FRA Rate > Reference Rate → Seller receives cash settlement payout.
Why is the Cash Settlement Discounted? (High Exam Yield ⭐)

In standard commercial lending, interest is paid at the end of the loan period (in arrears, at Month B). However, in an FRA, cash settlement occurs at the beginning of the borrowing period (at Month A). Because the payee receives the money $(B - A)$ months ahead of when interest would normally be paid, the gross interest differential must be discounted back to present value using the prevailing market reference rate!

FRA Cash Settlement Formula
Settlement Amount = Notional Principal × (Reference Rate - FRA Rate) × (Days / Basis) 1 + [Reference Rate × (Days / Basis)]
Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example:

Scenario: ABC Ltd buys a 3×6 FRA on a Notional Principal of ₹50 Crore at an agreed FRA rate of 6.50% p.a. At the end of month 3, the 3-month MIBOR reference rate fixes at 7.50% p.a. The borrowing period is 90 days (using a 360-day year convention). Calculate the settlement cash flow.

1. Gross Interest Differential = ₹50,00,00,000 × (0.0750 - 0.0650) × (90 / 360) = ₹50 Cr × 0.01 × 0.25 = ₹12,50,000
2. Discounting Denominator = 1 + [0.0750 × (90 / 360)] = 1 + 0.01875 = 1.01875
3. Discounted Settlement Payout = ₹12,50,000 / 1.01875 = ₹12,26,994
→ Result: The FRA seller pays ABC Ltd (the buyer) ₹12,26,994 at month 3.

Swap Structures

Interest Rate Swaps (IRS) & Overnight Indexed Swaps (OIS)

An Interest Rate Swap (IRS) is an OTC derivative agreement between two counterparties to exchange streams of interest payments on an agreed notional principal over a specified term (tenor from 1 to 10+ years).

The Cardinal Rule of Plain Vanilla IRS: There is NEVER any exchange of the notional principal at inception, during the life of the swap, or at maturity. The principal exists solely as a reference base to calculate interest obligations. On every payment reset date, only the net interest differential is exchanged between the counterparties.
Feature Fixed Rate Payer (Payer Swap / Buyer) Floating Rate Payer (Receiver Swap / Seller)
Leg Paid Pays Fixed Coupon Rate Pays Floating Benchmark Rate (e.g., MIBOR)
Leg Received Receives Floating Benchmark Rate Receives Fixed Coupon Rate
Market Rate Outlook Bearish on bond prices → Expects interest rates to RISE Bullish on bond prices → Expects interest rates to FALL
Typical Hedging Application Corporate borrower with floating bank debt converting it to predictable fixed interest Pension fund or NBFC with fixed assets converting to floating income to match floating liabilities
Net Settlement Payoff Notional × (Floating Rate - Fixed Rate) × (d/b) Notional × (Fixed Rate - Floating Rate) × (d/b)
Worked Example: IRS Net Settlement Calculation

Contract: 3-year plain vanilla IRS, Notional = ₹100 Crore, Fixed Swap Rate = 6.80% p.a., Semi-annual reset (182 days / 365-day basis). At the first coupon reset date, the semi-annual floating benchmark rate fixes at 7.40% p.a.

Fixed Leg Obligation = ₹100 Cr × 6.80% × (182 / 365) = 100,00,00,000 × 0.068 × 0.49863 = ₹3,39,06,849
Floating Leg Obligation = ₹100 Cr × 7.40% × (182 / 365) = 100,00,00,000 × 0.074 × 0.49863 = ₹3,68,98,630
→ Net Exchange: Floating payer pays the Fixed payer the net difference = ₹3,68,98,630 - ₹3,39,06,849 = ₹29,91,781.
Overnight MIBOR & FBIL Benchmark
  • Administrator: Financial Benchmarks India Pvt Ltd (FBIL) is the designated independent benchmark administrator under RBI oversight.
  • Calculation: Calculated daily using volume-weighted average of trades executed in the interbank call money market on the NDS-CALL platform.
  • Usage: Serves as the national risk-free money market floating benchmark for swaps and FRAs.
Overnight Indexed Swaps (OIS) in India
  • Dominant Contract: OIS is the single most liquid domestic interest rate derivative instrument in India.
  • Floating Leg Compounding: The floating leg is benchmarked to the daily compounded Overnight MIBOR over the payment period (geometric compounding, not simple average).
  • Key Tenors: 1-year and 5-year OIS are the sovereign barometers for future RBI monetary policy expectations.
  • Clearing: Bilateral OTC trades are guaranteed and centrally cleared through CCIL.

OTC Options

Interest Rate Caps, Floors, Collars & Swaptions

In addition to linear instruments (FRAs, Swaps), the OTC debt market utilizes nonlinear option-based contracts to manage asymmetric interest rate risk.

Interest Rate Cap Call Options

A sequence of European call options on interest rates (each individual option is called a caplet).

• Buyer: Floating-rate borrower pays an upfront premium.

• Payoff: If Market Rate > Cap Strike Rate → Seller pays: Notional × (Rate - Strike) × (d/b).

• Protection: Caps maximum borrowing cost while allowing full benefit if market rates plunge.

Interest Rate Floor Put Options

A sequence of European put options on interest rates (each individual option is called a floorlet).

• Buyer: Floating-rate lender/investor pays an upfront premium.

• Payoff: If Market Rate < Floor Strike Rate → Seller pays: Notional × (Strike - Rate) × (d/b).

• Protection: Guarantees a minimum return floor if yields crash, while retaining unlimited upside if rates rise.

Interest Rate Collar Cap + Floor

Simultaneously buying a Cap (e.g., at 8.00%) and selling a Floor (e.g., at 5.50%).

• Zero-Cost Collar: Premium earned from selling the floor offsets the premium paid for the cap.

• Corridor: Locks borrowing costs within a defined corridor [Floor, Cap].

• Tradeoff: Caps maximum cost but gives up benefit if rates drop below floor.

Swaptions (Options on Interest Rate Swaps)

A Swaption is an option contract giving the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to enter into an Interest Rate Swap at a predetermined swap rate on or before a specified future date:

Payer Swaption (Call on Swap Rate)

The buyer has the right to PAY FIXED and RECEIVE FLOATING. Exercised if market swap rates rise above the swaption strike rate. Used by prospective borrowers to set a ceiling on future fixed swap financing.

Receiver Swaption (Put on Swap Rate)

The buyer has the right to RECEIVE FIXED and PAY FLOATING. Exercised if market swap rates drop below the swaption strike rate. Used by debt investors or pension funds to lock in guaranteed high yields.

Regulatory Framework

Bond Forwards & OTC vs. Exchange-Traded IRD Master Comparison

India's interest rate derivatives market operates under a dual framework: Over-the-Counter (OTC) contracts regulated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and Exchange-Traded Derivatives (ETIRD) regulated by SEBI.

Bond Forwards (RBI Directions)

A Bond Forward is an OTC contract to purchase or sell a specific Central or State Government Security (dated G-Sec) at a specified price on a predetermined future date. Under RBI directions, entities can hedge interest rate risk associated with upcoming bond issuances, pipeline loans, or balance sheet portfolios.
