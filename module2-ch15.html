Foundations

Derivatives Foundations: Forward Contracts vs. Futures Contracts

Under Section 2(ac) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act (SCRA), 1956, a derivative is a financial contract whose value is derived from the price, value, or index of an underlying asset (equities, indices, commodities, currencies, or interest rates).

Forward Contract (Bespoke OTC)

A private, customized bilateral agreement negotiated directly between two parties Over-The-Counter (OTC) to buy or sell a specific quantity of an underlying asset at a pre-agreed price on a specified future date.

⚠️ Critical Risk: Exposed to bilateral counterparty default risk.
Futures Contract (Exchange-Traded)

A standardized, legally binding exchange-traded contract to buy or sell a standard lot of an asset at a predetermined price on a fixed expiration date, guaranteed by a central Clearing Corporation.

✅ Key Advantage: Zero counterparty credit risk via clearing corporation novation.
Detailed Side-by-Side Comparison: Forwards vs. Futures
Parameter Forward Contract Futures Contract
Trading Venue Over-The-Counter (OTC); negotiated directly between two private parties or through dealers. Recognized Stock Exchanges (NSE / BSE) via electronic order-matching books.
Contract Terms Customized: Size, quality, expiration date, and delivery location are tailored to counterparty needs. Standardized: Exchange fixes lot size, tick size, quality grade, and expiry dates.
Counterparty Default Risk High: Each party bears credit risk of default if the other party fails to perform at maturity. Virtually Zero: Clearing Corporation (NCL / ICCL) acts as legal counterparty to both sides (novation).
Settlement & Margining Single settlement at contract maturity. Typically no interim daily Mark-to-Market (MTM) margin unless contractually agreed. Mandatory upfront margins (SPAN + ELM) and daily cash Mark-to-Market (MTM) settlement.
Liquidity & Secondary Market Illiquid: Very difficult to close or transfer prior to maturity without counterparty consent. Highly Liquid: Can be squared off or rolled over instantly anytime during exchange trading hours.
Price Transparency Opaque; prices and transaction terms are private and not publicly reported. Completely transparent; real-time bids, asks, execution prices, and depths disseminated publicly.
Regulatory Oversight Unregulated or lightly regulated by contract law / bilateral master agreements (ISDA). Strictly regulated by SEBI, exchange bylaws, and clearing corporation risk-management rules.

Specifications & Payoffs

Exchange Futures Contract Specifications & Settlement Framework

Equity derivatives in India trade on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). SEBI prescribes strict standards for eligibility, contract lot sizes, expiry dates, and settlement mechanics.

1. Tick Size

₹0.05 (5 paise)

The minimum price fluctuation by which bids and asks can change for all Indian equity and index futures.

2. Expiry Cycle

Last Thursday

3 concurrent monthly contracts (Near, Next, Far month). Expires on the last Thursday of the month (or previous trading day if holiday).

3. Contract Value Band

₹15L – ₹20L

SEBI-mandated value band. Lot sizes are periodically calibrated so that (Lot Size × Stock Price) stays within ₹15 Lakhs to ₹20 Lakhs.

4. Trading Hours

9:15 AM – 3:30 PM

Standard trading session Monday to Friday on NSE and BSE derivative segments.

Settlement Framework: Stock F&O vs. Index F&O
Single Stock Futures: Compulsory Physical Delivery

As per SEBI mandates, all open positions in individual stock futures and in-the-money options at contract expiry must be settled via mandatory physical delivery of shares into Demat accounts.

  • Long Futures: Must pay full contract value in cash and take delivery of shares into Demat.
  • Short Futures: Must deliver equivalent shares from Demat to receive cash.
  • Short delivery results in stock auction with punitive penalty charges.
Index Futures: Cash Settlement

Contracts on equity indices (Nifty 50, Nifty Bank, BSE Sensex) are 100% cash settled.

  • Underlying index cannot be physically delivered as a single security.
  • Final settlement price is the official closing index value on expiry day.
  • Net profit or loss is credited or debited directly to the trading account in cash on T+1.
Symmetrical Linear Payoff Profiles

Futures contracts have linear and symmetrical payoffs. Unlike options (which offer asymmetric rights without obligations), futures create binding obligations on both buyer and seller. Futures are a zero-sum game: the buyer's gain is the seller's exact loss, and vice-versa.

Long Futures (Buyer) Payoff
Long Payoff = ST - F0
  • Market View: Bullish (expects price to rise).
  • Profit: Unlimited as underlying spot price (ST) rises above entry futures price (F0).
  • Loss: Substantial / unlimited as spot price falls toward zero.
Short Futures (Seller) Payoff
Short Payoff = F0 - ST
  • Market View: Bearish (expects price to decline).
  • Profit: Substantial as underlying price falls toward zero.
  • Loss: Unlimited as spot price continues to surge upward.

Cost of Carry Model

Pricing Futures: The Cost of Carry Model & Numerical Workings

The theoretical fair price of a futures contract is governed by the Cost of Carry model. An investor can either buy the underlying stock today and store/carry it until maturity, or buy a futures contract today for future delivery. Under no-arbitrage equilibrium, both paths must yield identical economic outcomes.

1. Discrete Compounding (Stocks with Dividends) Tested in NISM

When risk-free interest is quoted simply per annum and a discrete rupee dividend (D) is paid during the contract life:

F = S × [1 + r × (T / 365)] - D
Or: Fair Futures = Spot Price + Financing Cost - Dividend Payout

If dividend has time value from distribution date to contract expiry:
F = (S - PV(D)) × [1 + r × (T / 365)]

2. Continuous Compounding (Indices) Black-Scholes Standard

For broad market equity indices where constituents pay dividends continuously across the year at dividend yield (q):

F = S × e(r - q)T
Where: S = Spot Index, r = Annualized Risk-Free Rate, q = Annualized Dividend Yield, T = Time to expiry in years.

Net Cost of Carry = (r - q). When r > q, futures trade at premium. When q > r, futures trade at discount.

Step-by-Step Numerical Walkthroughs (Direct NISM Exam Problems)
Example 1: Single Stock with Discrete Dividend

Problem Data:

  • Spot Price of Tata Motors (S) = ₹1,000
  • Risk-Free Rate (r) = 7% p.a. (0.07)
  • Time to Maturity (T) = 73 days (73 / 365 = 0.20 years)
  • Dividend Expected Before Expiry (D) = ₹10.00 per share
Step 1: Compute Financing Carrying Cost
Interest = S × r × (T / 365) = 1,000 × 0.07 × 0.20 = ₹14.00
Step 2: Apply Cost of Carry Formula
F = Spot + Interest - Dividend
F = ₹1,000 + ₹14.00 - ₹10.00 = ₹1,004.00

Interpretation: The buyer of futures saves ₹14 of interest by not deploying capital upfront, but foregoes ₹10 in dividend received by cash stock holders. Fair price reflects net addition of ₹4.

Example 2: Continuous Compounding for Index Futures

Problem Data:

  • Spot Index (S) = 24,000
  • Risk-Free Interest Rate (r) = 6.5% p.a. (0.065)
  • Index Continuous Dividend Yield (q) = 1.2% p.a. (0.012)
  • Time to Expiration (T) = 73 days (0.20 years)
Step 1: Calculate Net Carry Rate
(r - q) = 0.065 - 0.012 = 0.053 (5.3% p.a.)
Step 2: Calculate Power Term
(r - q) × T = 0.053 × 0.20 = 0.0106
e0.0106 ≈ 1.010656
Step 3: Calculate Fair Index Futures Price
F = 24,000 × 1.010656 = 24,255.75

Exam Rule: If dividend yield exceeds interest rate (q > r), the exponent becomes negative, causing index futures to quote at a discount below spot.

Sensitivities: Factors Impacting Fair Futures Price
Spot Price (S) ↑ Futures Price ↑
Interest Rate (r) ↑ Futures Price ↑
Dividend (D / q) ↑ Futures Price ↓
Time to Expiry (T) ↑ Futures Spread ↑

Basis Dynamics

Basis, Contango, Backwardation & The Law of Convergence

Understanding the spread between cash spot prices and derivative futures prices is fundamental to hedging, spread trading, and arbitrage in financial markets.

Universal NISM Examination Standard
Basis = Spot Price − Futures Price
Note: While some international texts define Basis as Futures - Spot, NISM explicitly tests Basis = Spot - Futures.
Market State Price Relationship Sign of Basis Economic Drivers & Exam Context
Contango (Normal Market) Futures Price > Spot Price Negative (−) Standard condition in equity markets. Reflects positive financing cost of carry. The longer the maturity, the higher the futures price. Futures curve slopes upward.
Backwardation (Inverted Market) Futures Price < Spot Price Positive (+) Occurs when heavy dividends are payable before expiry, or during extreme physical supply shortages (high convenience yield), or intense bearish market sentiment. Futures curve slopes downward.
The Law of Convergence at Expiration

As time moves closer to the contract expiration date (T → 0), the carrying cost (financing interest and storage) progressively diminishes. As a result, the futures price converges toward the cash spot price.

On Expiration Date: Futures Price = Spot Price Basis = Spot − Futures = 0

Why Convergence is Inevitable: If the futures price differed from the spot price at the instant of expiration, arbitrageurs would execute instantaneous, zero-risk transactions (buying the cheaper and selling the dearer) until parity is strictly restored.

Calendar Spread

Simultaneously taking a long position in one expiry month and a short position in another expiry month of the same underlying (e.g., Long Oct Futures, Short Nov Futures). Exploits changes in the cost of carry rather than outright price direction.

Basis Risk

The uncertainty associated with the basis changing between the time a hedge is initiated and when it is closed out. While price risk is largely eliminated through futures hedging, the hedger remains exposed to basis risk.

Margining Architecture

Risk Management: SPAN Margin, ELM & Daily MTM Cash Settlement

To eliminate systemic risk and guarantee financial integrity across the settlement ecosystem, Indian clearing corporations employ a comprehensive, multi-tiered margining framework.

1. SPAN Margin 99% VaR

Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk (SPAN) was developed by the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) and adopted in India. It simulates 16 different market risk scenarios of underlying price moves and volatility shifts to cover 99% 1-day Value-at-Risk (VaR) across the entire portfolio.

2. Extreme Loss Margin (ELM) 3.5% Buffer

A prudential cushion mandated by SEBI (fixed at a minimum of 3.5% of gross contract value for equity derivatives). It cushions against catastrophic tail-risk events and black swan market shocks exceeding normal 99% VaR.

3. Daily MTM Settlement Cash T+1

At the close of each trading day, all open futures positions are revalued to the official Daily Settlement Price (volume-weighted average price of the last 30 minutes). Daily cash profits are credited, and daily cash losses are debited on T+1 morning.

SEBI Upfront Margin Collection Mandate & Intraday Peak Snapshots

Trading members must collect 100% of Total Initial Margin (SPAN + ELM) upfront from clients before placing any futures order. The clearing corporation records 4 random intraday snapshots of client positions to verify peak margin adequacy. Any shortfall results in progressive financial penalties levied directly on the broker.

Total Initial Margin Mandate = SPAN Margin (Portfolio VaR) + Extreme Loss Margin (ELM 3.5%)
Comprehensive 3-Day Daily MTM Settlement Numerical Tracking

OI & Volume Analytics

Open Interest (OI) vs. Trading Volume & Trend Interpretation Matrix

Derivatives market participants track the interplay between price movements and open interest to discern whether real smart money is accumulating long positions, aggressively shorting, or merely covering existing commitments.

Trading Volume

The aggregate count of contracts bought and sold during a single trading session. Volume reflects the intensity of market trading activity during the day and resets to zero at the start of every new session.

Open Interest (OI)

The total number of outstanding active derivative contracts held by market participants that have not been squared off or physically settled. OI carries forward from day to day until expiration.

The 4 Transaction Scenarios: How Open Interest Expands or Contracts
Buyer Action Seller Action Impact on Open Interest Rationale
Initiates New Long Position Initiates New Short Position +1 Contract (Increases) A brand new contract is brought into existence between two new market entrants.
Squares off Existing Short (Buys) Squares off Existing Long (Sells) −1 Contract (Decreases) Both parties are closing out existing commitments, extinguishing the contract.
Initiates New Long Position Squares off Existing Long (Sells) No Change (Remains Unchanged) Contract is simply transferred from an old bull to a new bull.
Squares off Existing Short (Buys) Initiates New Short Position No Change (Remains Unchanged) Contract is transferred from an old bear to a new bear.
The 4-Quadrant Price & Open Interest Interpretation Matrix (High Exam Yield ⭐)
Price ↑ | OI ↑ : Long Build-up Strongly Bullish

Prices are climbing accompanied by an expansion in open interest. Fresh capital is aggressively entering the market on the long side. Expect bullish upward momentum to sustain.

Price ↓ | OI ↑ : Short Build-up Strongly Bearish

Prices are declining accompanied by an expansion in open interest. Fresh money is aggressively creating short positions. High probability of further downside decline.

Price ↑ | OI ↓ : Short Covering Weak Bullish / Rally

Prices are rising while open interest is falling. Bears are rushing to buy back contracts to cut losses or protect profits. The rally is technical and may lose steam once short covering concludes.

Price ↓ | OI ↓ : Long Unwinding Weak Bearish / Pullback

Prices are falling while open interest is contracting. Existing bulls are dumping positions to book profits or stop losses. Selling pressure usually abates once stale long positions are liquidated.

Arbitrage Dynamics

Arbitrage Mechanics: Cash-and-Carry vs. Reverse Cash-and-Carry

Arbitrage is the simultaneous purchase and sale of an equivalent asset in different markets to exploit pricing discrepancies without bearing directional market risk. Arbitrage activity forces market futures prices to gravitate toward theoretical fair values.

1. Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage Factual > Ffair

Triggered when the market futures contract is trading at an unjustified premium above its theoretical fair price (Overpriced Futures).

Strategy: "Buy Cheap Spot Stock + Sell Expensive Futures"
Execution Workflow:
  1. Borrow Cash: Borrow funds at risk-free rate (r) for contract duration (T).
  2. Buy Spot Asset: Buy shares in the cash market at current spot price (S0).
  3. Short Futures: Sell (short) futures contract at market price (Factual).
  4. Hold to Maturity: Carry the shares to expiration date, collecting any dividend (D).
  5. Deliver & Repay: Deliver shares against short futures to receive Factual; repay the bank loan with interest [S0 × (1 + rT)].
Net Arbitrage Profit = Factual − Ffair > 0
2. Reverse Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage Factual < Ffair

Triggered when the market futures contract is trading at an unjustified discount below its theoretical fair price (Underpriced Futures).

Strategy: "Short Expensive Spot Stock + Buy Cheap Futures"
Execution Workflow:
  1. Borrow Shares: Borrow shares via Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB).
  2. Short Spot Stock: Sell shares in cash spot market at price (S0).
  3. Lend/Invest Proceeds: Deposit cash proceeds at risk-free lending rate (r).
  4. Long Futures: Buy (long) underpriced futures contract at (Factual).
  5. Collect & Return: At maturity, collect principal plus interest [S0 × (1 + rT)], take delivery of shares via long futures at Factual, and return shares to lender.
Net Arbitrage Profit = Ffair − Factual > 0
Real-World Frictions & The "No-Arbitrage Band"

In frictionless textbook theory, any price deviation triggers instantaneous arbitrage. In reality, arbitrageurs incur transaction costs: Securities Transaction Tax (STT), exchange turnover fees, SEBI turnover charges, GST, stamp duty, bid-ask spread slippage, and SLB borrowing fees.

No-Arbitrage Band: [Ffair − Transaction Costs] ≤ Fmarket ≤ [Ffair + Transaction Costs]

As long as market futures trade within this band, arbitrage is unprofitable after accounting for round-trip friction costs.
