Section 18.1 • Conceptual Foundations
High-Yield Definition Matrix ⭐

The Tripartite Risk Spectrum: Capacity vs. Tolerance vs. Attitude

In financial advisory practice under SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, "Risk" is not a monolithic number. An adviser must dissect an investor's profile into three distinct dimensions:

Objective / Financial

Risk Capacity

The objective, mathematical ability of the client to absorb capital loss without compromising core financial goals, baseline lifestyle, or emergency liquidity.

• Driven by: Net worth, age, time horizon, income stability, liabilities, and number of dependents.
• Nature: Completely quantifiable via balance sheets and cash flows.
Subjective / Emotional

Risk Tolerance

The psychological willingness of the investor to experience portfolio fluctuations and mark-to-market drawdowns in pursuit of higher future returns (the "sleep-at-night factor").

• Driven by: Personality traits, emotional resilience, loss aversion, and financial literacy.
• Nature: Subjective, psychometrically assessed via scenario-based surveys.
Cognitive / Mindset

Risk Attitude

The client's cultural belief system, preconceived notions, and past experiences regarding financial risk (e.g., trauma from suffering losses during the 2008 GFC or 2020 crash).

• Driven by: Past market burns, family heritage, socio-economic conditioning.
• Nature: Mutable over time through professional behavioral coaching.
Section 18.2 • Decision Framework
Mandatory Suitability Rule ⭐

The Compatibility Matrix & The Golden "Capacity Override" Rule

What happens when an investor's psychological Risk Tolerance conflicts with their financial Risk Capacity? Under SEBI IA Regulations, 2013, the adviser must strictly enforce the Capacity Override Rule.

Risk Capacity (Ability) Risk Tolerance (Willingness) Investor Persona Adviser Mandate & Recommended Asset Allocation
HIGH CAPACITY HIGH TOLERANCE Aggressive Accumulator Ideal alignment. Allocate 70%–85% Equity for aggressive long-term compounding. High human capital, long horizon.
HIGH CAPACITY LOW TOLERANCE Hesitant Preserver Client has surplus wealth but fears volatility. Adviser Coaching Mandate: Do not force high equity immediately. Start with Balanced Advantage Funds (40-50% equity) and educate on inflation drag.
LOW CAPACITY HIGH TOLERANCE DANGER ZONE • Reckless Gambler CRITICAL EXAM TRAP ⭐: Capacity strictly overrides Tolerance! Even if the client begs for 100% small-cap or crypto, the adviser MUST RESTRICT equity to ≤20% or zero to protect non-negotiable living liabilities.
LOW CAPACITY LOW TOLERANCE Defensive Pure Preserver Full alignment on safety. Allocate 80%–100% High-Grade Debt (G-Sec, T-Bills, AAA Corporate Paper) and Arbitrage funds. Focus on capital protection.
The SEBI IA Fiduciary Suitability Standard

Regulation 17 of SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013 explicitly bars an adviser from recommending products aligned solely with an investor's psychological greed if the investor lacks the objective financial capacity to absorb potential losses. Risk Capacity acts as an unbreachable ceiling.

Section 18.3 • Psychometrics
Process Architecture

Questionnaire Design Principles & Behavioral Biases

A poorly structured questionnaire leads to severe mis-profiling. Understanding cognitive biases and design flaws is heavily tested in NISM Series X-B:

Best Practices in Questionnaire Design

  • • Scenario-based questions: Frame questions around concrete rupee losses rather than abstract percentages (e.g. "How would you feel if your ₹50 Lakhs became ₹37.5 Lakhs?").
  • • Multi-dimensional scoring: Assign separate scores for Capacity and Tolerance rather than combining them into a misleading single average.
  • • Time horizon segmentation: Differentiate risk profiles by specific goal milestones (e.g., Retirement in 20 years vs. House downpayment in 18 months).
  • • Past experience audit: Verify past reactions during major crashes rather than hypothetical speculation.

Behavioral Pitfalls & Cognitive Traps

  • • Recency Bias: Clients surveyed at the peak of a bull market score as "Aggressive", but panic and redeem when surveyed during a correction.
  • • Framing Effect: Asking "Do you want a 90% chance of success?" gets vastly different responses than "Can you accept a 10% risk of failure?".
  • • Hypothetical Bias: Investors routinely overestimate their emotional tolerance when answering abstract theoretical surveys without real skin in the game.
  • • Overconfidence Effect: DIY investors attributing past market-wide bull runs to their personal stock-selection skills.
Mandatory Periodic Reassessment Triggers

Under SEBI regulations, risk profiles cannot remain static. An adviser must formally re-evaluate client risk profiling: (1) At least once every year; or (2) Immediately upon any major life milestone (marriage, birth of child, loss of employment, receipt of substantial inheritance, divorce, or when a major goal approaches within 3 years).

Section 18.4 • Allocation Architecture
Exam Core Topic ⭐

Asset Allocation Linkages: Strategic (SAA) vs. Tactical (TAA) vs. Dynamic (DAA)

The pioneering Brinson, Hood & Beebower (1986) study established that 91.5% of portfolio return variation over time is determined by long-term asset allocation policy, while market timing and security selection account for under 10% combined.

Parameter Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA) Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA) Dynamic Asset Allocation (DAA)
Core Objective Long-term baseline policy weights anchored to client risk capacity and goal horizon. Active, opportunistic short/medium-term tilts to exploit market mispricing. Systematic downside protection and counter-cyclical algorithmic exposure.
Time Horizon Long-term (5 to 30 years). Short to intermediate (3 months to 2 years). Continuous / monthly algorithmic model shifts.
Decision Trigger Risk profiling & life goals; rebalanced on calendar or corridor drift (±5%). Macro indicators, Yield Gap (10Y G-Sec - Earnings Yield), P/E ratios, sentiment. Pre-defined quantitative models (e.g. Balanced Advantage Funds).
Permitted Tilt Range Fixed target weights (e.g., 60% Equity : 40% Debt). Controlled deviation within bounds (e.g., 60% ± 10%, i.e., 50% to 70%). Wide dynamic range (e.g., 30% to 80% net equity via derivatives hedging).
Market Dependency Independent of short-term bull or bear sentiment. Highly dependent on active market views and valuation signals. Rule-based model inputs (e.g., trailing P/E, moving averages).
Tactical Asset Allocation Indicator: The Yield Gap Formula ⭐
Yield Gap = 10-Year Benchmark G-Sec Yield (%) - Equity Market Earnings Yield (%)

Where Earnings Yield = 1 / (Nifty 50 P/E Ratio) × 100. When the Yield Gap is unusually high (e.g. >2.5%), equities are expensive relative to bonds → TAA signals an underweight tilt to equity. When the Yield Gap turns negative or narrow → TAA signals an overweight tilt to equity.

Section 18.5 • Portfolio Strategy
Standard Practice Framework

Core & Satellite Portfolio Construction Architecture

The industry benchmark architecture recommended by SEBI for balancing low cost, beta efficiency, and alpha opportunity:

The Core Portfolio (65% – 80%) Foundation
  • • Instruments: Low-cost broad-market Index Funds / ETFs (Nifty 50, Nifty Next 50), Large-Cap Mutual Funds, Sovereign G-Secs, AAA Corporate Bonds.
  • • Objective: Deliver market returns (Beta) with ultra-low Total Expense Ratio (TER) and low turnover.
  • • Holding Philosophy: Strict Buy-and-Hold; remains constant across market cycles.
The Satellite Portfolio (20% – 35%) Alpha Engine
  • • Instruments: Active Mid-Cap and Small-Cap Funds, Thematic/Sectoral Funds (e.g. Technology, Pharma), High-Yield Debt / Credit Risk, Tactical Commodity Funds.
  • • Objective: Generate superior risk-adjusted alpha by exploiting temporary market inefficiencies.
  • • Holding Philosophy: Dynamically rotated or trimmed upon reaching profit targets.
