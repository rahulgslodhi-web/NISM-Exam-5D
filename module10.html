Comparative Legal Matrix for Level 2

Essential Comparison Tables

1. Testamentary Succession vs. Intestate Succession

Parameter Testamentary Succession Intestate Succession
Governing Principle Governed by the deceased's written intention through a valid Will or Codicil. Governed strictly by default Personal Laws of Religion / Statutes (e.g. Hindu Succession Act).
Freedom of Distribution Testator enjoys complete freedom over self-acquired property; can exclude legal heirs or bequeath to third parties/charities. Zero individual freedom. Devolves strictly to predetermined statutory classes (Class I, Class II, etc.).
Primary Administrator Executor named in the Will (obtains Probate where required). Administrator appointed by court through Letters of Administration.
Dispute Risk & Delays Significantly lower dispute risk if properly drafted and witnessed; clear roadmaps. High risk of family friction, multiple claimant conflicts, costly legal proceedings to obtain succession certificates.

2. Nomination vs. Legal Ownership (⭐ Exam Classic)

Dimension Nominee (Custodian / Trustee) Legal Heir / Beneficiary (Owner)
Legal Nature of Right A nominee is NOT an owner. The nominee is merely a statutory receiver / custodian / trustee authorized to give a valid discharge to the financial institution. The legal heir holds the beneficial title and ownership of the asset under succession law or Will.
Supreme Court Precedents • Sarbati Devi v. Usha Devi (1984) (Life Insurance)
• Ram Chander Talwar v. Devinder Kumar (2010) (Bank Deposits)
• Shakti Yezdani v. Jayanand Salgaonkar (2023) (Company Shares under Companies Act) 		Legal heirs are legally entitled to claim the entire proceeds from the nominee, who is legally bound to hold the asset in trust for them.
Insurance Act Exception Section 39 Amendment (2015): If the nominee in a life insurance policy is an immediate family member (spouse, children, or parents), they become a "Beneficial Nominee" and receive absolute title, excluding other legal heirs.

3. Private Family Trust Architecture & Taxation Matrix

Trust Type Beneficiary Rights Tax Treatment under Income Tax Act, 1961
Revocable Trust Settlor retains power to alter, revoke, or claw back trust assets. Clubbed in Hands of Settlor under Sections 61 to 63 of the Income Tax Act. Zero tax shelter.
Irrevocable Determinate (Specific) Settlor cannot revoke. Beneficiaries and their respective shares are explicitly identified and fixed. Taxed at the individual slab rates of the beneficiaries (Trustee assessed as representative assessee under Section 161).
Irrevocable Discretionary (⭐ Exam Classic) Trustees hold complete discretion over the quantum and timing of distribution; shares are indeterminate. Taxed at the Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR - ~39% or 42.74%) under Section 164.
Exceptions: Beneficiaries with no taxable income, testamentary trust for relatives, or Section 80U disabled dependent trust.

Common Traps in NISM Level 2 Estate Planning Questions

Memorize these 6 counter-intuitive rules to ensure full 20/20 marks in Module 10:

1. The Father Trap

Under Hindu Succession Act, the Mother is a Class I heir, but the Father is a Class II heir. If a Hindu male dies leaving mother and father, mother takes the entire estate to the exclusion of the father!

2. Female Intestate Reversion

If a Hindu female dies intestate without children, property inherited from her parents reverts to her father's heirs, NOT her husband or husband's heirs (Sec 15(2)(a)).

3. Presidential Probate Rule

Probate is only compulsory for Hindu Wills executed in or dealing with immovable property situated in the former presidency towns: Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai (Sec 57 & 213 of ISA).

4. Attesting Witness Bequest Void

Under Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act, if an attesting witness is named as a beneficiary under the Will, the bequest to that witness is VOID, though the Will itself remains valid.

5. Mutation Does NOT Equal Title

Mutation in municipal/Panchayat revenue records is strictly for collecting property taxes. The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that mutation does NOT create or extinguish legal ownership.

6. Discretionary Trust MMR

An irrevocable trust where trustee has discretion over payout is taxed at the Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR). It does not get individual slab rates unless it falls under the 3 statutory exemptions.
